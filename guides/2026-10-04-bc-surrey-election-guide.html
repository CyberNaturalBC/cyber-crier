📅 Key Dates & How to Vote
Two separate elections, a week apart, run by two different bodies: Elections BC (provincial) and the City of Surrey (civic).
UPDATE
THE BALLOT IS SET: WHAT'S LEFT BEFORE YOU VOTE

B.C. nominations closed Sat Oct 3 at 1 PM, so the provincial ballot is final. The Oct 1 deadline to get a Where to Vote card mailed to you has passed, but you don't need the card to vote: if you missed it, you can still register at the polls. You can also check or update your registration online at elections.bc.ca or by calling 1-800-661-8683.

B.C. (provincial)
• Mon Oct 12: district electoral offices closed (Thanksgiving).
• Fri Oct 16 to Wed Oct 21: advance voting, 8 AM to 8 PM.
• Sun Oct 18: last day to ask for a vote-by-mail package online or by phone.
• Sat Oct 24: final voting day, 8 AM to 8 PM. You can vote at any voting place in B.C. and still vote for your own riding.
• Nov 6–10: planned final count, so close ridings may not be settled on election night.

Surrey (city, school board)
• Wed Oct 7, Sat Oct 10, Wed Oct 14, Thu Oct 15: advance voting, 8 AM to 8 PM, at 10 sites including Cloverdale Recreation Centre and Fleetwood Community Centre.
• Until Oct 14: request a mail ballot from the City.
• Sat Oct 17: general voting day, 8 AM to 8 PM, at 52 locations. You can vote at any of them. Cloverdale sites include Salish Secondary, Surrey Centre Elementary, Adams Road, Katzie, Don Christian and Hazelgrove elementaries.
• You elect 1 mayor, up to 8 councillors and up to 6 school trustees.

See them in person: the Surrey & White Rock Board of Trade has a mayoral forum on Wed Oct 7, 5:30 to 8 PM, at the Sheraton Vancouver Guildford (registration required). As of Sat Oct 3 afternoon I still couldn't find a date for a provincial leaders' debate.

🏛️ B.C.: The Big Picture
UPDATE
A SNAP ELECTION, A NEAR-TIE IN THE POLLS AND A REBUILT CONSERVATIVE PARTY

The writ dropped Sep 22, a year ahead of the fixed date. In Leger's poll, 57% disapproved of calling an early election.

Seats going in: In 2024 the result was NDP 47, Conservatives 44, Greens 2, and 47 seats is a majority. Since then the Conservatives have lost and regained MLAs. Kerry-Lynne Findlay won the leadership in May and resigned Sep 20, after 13 caucus resignations or expulsions in four months. Lorne Doerkson is interim leader. Seven MLAs who had left for CentreBC came back on writ day. BCPoliTracker's count is NDP 47, Con 36, OneBC 2, Greens 2, CentreBC 1, independents 3, vacant 2. The Legislature's own standings page lists NDP 47, Con 39, Greens 2, Independents 5. The two sources don't match.

Polls (all within the margin of error, all taken during the campaign):
• New since Oct 1 · Ipsos (for Global BC), Sep 25–29, online, 1,001 adults, ±3.7 (credibility interval), released Oct 1: Con 42, NDP 40, Green 10, OneBC 3, CentreBC 3. That's Con up 7 and NDP down 5 from Ipsos's Sep 8–14 poll. 27% undecided. Best premier: Eby 33, Doerkson 17. Metro Vancouver is close (NDP 43, Con 41). Half disapprove of the early election and 32% approve. Top issues (pick up to three): cost of living 56%, health care 39%, housing 31%.
• Leger, Sep 24–28, online, 1,003 adults: Con 39, NDP 36, Green 13, OneBC 5, CentreBC 5. Preferred premier: Eby 39, Doerkson 35. 28% say they could still switch (41% of those 18–34). 58% say B.C. is on the wrong track. 57% are concerned some Conservative candidates hold views they see as too extreme.
• Liaison, Sep 26–27, phone (IVR), 1,000, ±3.1: Con 40, NDP 39, Green 11, CentreBC 4, OneBC 3. Its Sep 21–22 poll had the NDP ahead 41–36.
• Angus Reid, Sep 22–24, online, 801: Con 43, NDP 35, Green 12.
• Innovative Research, Sep 25–28, online, 500: NDP 41, Con 39, Green 11. I only saw this through trackers, not the original release.
• 338Canada projection, Sep 29 (a seat model, not a poll): Con 49 seats, NDP 42, with wide ranges overlapping. Odds of winning the most seats: Con 54%, NDP 45%.

Top issues: Leger (one choice only) has health care 15%, housing 14%, economy 13%. Ipsos (up to three) puts cost of living first (see above). Also hanging over the campaign: the U.S. trade war, a $13.8-billion provincial deficit, and LNG Canada Phase 2 (about $33B, up to 4,000 construction jobs), announced during the campaign.

UPDATE
NDP · DAVID EBY: "BUILD BC STRONG"

Leader: David Eby, premier since 2022, running for a second full term. He's a former human-rights lawyer, attorney general and housing minister.

Main pitches so far (no full platform document yet):
• Jobs and projects: new "Built in B.C. and Canada" buying rules requiring a minimum share of B.C./Canadian wood, steel, aluminum and cement on public projects, with penalties for U.S. goods. A target to double critical-mineral exports in five years by cutting approval times more than 60% and fast-tracking the North Coast Transmission Line, built by what Eby calls a "skilled, union-led workforce". Takes credit for LNG Canada Phase 2.
• Trump/trade: keep U.S. alcohol off B.C. shelves and "stand up" to U.S. tariffs.
• Housing: double the speculation and vacancy tax for B.C. owners (1% to 2%) and raise it for foreign owners (4% to 5%). A new tax on unsold new condos starting in year two, at 2% and rising 1% a year. The NDP says it could free up to 5,000 condos. Experts quoted by The Canadian Press were split.
• New since Oct 1 · Gas prices: cut the provincial motor-fuel tax by 10¢/L on gas and diesel for a limited time, plus a permanent "price guard". Under it, the B.C. Utilities Commission would set a maximum pump price, updated weekly with crude prices, and cap what the NDP calls excess industry margins. The party claims the two together could save up to about 30¢/L and $20 a tank, or up to $100 a month for a two-car Surrey family. The Canadian Press reports the plan would cost about $250 million, with more details to come. Eby said similar rules work in Atlantic Canada.
• New since Oct 1 · Groceries: a grocery "price guard" capping large retailers' margins on six staples (milk, eggs, butter, cheese, chicken, turkey). Also unit pricing on shelf labels, a ban on property deals that block competing grocery stores, rules against predatory pricing, and work with Ottawa on an independent Lower Mainland food terminal. Eby also pledged to match Red Cross wildfire donations up to $20M.
• Health: a $15M push to triple recruitment of U.S. health workers (from about 800 to 2,400+), with loan forgiveness and moving help.
• Record: ended the consumer carbon tax Apr 1, 2025 (industrial carbon pricing stays). Eased the zero-emission vehicle rule from 100% to 75% of new sales by 2035, so there's no outright 2035 gas-car ban. Community benefits agreements (union-hiring deals) on major public projects, including the SkyTrain to Langley.

Main criticism: a $13.8-billion deficit after seven years in power. Doerkson notes the $1,000 household rebate Eby promised in 2024 was never paid (the government said in Feb 2025 the deficit ruled it out). The Retail Council of Canada says grocery caps miss the real cost drivers: transport, energy, labour and regulation. Builders complain big projects have been "re-paced". Locally, the new Cloverdale hospital has slipped from 2027 to 2030 and now costs $2.88B, and the Vancouver Sun reported Eby sidestepped Surrey health-care questions on a campaign stop.

Why it matters: For B.C. tradespeople: NDP governments have used CBAs, which favour union hiring on public jobs, and "Built in B.C." rules aim to steer more public work to local suppliers. For homeowners: the speculation and condo taxes target vacant units, not owner-occupied homes. For drivers: the 10¢/L cut and price cap help anyone filling up a gas or diesel vehicle or work truck. Tesla and other EV owners wouldn't benefit, and there's still no provincial passenger-EV rebate on offer. For Cloverdale families: the hospital delay is the NDP's record to defend.
 
UPDATE
CONSERVATIVES · LORNE DOERKSON (INTERIM): "NO NEW TAXES. PERIOD."

Leader: Lorne Doerkson, MLA for Cariboo-Chilcotin since 2020. He was elected as a BC Liberal and switched to the Conservatives in 2024. Before politics he worked in small business, newspaper publishing, auto sales and financial services. He was named interim leader Sep 20 after Kerry-Lynne Findlay resigned. Party rules bar interim leaders from seeking the permanent job, but the board says he would stay on as leader if the party wins.

Main pitches so far (no full 2026 platform yet; the website still lists older planks such as Putting Patients First and Get BC Building):
• Taxes: "No new taxes. Period." Remove the PST from Canadian beer, wine and spirits for as long as the trade war lasts, and keep U.S. alcohol off shelves. CityNews reported no cost estimate.
• Energy and projects: an "Energy Superpower" plan to double LNG output by 2032 and triple it by 2035, a 120-Day Permitting Act, matching federal one-year approvals, and declaring the Montney gas field a strategic asset.
• New since Oct 1 · Pipeline: on Oct 1 Doerkson welcomed Ottawa's listing of the Pacific Link oil pipeline (northern Alberta to B.C.'s coast by a southern route) as a project of national interest: "We welcome it because British Columbia's economy desperately needs a boost." The listing speeds up the federal review, but it isn't final approval: Ottawa aims to set conditions by Sep 1, 2027. Eby said B.C. has a role in the process to protect B.C. interests.
• New since Oct 1 · Wildfires: a Wildfire Action Plan announced Oct 2 in Summerland: about half a billion dollars for a fleet of Crown-owned water bombers and an innovation fund for firefighting technology, including AI, plus more prevention work on the ground and less bureaucracy. Doerkson said renting the planes to other jurisdictions would help pay for them, and that he wouldn't cut services.
• Reconciliation law: repealing DRIPA (B.C.'s UN Declaration law) is the top priority, according to the Globe.
• Schools: repeal SOGI 123 and refocus on reading, writing and math. Doerkson also said abortion is "a woman's choice".
• Housing (past statements; Postmedia couldn't get updated positions): scrap provincial upzoning mandates, let cities set zoning, cut red tape, and oppose the NDP's higher speculation and condo taxes.
• Carbon: the 2024 platform promised to scrap all carbon taxes "including hidden" ones and to reverse bans on gas heating and non-electric vehicles. The consumer tax is already gone. The Tyee (a left-leaning outlet) reports that the party's 2026 "Nanaimo declaration" still says to scrap the carbon tax.

Main criticism: instability: two leaders in four months and a dozen-plus MLAs leaving or being pushed out. 57% in Leger's poll worry some candidates are too extreme. The party hasn't said how a no-new-taxes pledge and the new wildfire spending fit with a $13.8B deficit. UBC political scientist Stewart Prest told Global that new money needs a concrete explanation, "not just efficiencies and undefined growth". NDP Forests Minister Ravi Parmar asked how the planes would be paid for. The NDP (a partisan source) says the Conservatives would cut health care and points to MLA Tony Luck saying health and education would "take the brunt". It also says the LNG targets fall short of projects already underway.

Why it matters: Big LNG and resource buildout means pipefitting, sheet metal and industrial work, though mostly in the North. Conservative MLAs have tried to end union-only project agreements (see Things to Weigh below), so ask the candidates where they stand. Letting cities set zoning would affect density decisions in Cloverdale. For families with kids in school, SOGI 123 is the main schools difference.
 
UPDATE
GREENS · EMILY LOWAN: RENTERS, CHILD CARE, NO NEW FOSSIL FUELS

Leader: Emily Lowan, 26, leader since September 2025. She doesn't hold a seat and is running in Saanich North and the Islands. The Greens have 2 MLAs and ended their working agreement with the NDP in February. By my count, Elections BC's official candidate list shows 73 Green candidates. The party is releasing its platform in pieces.

Main pitches:
• Renters: vacancy control (capping rent increases between tenants), end blanket no-pet rules, and restart community and Indigenous housing funds. Reported cost figures conflict: CityNews says $3.3B, while another outlet says $1.4B.
• Families: lift the freeze on $10-a-day child care spaces, universal free child care by 2031, and early-childhood-educator pay of $35–45/hr by 2028.
• Housing supply: 26,000 non-market homes a year.
• Transit: "fast, frequent and free" transit.
• Taxes: a "Fair Share Act" with a wealth tax on people worth more than $100M plus a windfall-profits tax, which the party says would raise more than $13B.
• Energy and labour: no new fossil-fuel development, so they oppose LNG Canada Phase 2. Building retrofits and "green union jobs", and sectoral bargaining to make unionizing easier.
• New since Oct 1 · Toxic drugs: regulated pharmaceutical alternatives to the street supply, more supervised-consumption and overdose-prevention sites (including for inhalation, and mobile and 24-hour sites), decriminalizing possession for personal use, and more treatment and housing. Former B.C. chief coroner Lisa Lapointe (13 years in the job) is running for the Greens in Saanich South against the NDP's Richard Zussman and Conservative Cathie Ounsted.
• New since Oct 1 · Farmland: limit Agricultural Land Reserve parcels over 4 hectares to citizens and permanent residents, and let cities tax productive farmland left idle.
• New since Oct 1 · AI: a moratorium on building and running AI data centres until a public-interest audit and rules are in place, an independent AI oversight commission with Ottawa, and no AI in classrooms until there's a plan.
• Other: proportional representation within 100 days, and support for SOGI 123.

Main criticism: opposing LNG puts thousands of construction jobs at stake. The revenue from the wealth tax is the party's own estimate and hasn't been tested. With 11–13% support, their realistic role is holding the balance of power, not governing.

CENTREBC · ELENORE STURKO: A SURREY MLA'S CENTRIST BET

Leader: Elenore Sturko, MLA for Surrey-Cloverdale and a former RCMP officer (she was a Surrey RCMP spokesperson). She was elected as a BC Liberal in 2022, crossed to the Conservatives in 2024, and was expelled in September 2025. She became leader Sep 22 after the party's other MLAs went back to the Conservatives, so she's its only MLA. She's running in Surrey South against Conservative Megan Knight. Andrew Weaver, a former Green leader, is a CentreBC candidate in Oak Bay-Gordon Head. By my count, Elections BC's official candidate list shows 35 CentreBC candidates, including Judy Higginbotham in Surrey-Serpentine River.

Stated direction (no platform released):
• Pragmatic, "centre" politics: support both resource industries and green tech, and a long-term balanced budget.
• Manufacturing: lower cost burdens, faster approvals, protect industrial land.
• Public safety backed by both the justice system and addiction treatment.
• Won't repeal anti-discrimination protections; supports inclusive schools.

Main criticism: 3–5% in the polls with one MLA, so votes may scatter. Sturko has belonged to three parties in four years. Policy details are thin, and some points come only from her social-media posts.

ONEBC · DALLAS BRODIE (INTERIM): DEEP TAX CUTS, PIPELINES

Leader: Dallas Brodie, MLA for Vancouver-Quilchena, a former lawyer, broadcaster and tenancy arbitrator. The Conservatives expelled her in March 2025 over comments then-leader John Rustad said mocked residential-school survivors. She later proposed a bill against land acknowledgements, calling them the "anthem of a suicidal nation". 2 MLAs. By my count, Elections BC's official candidate list shows 33 OneBC candidates.

Platform (published):
• Cut every income-tax bracket 25%, including corporate tax, and take 2 points off the PST.
• A new oil pipeline to Prince Rupert or Kitimat.
• Balance the budget within 4 years.
• Private health care alongside the public system.
• Repeal DRIPA, remove SOGI 123 and add a "parental bill of rights".
• Scrap the BC Energy Step Code, which sets building efficiency rules.
• Block municipal rent control.
• End safe supply and supervised consumption sites, and move to involuntary care.

Main criticism: the NDP (a partisan source) says the tax cuts would leave a $10.6B hole. Critics point to Brodie's comments on residential schools. At 3–5%, OneBC could split the right-of-centre vote. The party says it would avoid running against "true conservatives", but has partly walked that back (reported by one outlet).

Sources (6): CBC: the party leaders · OneBC: priorities · BC NDP (partisan): OneBC costing · BCPoliTracker · Leger (Sep 24-28) · Elections BC: official candidate list
Why it matters: The tax cuts are the biggest on offer from any party. The Step Code shapes how new homes are built and ventilated, which matters to builders and HVAC trades. OneBC's candidate in Surrey-Serpentine River is Todd Holowchak (see The Riding).
 
FREEDOM PARTY OF BC · AMRIT BIRRING: A SMALL PARTY ON THE SURREY-SERPENTINE RIVER BALLOT

New since Oct 1: this party wasn't in the Oct 1 guide because it had no candidate here then.

Leader: Amrit Birring, a Surrey software developer (UBC computer science, 2006) who co-founded the party in January 2023. He ran federally for the People's Party of Canada in 2021, ran for Surrey mayor with his own civic slate in 2022, and ran in Surrey-Newton in 2024. Elections BC's official candidate list shows Errol Povah in Surrey-Serpentine River as its only 2026 candidate. Pollsters don't list it separately.

What it says it stands for (party website; no costed platform found):
• "Freedom of medical choice", schooling "free of indoctrination", and parental rights over children's medical and education decisions.
• Remove SOGI 123, keep drugs out of schools, and raise K–12 academic standards. These were Birring's top three priorities in 2024.
• Lower taxes, "Canadian housing only for Canadians", gun ownership for self-defence, and freedom from government surveillance.
• At a 2024 campus panel Birring said: "We don't believe in climate change. It's a big hoax," and called climate taxes a way to make people poor.

Context: in June 2026 Birring and Povah were each charged with mischief over $5,000 over black paint on a rainbow crosswalk at Holland Park in Surrey. Both were released with conditions. The charges haven't been tested in court.

⚖️ Things to Weigh, Whichever Way You Lean
THE RECORD NEXT TO THE PITCH: THE BIG TRADE-OFFS

This box doesn't recommend anything. It puts each big party's record next to its pitch on the questions that come up most, whichever way you lean. Sources for every party line are in the party stories above.

Project labour agreements / CBAs
• What happened: in March 2026 Conservative MLA Kiel Giddens introduced Bill M233 to ban CBAs and PLAs on public projects. It was defeated 48–42 in April.
• Arguments for the bill: Giddens says CBAs added $17B in costs and 158 combined years of delays. The open-shop builders' group ICBA says 85% of construction workers are non-union and more bidders means better value.
• Arguments against: the BC Building Trades calls CBAs the route to apprenticeships and local hiring, and called the bill a "trial balloon" for the Conservative platform.
• Where the parties stand: the NDP uses CBAs on major public projects, including the SkyTrain to Langley. I found no statement from Conservative leader Lorne Doerkson on CBAs during this campaign.

Work volume
• Both big parties promise major projects. The NDP backs LNG Canada Phase 2, more critical-mineral exports and the North Coast Transmission Line. The Conservatives promise to double LNG by 2032 and triple it by 2035, with 120-day permitting, and back the federally listed Pacific Link oil pipeline. Ottawa aims to finish setting its conditions by Sep 1, 2027, so construction couldn't start before then. The Greens oppose new fossil-fuel projects. The open question is under what labour terms the work gets done.

Taxes and money
• Neither big party has shown how it would close the $13.8B deficit. The NDP's new 10¢/L gas-tax cut and price caps would cost about $250 million (The Canadian Press). The Conservatives' no-new-taxes pledge and PST break on Canadian alcohol are concrete, but they haven't said what spending would change, and they added a roughly $500M wildfire plan on Oct 2. OneBC's 25% income-tax cut is the biggest on offer; the NDP says it would leave a $10.6B hole.

Carbon tax and EVs
• The consumer carbon tax at the pump is already gone. The NDP has eased the zero-emission vehicle rule to 75% of new sales by 2035. The Conservatives' 2024 platform promised to scrap "all carbon taxes", which now means the industrial levy, and to reverse gas-heating and gas-car phase-outs.

Housing
• NDP: higher speculation and vacancy taxes and a new tax on unsold condos, with province-set density rules. Conservatives: local zoning control and fewer provincial mandates (good if you worry about density on your street; less helpful if you want faster building). Greens: vacancy control and 26,000 non-market homes a year.

Schools and values
• The Conservatives, OneBC and the Freedom Party would remove SOGI 123. The Greens and CentreBC back inclusive-schools policies, and the NDP ended funding for the group that created SOGI 123 but says anti-bullying work continues. Repealing DRIPA is a firm Conservative commitment. Leger found 57% of voters concerned that some Conservative candidates hold views they see as too extreme.

Stability and record
• Conservatives: an interim leader, a leadership race likely if they lose, and 13 departures under the last leader. NDP: seven years in power, a $13.8B deficit, the Cloverdale hospital delay to 2030, and an early election that 57% disapproved of in Leger's poll.

Smaller parties on the Surrey-Serpentine River ballot
• Todd Holowchak (OneBC) and Errol Povah (Freedom Party of BC) are running from the right of centre, and Judy Higginbotham is running for CentreBC, which describes itself as centrist. In a seat decided by 435 votes in 2024, votes for smaller parties can change who wins.

Questions you could ask any candidate: Would you vote to ban, keep or expand CBAs/PLAs on public projects? How would your party balance the budget, and what would it cut or tax? When will the Cloverdale hospital open under your government?

UPDATE
FINAL BALLOT (CORRECTED): HIGGINBOTHAM (CENTREBC), HOLOWCHAK (ONEBC), KANG (NDP), POVAH (FREEDOM), SAHOTA (CON)

The area: created in 2021 from parts of Surrey-Cloverdale, Surrey-Panorama and Surrey-Fleetwood, and first contested in 2024. It includes part of Cloverdale. To be sure which riding you're in, look up your address with Elections BC's where-to-vote tool.

History: in 2024 Conservative Linda Hepner (a former Surrey mayor) took 9,782 votes (49.7%) to the NDP's Baltej Singh Dhillon at 9,347 (47.5%) and independent McMurtry at 554, a margin of 435. Turnout was 54.5% of 36,196 voters. Estimates of the 2020 vote on these boundaries had the NDP ahead. Hepner isn't running again: "At this stage in life, I just can't make the commitment of another four years."

The final ballot (corrected): Elections BC's official candidate list, after nominations closed Sat Oct 3 at 1 PM, shows five candidates, listed here in its alphabetical order. bcballot.ca shows the same five. There's no Green or independent candidate. Judy Higginbotham, CentreBC (added in this correction)
• Former Surrey city councillor for 25 years, 1983 to 2008 (Surrey Now-Leader). In 2015 the paper reported she sat on more than 40 council committees and received the Solicitor General's Crime Prevention Award.
• Has run often without winning a provincial or federal seat: federal Liberal in 1988, 2004, 2008 and 2015 (second in the last three), BC Liberal in Surrey-Whalley in 1996, and independent for Surrey council in 2011. In 2024 she tried to run as an independent in Surrey North, but Elections BC rejected her nomination papers as incomplete, which she disputed. She then called herself a "moderate centrist" offering an alternative to "the excesses of the NDP left and Conservative right-wing".
• Not confirmed: CentreBC's candidates page didn't list her when I checked Oct 4, and I found no 2026 bio, local news profile or published priorities. bcballot.ca links this candidate to the former councillor's record; it's very likely the same person, but nothing official from Elections BC or CentreBC says so. For the party's positions, see the CentreBC story.

Todd Holowchak, OneBC (new since Oct 1)
• Not much is public. Elections BC lists him as OneBC's candidate, with himself as financial agent. OneBC's candidates page had no bio for him when I checked Oct 3, and I found no local news profile. bcballot.ca says he has no previous run on record.
• A LinkedIn profile under the same name describes a Surrey-based sales and business-development director at a Vancouver staffing-tech firm, but nothing official confirms it's him.
• He hasn't published any local priorities that I could find. For the party's positions, see the OneBC story.

Sikander Kang, BC NDP
• Registered physiotherapist (BSc, MSc) who manages five Lower Mainland clinics. Board member-at-large with PICS Society, a Surrey settlement and seniors agency.
• Was financial agent for NDP MLA Jagrup Brar's 2024 campaign. Married with two young children.
• Says: "As a physiotherapist, I see how healthcare and support close to home affect people's lives." His focus is health-worker recruitment, homes and B.C. jobs (one profile source).

Errol Povah, Freedom Party of BC (new since Oct 1)
• Retired B.C. Ferries second (navigating) officer, after a 30-year career that ended in 2008. Royal Canadian Navy veteran, raised in Nanaimo. This is from his 2024 candidate profile.
• He has run before: as an independent in Richmond-Queensborough in 2024 (Richmond News). bcballot.ca's "no previous run on record" misses this. His 2024 priorities were education, getting rid of the carbon tax, and health care that works. He opposed SOGI 123, safe supply and consumption sites, supported involuntary care, and called COVID a "scam". He has no published 2026 platform that I found.
• Surrey Police say he faces two counts of mischief over $5,000 tied to the Holland Park rainbow crosswalk (May 30, with party leader Amrit Birring, and June 27). He was released with conditions. These are allegations that haven't been tested in court.

Jessy Sahota, BC Conservative
• Party bio: "decorated law enforcement officer", one of the International Association of Chiefs of Police's 2022 Top 40 Under 40, a youth coach with the Yo-Bro Yo-Girl Youth Initiative since 2009, and a 2022 Queen's Platinum Jubilee Award recipient.
• National wrestling champion at SFU. Tamanawis Secondary grad, married to Ikjot Sandhu.
• The bio lists no policy priorities. The IACP award went to a Delta Police youth-liaison officer of the same name, and a Jessy Sahota ran federally for the Conservatives in Delta in 2025 and lost. It's probably the same person, but the party bio doesn't say so.

Local issues on the ballot:
• The Cloverdale hospital, delayed to 2030.
• The SkyTrain extension to Langley (now the "Dave Barrett Extension"): 16 km and 8 stations, with the guideway 65% built and opening set for 2029.
• School crowding: SD36 has about 83,000 students and asked for $5.49B in capital, including 29 new schools. A trustee says he's "not optimistic" the money comes.
• Extortion targeting businesses: 132 attempts in Surrey in 2025.

UPDATE
HOW THE MAYOR CANDIDATES COMPARE ON THE BIG ISSUES

The incumbent and 2022's top finishers come first, then the new parties. Locke beat McCallum by 973 votes in 2022. Positions come from party sites, forum reports and local news. Where only Wikipedia's roundup supports a point, it's marked in the sources.

BRENDA LOCKE · Surrey Connect · incumbent
• Taxes: won't promise a freeze (says it would force cuts); points to a proposed 2.6% increase for 2026.
• Policing: would fund both more police and new technology; city bought land for a public-safety training campus.
• Transit: pushes for more RapidBus and SkyTrain.
• Development: City Centre entertainment district with a 10,000-seat arena.
• Cloverdale: new pool for Cloverdale/Clayton Heights (site TBD); a Fairgrounds master plan including a convention-centre proposal; new Cloverdale ice sheets (record).
• Main criticism: years spent fighting the province's order to keep the Surrey Police Service. Ex-chief Norm Lipinski's court petition alleges his June firing was political. That's untested; Locke says it has nothing to do with her.

DOUG McCALLUM · Safe Surrey Coalition · mayor 1996–2005 and 2018–22
• Taxes: 0% property-tax increase for 4 years; forensic audit.
• Policing: "zero tolerance" on crime and extortion; police helicopter within 90 days; drones.
• Transit: SkyTrain down King George to Newton.
• Development: residential permits in 60 days or the fees are waived; a fee-free Surrey Centre "Opportunity Zone"; leave Metro Vancouver (except water, sewer, waste).
• Cloverdale: pool in Clayton Heights; farmland protection and direct farm sales.
• Main criticism: lost in 2022. His council cancelled the earlier King George light-rail plan. Rivals say the province won't fund another SkyTrain line soon.

LINDA ANNIS · Surrey First · councillor since 2018
• Taxes: says taxpayers shouldn't be an "ATM"; no specific rate found.
• Policing: 300 more officers over 4 years and a police training centre; end the city's police lawsuits and disputes.
• Transit: push for light rail.
• Development: cancel the arena (which the party calls a $600M project with no plan); spend on neighbourhood parks, pools and rinks; cut red tape.
• Cloverdale: no specific item found.
• Main criticism: January texts to the police chief relaying complaints about impaired-driving checks near her neighbourhood drew complaints to the police watchdog. She says she directed nothing.

MIKE STARCHUK · Imagine Surrey · retired fire prevention chief; NDP MLA for Surrey-Cloverdale 2020–24
• Taxes: cap increases at inflation, "no service cuts".
• Policing: extortion first, with an anti-extortion command, weekly public updates and replies to victims within 48 hours; finish the police transition by Dec 2027.
• Transit: three bus rapid transit routes in the first term, a Hwy 10 station, and a business case for King George SkyTrain.
• Development: 180-day permit guarantee; cancel the arena pending a business plan; Fleetwood stadium.
• Cloverdale: indoor pool at the Clayton Hall site; Fairgrounds as a "world-class" venue; Rodeo special-event status and grant; protect the ALR; West Coast Express via Cloverdale (advocacy).
• Endorsed by the New Westminster & District Labour Council and Andrew Weaver.
• Main counterpoint: his site promised a costed platform "fully revealed in September 2026", and I couldn't find the costing. McCallum (SkyTrain) and Annis (light rail) want rail rather than buses.

HONVEER SINGH RANDHAWA · Conservative Party of Surrey (a civic group, separate from the provincial party)
• Taxes: 4-year freeze plus a $600 cut per homeowner; halve water bills through meters.
• Policing: "fully fund" police against extortion.
• Development: no arena, though he supports investing in the entertainment district.
• Schools: wants SOGI out of Surrey classrooms (a provincial and school-board matter, not city) and a committee to review library books.
• Lawyer; lost Surrey-Guildford provincially by 22 votes in 2024.
• Main criticism: at the forum, he was pressed on how a freeze plus a cut pays for services.

TROY VAN VLIET · Surrey NOW · builder, founded St. John Paul II Academy
• Housing: rent-to-own program.
• Budget: zero-based budgeting; auditors reporting to the mayor.
• Few other specifics found.

IGAL AMMONS-II · New Surrey+
• Proposed a mayor-premier-PM panel on homelessness. Few specifics.

COUNCIL AND SCHOOL BOARD: A LONG BALLOT

Council: 65 candidates for 8 seats, voted city-wide (no wards). You can vote for up to eight, and you don't have to pick all eight. Full slates of eight are running for Surrey Connect, Safe Surrey Coalition, Surrey First, Imagine Surrey, the Conservative Party of Surrey and Surrey NOW, with independents and smaller groups also on the ballot. Council is now Surrey Connect 4, Safe Surrey 2, Surrey First 2.

School board (SD36): 6 Surrey seats. All six incumbents are running again as Surrey First Education. Surrey NOW has six candidates and Our Surrey has three, plus independents. Counts vary by source, from 23 to 26 trustee candidates. Key issues are crowding (most secondary schools are over capacity) and the $5.49B capital request.

How to research fast: read the candidate profiles on surrey.ca (council and trustee pages), check each slate's website, and look up Surrey Now-Leader and Surrey Citizen forum coverage. The Board of Trade's council forum is Tue Oct 6.

Traffic notes: the 64 Ave widening between 152 and 164 St finished this summer. Hwy 15 (176 St) has had overnight closures for SkyTrain work. I found no candidate with a specific Hwy 15/176 St plan beyond general road and transit pledges (one source each).

Why it matters: For Surrey families, the trustee race is the one most directly tied to class sizes and portables. Turnout in 2022 was only 34.5%, so a few votes go a long way.

• Tip: when a party says what the other side will do (e.g. "cuts"), that's an attack line. Look for the other party's own words.
