🌎 World & Politics
🚨 BREAKING
PILOT STABS CAPTAIN AT 33,000 FEET, AND PASSENGERS STORM THE COCKPIT TO SAVE THE JET!

BBC and Al Jazeera/Reuters report that on Flydubai flight FZ1073 from Dubai to Tel Aviv on Wednesday, one pilot stabbed the captain, Indian national Smit Machchhar, and, according to Israeli PM Benjamin Netanyahu, apparently tried to crash the Boeing 737 Max 8. Flightradar24 data shows the jet dropping from about 33,000 to 17,000 feet in under two minutes. The plane squawked emergency and then hijack codes before passengers and crew overpowered the attacker, and it landed safely in Tabuk, Saudi Arabia. Saudi authorities arrested the attacker. Israel's defence minister calls it an attempted jihadist terror attack, but the BBC and Al Jazeera both stress that no motive has been officially established, and Trump said the man was "perhaps a terrorist or perhaps a whack-job."

Passenger Yaniv Hayun (Trump called him an "Israeli plumber") told reporters he put the attacker in a chokehold and pulled back on the controls until relief pilots took over. Netanyahu credits the wounded captain with saving 174 people (the BBC says at least 150 were aboard). The Indian embassy says Machchhar is in stable condition. Flydubai has suspended Israel flights, and Israel says it will now vet every pilot flying into the country. The Free Press ran an exclusive interview with Hayun.

Sources (4): BBC · Al Jazeera / Reuters · The Guardian · The Free Press (newsletter interview)
Why it matters: Expect tighter cockpit and crew screening on Middle East routes, and maybe new 'two in the cockpit' debates for airlines everywhere.

UPDATE: RUBIO BOOTS IRAN'S DIPLOMATS, AND TRUMP SAYS IT'S A DEAL OR 'BLOW THEM UP'

Axios (scoop, citing a U.S. official and a second source) and ABC/AP (two U.S. officials) report that Secretary of State Marco Rubio ordered Iranian FM Abbas Araghchi's delegation to leave New York Monday evening after Qatari-mediated talks stalled. Officials said the delegation had "overstayed their welcome." Axios's second source notes Araghchi was already scheduled to leave that night, and the delegation flew to Doha early Tuesday. On Wednesday, Al Jazeera reports, Trump told reporters the U.S. can either make a deal or "blow them up." Defense Secretary Hegseth told commanders at Quantico that U.S. forces have "destroyed" Iran's navy, air force and military-industrial base. That is his claim, and it has not been independently verified.

Separately, UK PM Andy Burnham told the BBC there are "strong indications" Iran played a part in last weekend's RAF Fairford incident. Five men were arrested and then bailed, and police found petrol but no explosives. Araghchi replied that Britain is "barking up the wrong tree." Iran paraded Shahed drones through Tehran (Al Jazeera). Tonight's SitDeck brief (newsletter) places two U.S. carriers in the Arabian Sea and rates the odds of imminent strikes as moderate. That part is unconfirmed.

Sources (5): Axios · ABC News / AP · Al Jazeera (live) · Al Jazeera / AP / Reuters (Fairford) · SitDeck brief (newsletter)
Why it matters: If talks are dead and strikes resume, oil jumps first, and B.C. pump prices and job-site fuel costs follow.

🤖 AI & Tech
GOOGLE UNVEILS GEMINI 4 ARGON, BUT ONLY 'TRUSTED CYBER DEFENDERS' GET IT FOR NOW!

A day after OpenAI's DevDay, Google revealed Gemini 4 Argon, its new frontier model. DeepMind chief Koray Kavukcuoglu says it delivers top performance in software engineering, legal and finance work, and cyber defence (The Verge). Google is limiting first access to vetted security partners through its Fairwind Program, and TechCrunch says it was trained to "autonomously find, validate, and patch critical software vulnerabilities." Google is also taking part in the U.S. government's voluntary pre-release model-access process while it adds safeguards against misuse, prompt injection and misalignment. Google's own benchmark chart claims Argon beats OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra and Anthropic's models. Those numbers are vendor-supplied and have not been independently verified. No date has been given for public access.

Sources (3): The Verge · TechCrunch · Google DeepMind
Why it matters: Labs are now holding back their best models over hacking risk. Expect stronger security tools and stronger attackers alike.

UPDATE: CANADIAN JOSHUA KUTRYK RIDES CREW-13 TOMORROW, ON A TRIPLE-HEADER DAY FOR SPACEX!

CBC reports CSA astronaut Joshua Kutryk, 44, is "ready to fly" on NASA's SpaceX Crew-13, targeting Thursday 11:10 a.m. ET (8:10 AM PT) from Cape Canaveral with Jessica Watkins (commander), Luke Delaney and cosmonaut Sergey Teteryatnikov. Kutryk was originally assigned to Boeing's Starliner. Teslarati notes he will be the first Canadian to reach orbit through NASA's Commercial Crew Program, and docking is planned about eight hours later, around 5 PM PT. Florida weather is iffy (showers, possible storms), with Friday as the backup. Teslarati says Thursday is a triple-header: a Vandenberg rideshare, then Falcon Heavy's first-ever NRO spy-satellite launch (NROL-97) at 11:53 p.m. ET (8:53 PM PT).

🏔️ Local: BC / Fraser Valley / Surrey
TWO TSAWWASSEN FIRST NATION ELDERS KILLED AS VEHICLE HITS PEDESTRIANS AT TSAWWASSEN MILLS

Delta police say that around 10:25 a.m. Wednesday a driver who may have suffered a medical emergency struck four pedestrians outside Tsawwassen Mills mall. Two people died, and two more were taken to hospital along with the driver (Global News). Police later confirmed the two who died were Tsawwassen First Nation elders. They say the crash was not an intentional attack and was not connected to the Truth and Reconciliation walk happening nearby, though the walk was cancelled (Surrey Now-Leader, Vancouver Sun). Global reports the truck carried Alpine Building Maintenance markings, and the company says it is cooperating with investigators. PM Carney and B.C. NDP Leader David Eby both offered condolences. Police say there is no ongoing risk to the public.

Sources (4): Global News · Surrey Now-Leader · Vancouver Sun · CBC
Why it matters: A heavy day next door in Delta. The mall stayed open, but expect police around the scene as the investigation continues.

UPDATE: TWO FRESH POLLS PUT THE B.C. RACE AT A COIN TOSS, AND A QUARTER OF VOTERS COULD STILL SWITCH!

Leger (online panel, 1,003 residents, Sept 24–28) has the Conservatives at 39% and the NDP at 36% among decided voters, with the Greens at 13% and OneBC and CentreBC at 5% each. Liaison Strategies (IVR phone poll, 1,000, Sept 26–27) has it closer still at 40–39 among decided and leaning voters. That is a swing from its own Sept 23 release, which had the NDP ahead 41–36. Angus Reid's earlier Sept 22–24 poll had the Conservatives up 43–35, so the pollsters disagree on the size of the gap but agree it is close. Leger finds 28% likely to switch their vote and 57% disapproving of the snap election.

On the Conservative side of the ledger, 58% of B.C. residents say the province is on the wrong track, and only 19% say the NDP deserves another term outright. On the NDP side, Eby still leads as preferred premier (39% vs. 35% for Conservative interim leader Lorne Doerkson), and 57% worry some Conservative candidates hold views they see as too extreme.

Sources (4): Leger · Liaison Strategies · Daily Hive · Indo-Canadian Voice
Why it matters: A province-wide toss-up makes swing seats like Surrey-Serpentine River (decided by 435 votes in 2024) a real fight. Register by tomorrow for a mailed voter card.
