🚨 The Big Story
🚨 BREAKING
PHP2SHELL! TWO DECADE-OLD BUGS IN THE ENGINE UNDER MOST OF THE WEB!

In plain words: PHP is the behind-the-scenes language that builds web pages on the server: WordPress, Drupal, Joomla and Wikipedia's software all run on it. On Wed Oct 21 the PHP team fixed two flaws, CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, each rated 9.2 out of 10. Think of a jobsite where a crew gets pulled off mid-task and the next crew trusts the half-done work: when PHP runs out of memory part-way through handling a request (for example a big form upload), its cleanup can trust half-built data (CVE-...618), or a later request can reuse leftover data from an earlier one (CVE-...619), per CERT-EU and the NCSC.

Why it's nasty: one specially crafted web request, with no login, can make the server run the attacker's commands before the website's own code even starts, so a secure WordPress setup doesn't save you. Datadog says it rebuilt the attack independently and confirmed a site's login can't block it. Wiz adds a second danger: a memory leak that can expose passwords, keys and certificates sitting in server memory. Wiz says every PHP version back to 5.4 (about 14 years) is affected by at least one of the bugs.

Sources (5): CERT-EU advisory · UK NCSC · Datadog Security Labs · Wiz Research (finders) · CISA alert
Why it matters: Most websites you use run PHP. The fix is on the site owner, not on you, but if you own a website it's on you today.
 
SITES GO DARK WORLDWIDE: WIKIPEDIA, GOVERNMENTS, EVEN POKÉMON!

The BBC reports that Finland temporarily shut its government's public web services, the Dutch cyber agency took its own site offline, and Wikimedia took several systems down as a precaution, which knocked out parts of Wikipedia on Wednesday. The Verge adds 4chan, DeviantArt and Pokémon Home among the sites affected. The NSA, the UK's NCSC and CISA all told organizations that can't patch right away to take PHP systems offline. In Canada, the Canadian Centre for Cyber Security issued alert AL26-026 on Oct 21 telling IT teams to update immediately, and to isolate internet-facing systems they can't patch yet.

The disclosure got messy: BleepingComputer, citing the PHP team, says details (including the CVE numbers) leaked online about two days before the planned release, so PHP rushed the date forward.

Sources (5): BBC · The Verge · CISA alert · Canadian Centre for Cyber Security AL26-026 · BleepingComputer
Why it matters: Short outages on sites you use this week may be planned patching, not a hack.
🤖 Who Found It (With AI)
AI HELPED CRACK A BUG HIDING IN PLAIN SIGHT FOR OVER A DECADE!

The flaws were found by researchers at security firms Calif and Wiz (owned by Google), with OpenAI's security team also credited, according to the official php2shell.com disclosure page. Fortune and Gigazine report Calif found the first issue on Aug 1 and Wiz independently on Aug 19. The BBC says Calif and the PHP Foundation were part of an OpenAI program called Patch the Planet, which gave them access to advanced AI tools. Wiz says frontier AI models (it names Wiz Atlas, OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol and Google's Gemini 4 Argon) made building working attacks practical "in hours", work it calls "at the edge of what human researchers could practically achieve."

The PHP Foundation told The Verge an attack like this "was theorized" back in 2024 but no concrete way to pull it off was found at the time.

Sources (6): php2shell.com (disclosure site) · Wiz Research (finders) · @wiz_io on X · Fortune · BBC · The Verge
Why it matters: Expect more of these: the same AI that finds bugs for defenders makes them faster to weaponize for crooks.
🔥 Is It Being Used to Attack?
ATTACKS STARTED BEFORE THE PATCH, BUT HOW MANY WORKED IS UNKNOWN!

Amazon's security chief CJ Moses wrote that Amazon's decoy servers (honeypots) caught a PHP2Shell attempt on Oct 17, four days before the fix, from an anonymizing network, and that the flaws are now "broadly exploited in the wild." But Amazon adds it is "unclear if any of these attempts would have succeeded in real-world systems." Cloudflare's CTO told the BBC he's "seeing early attacks emerging." Insurer Coalition logged a spike in scanning just before the release but called the numbers "still small."

Official agencies were more cautious as of Oct 21: CISA said it had not seen exploitation at publication, and Canada's Cyber Centre said a proof-of-concept exists but no active exploitation had been identified "at this time, but that can change quickly." Bottom line: lots of attempts, no public confirmation yet of mass break-ins.

Sources (6): AWS Security Blog · BleepingComputer · BBC · Coalition · CISA alert · Canadian Centre for Cyber Security AL26-026
Why it matters: Site owners should check their logs back to Oct 17, not just from the patch date.
⚖️ Hype vs. the Measured Take
OPINION
“70% OF THE INTERNET HACKED”? NOT SO FAST, SAY THE EXPERTS!

The big claim: Wiz's headline says the flaws affect "70% of the Internet" and rank "among the most severe… arguably in software history." That 70% is PHP's share of websites whose server language is known (Coalition cites 69.3% from W3Techs); the BBC says it can't verify the figure. Exposed-server counts vary: BleepingComputer cites VirusTotal at more than 5.1 million PHP hosts; one analysis (detqt, one source) counted about 2.2 million servers announcing PHP on Shodan on Oct 22, about 1.31 million showing an unpatched version.

The measured view: Red Hat rates the bugs lower (8.1, "Important") because "reliable exploitation requires precise heap manipulation and timing," per detqt's summary. Published attacks needed lab conditions and often thousands of requests, and no reliable spray-at-any-site exploit is public yet. Researcher Daniele Berardinelli (opinion) says much of what's being seen is scanning and replayed non-working scripts, "not proof" of successful takeovers. The memory-leak half is easier, and enough to steal passwords.

YouTube second opinion: Security YouTuber LiveOverflow tried to rebuild the exploit with AI from public hints and "got really close" but couldn't finish it. He says a lot of viral "exploits" on X were "AI hallucinating slop" and that it's "not as easy as go find a zero day in PHP", while also warning: "This vulnerability is serious, so don't underestimate it." Another explainer, SimplyExplain, puts it bluntly: "'70% of the internet got hacked' is the headline. It's wrong." (opinion)

Sources (7): Wiz Research (finders) · Coalition · BleepingComputer · detqt analysis · Daniele Berardinelli (researcher blog, opinion) · LiveOverflow on YouTube · SimplyExplain on YouTube
Why it matters: Serious, but it's not a 'the internet is broken' moment for regular users. It's a patch-it-now moment for anyone running a server.
🛠️ What You Should Do
THE TO-DO LIST: SHORT FOR MOST FOLKS, URGENT FOR SITE OWNERS!

Phones, laptops, browsers: PHP runs on web servers, not on your devices, so there's no app or browser update for this one. Keep normal updates on anyway. The real risk to you is data stolen from a site you use, so watch for password-reset emails and phishing that mentions a site's "security incident." Use different passwords per site and turn on two-step login.

Tesla, Starlink, home routers: none of the advisories we read name cars, Starlink dishes or home routers. Some network gadgets (like storage boxes or devices with a web admin page) can bundle PHP, and the NCSC says to watch for vendor updates for "appliances and services that may bundle PHP"; install firmware updates when offered. (We found no report naming a specific home device.)

Small businesses with a website: this is the part that matters. Ask your web host or web person today, in writing: "Is our site's PHP updated for PHP2Shell (8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36 or 8.2.35)?" Running PHP 8.1 or older? It's out of official support: upgrade or take it offline. Big hosts and networks (Cloudflare, AWS, Automattic/WordPress.com and others) deployed protective filters; Automattic posted that its sites are protected (one source). Firewall filters help, but every agency says only the actual update fully fixes it. If your site was unpatched, have someone check for signs of a break-in back to Oct 17.

Sources (6): Canadian Centre for Cyber Security AL26-026 · Cyber Centre statement · UK NCSC · CISA alert · Datadog Security Labs · Automattic (WordPress.com)
Why it matters: For B.C. trades shops with a WordPress site for quotes or job photos: one call to your host is the whole job.
