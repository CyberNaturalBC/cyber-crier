🚨 BREAKING
GREEN WAVE AT CITY HALL! PETE FRY TOPPLES KEN SIM AS VANCOUVER'S FIRST GREEN MAYOR!

Yes, it's true: Pete Fry of the Green Party of Vancouver (a civic party, separate from the B.C. Greens) beat one-term incumbent Ken Sim of ABC Vancouver on Saturday. The City of Vancouver's unofficial count has Fry at 98,494 votes to Sim's 67,467 (about 47.8% to 32.7%), with TEAM's Colleen Hardwick third (18,105), the Vancouver Liberals' Kareem Allam fourth and Vote Vancouver's Rebecca Bligh fifth. Earlier election-night tallies quoted by the Vancouver Sun and BIV were lower (about 89,700 to 63,000) because not every poll had reported; the city's numbers are the latest. Official results, plus the two public-safety plebiscite questions, are due Wednesday Oct 21 by 4 PM. Turnout rose to about 43%, from 36% in 2022, the city says.

Council swings hard left with no single-party majority: four Greens (Annette Reilly, Stephanie Smith, Camil Dumont, Bridget Burns), four COPE (Sean Orr, the top vote-getter, plus Chloe Leslie, Devyani Singh, Solomon Yi-Kieran), OneCity's Lucy Maloney, and ABC's Mike Klassen as the party's lone survivor of its 2022 sweep. ABC was shut out of park board and school board. Sim conceded by phone; CTV quotes him saying voters "wanted to go in a different direction." Fry and council are sworn in Nov. 2 (BIV).

Sources (6): City of Vancouver unofficial results · Vancouver Sun · CBC BC live · CTV Vancouver · BIV / Vancouver Is Awesome (Mike Howell) · X: @TrendPolCa post
Why it matters: Vancouver sets the tone for Metro Vancouver on housing, regional transit and TransLink votes, so a left-leaning council there shapes the regional table Surrey sits at.
 
WHO IS PETE FRY? STRATHCONA DESIGNER, TWO-TERM COUNCILLOR, SIM'S LOUDEST CRITIC!

BIV reports Fry is a longtime Strathcona resident who worked as an illustrator and designer, and ran or worked in small businesses, before winning a council seat in 2018; he was re-elected in 2022 and served eight years with the Union of B.C. Municipalities. He is the son of longtime Liberal MP Hedy Fry (Vancouver Sun, CTV). Over the last term he led opposition to ABC's attempt to abolish the elected park board, its pause on new supportive housing, a study of putting city funds into Bitcoin, and he filed the complaint that led the integrity commissioner to find Sim and ABC colleagues broke local-government rules by meeting privately before votes (ABC disputed that). The Greens had never run for mayor; COPE and OneCity folded their mayoral bids to back him and limited council candidates to avoid vote-splitting (BIV). Fry said he raised money in the "low triple digits" against ABC's more than $2 million (BIV, one source for those figures).

Sources (4): BIV / Vancouver Is Awesome (Mike Howell) · Vancouver Sun · CTV Vancouver · Daily Hive
Why it matters: A mayor with a council-and-UBCM background, not a business-executive one: expect a process-heavy, coalition-style city hall.
 
THE FRY PLATFORM: FASTER WOOD-FRAME HOUSING, NON-POLICE CRISIS TEAMS, INFLATION-LEVEL TAXES, BROADWAY BIKE LANES!

From the Greens' own platform and releases: Housing: pre-approved (including prefab) designs, streamlined permits for low-rise apartments, townhomes, row homes and infill, a push for mass-timber and wood-frame building with Canadian procurement and hiring rules on major projects, a partnership with Build Canada Homes, an "affordable housing czar," a fund to buy at-risk rental buildings, and affordability targets tied to 30% of median income. He backs reversing the pause on new supportive housing (Vote Compass, citing a Sept. 8 debate). Taxes: he supports property-tax increases roughly in line with inflation instead of Sim's "zero means zero" freeze, and promises a line-item budget review first (Daily Hive). Safety: a 24/7 non-police Community Response Outreach Workers (CROW) team for non-emergency crises and an Office of Community Safety; police (24% of the budget) get independent performance reviews and more Police Board reporting. @VanGreens posted: "People in crisis need help, not handcuffs." Business: one contact person for permits, lower permit costs, vacant-storefront fast-tracks, and a living wage for the city and major contractors. Streets: convert at least 11% of road space to green and active-transport uses, bike lanes on Broadway, more 30 km/h zones. Drug policy: the platform frames it around outreach and supportive housing; he called ABC's drug-use plebiscite a "non-binding, performative election stunt" (@PtFry, Aug. 14 post).

Sources (6): Vancouver Greens: housing plan · Green Platform 2026 (PDF) · @VanGreens post · @PtFry post · Daily Hive: platform · BIV / Vancouver Is Awesome (Mike Howell)
Why it matters: B.C. tradespeople: faster permits and a wood-frame/mass-timber push could mean more mid-rise work; living-wage and Canadian-content rules could change bids on big Vancouver jobs.
 
WHY SIM FELL, AND WHAT EACH SIDE NOW EXPECTS!

Explanations so far: BIV points to the fallout of the 2026 property-tax freeze (about $150 million in cuts and new revenue per the Sun, plus layoffs and rising fees), a string of unexplained senior-staff departures, the integrity-commissioner finding, and ABC attack ads that Fry says made him the "anti-Sim" choice. Fry called the result "a repudiation and rejection of ABC's style" (BIV). UBC analyst Hugh Chan told the Sun "voters are angry" in a province-wide anti-incumbent wave (analysis). ABC's pitch was a fully funded police and fire service, a second tax freeze, and plebiscites on public drug use and involuntary care (@KenSimCity, election-day post).

Supporters expect faster housing approvals, protection for renters, the CROW team and a more open budget. Critics on the right worry about the opposite: the Greater Vancouver Board of Trade backed the freeze and wants increases capped at the lesser of inflation or 4% (CityNews, 2025); retail groups say business tax rates are already up (Vancouver Television); ABC warned the 11% road conversion means 220 km of lost roads and 30 km/h arterials (@Vote4ABC, campaign claim); and Sim supporters on X fear police cuts given COPE's Sean Orr's influence (opinion posts, e.g. @DrewCouver56). Fry's platform does not propose defunding police; it proposes performance reviews. The plebiscite results on Oct. 21 will test the new council early.

Sources (8): BIV / Vancouver Is Awesome (Mike Howell) · Vancouver Sun: Sim state of the city · Vancouver Sun: 5 take-aways · CityNews (2025) · Vancouver Television · @KenSimCity post · @Vote4ABC post · @DrewCouver56 post (opinion)
Why it matters: Property owners and contractors: Vancouver's 2027 budget (likely an inflation-level hike, not zero) is the first real test; watch permit fees and the Broadway decision.
 
UPDATE
SURREY: MCCALLUM ROARS BACK, UNSEATING LOCKE IN THE REMATCH!

Brenda Locke was the sitting mayor, so this is an incumbent defeat too. With all 164 tabulators counted, Doug McCallum (Safe Surrey Coalition) had 42,741 votes (31.9%) to Locke's (Surrey Connect) 30,886 (23.1%), ahead of Mike Starchuk, Linda Annis and Troy Van Vliet (Vancouver Sun, Apna News citing city results). The Sun says Safe Surrey won almost the whole council; the only non-slate councillor is Locke-team incumbent Harry Bains, the top vote-getter. It is McCallum's fifth term (1996-2005, 2018-22, now). CBC says he promised a four-year property-tax freeze, more police including a Surrey Police Service helicopter, SkyTrain to Newton and a Newton hospital, a ward system, a forensic audit of land transfers, partial withdrawal from Metro Vancouver, and "protected heritage zones" for Crescent Beach and Ocean Park. Former councillor Barinder Rasode told CBC it was a vote on Locke's handling of the policing transition and the tax hikes that followed (analysis). Locke: "The voters have truly spoken."

Sources (4): Vancouver Sun: Surrey · CBC BC · Apna News · CTV Vancouver
Why it matters: Surrey homeowners: a promised 4-year tax freeze plus a helicopter and SkyTrain asks; watch how the numbers add up in the first budget. Builders: heritage-zone exemptions could block multiplexes in South Surrey.
 
INCUMBENTS TUMBLE ACROSS METRO AND THE VALLEY!

Other sitting mayors who lost on Oct. 17: Langley Township: Jay Lundgren (Langley Strong) beat Eric Woodward 18,302 to 15,459; his team took six of eight council seats. He's a business-degree local, coach and father of four running on an independent forensic audit, no new borrowing until it's done, and public budgets (Langley Advance Times, Langley Strong; the Langley Union notes no costed tax range yet). New Westminster: Daniel Fontaine (New West Progressives), a Métis former councillor and ex-Métis Nation BC CEO, beat Patrick Johnstone about 55% to 45% on fiscal discipline, safer streets and infrastructure repair (Global, Vancouver Sun). Delta: newcomer Melissa Granum (BCLC governance director, police-board governance background) won 48% over Dylan Kruger and incumbent George Harvie (23%), promising more harmony on council (Vancouver Sun). Maple Ridge: Jerry Kok beat Dan Ruimy 58% to 42% (Poliwave tally, Vancouver Sun). West Vancouver: Kirk LaPointe beat Mark Sager; Port Moody: Haven Lurbiecki beat Meghan Lahti (Global, Sun). Langley City's Nathan Pachal was also ousted (Vancouver Sun list; winner not confirmed here, one source). Outside the region, Kamloops, Prince George and Penticton incumbents lost too.

Open seats, not incumbent defeats: Richmond (Rob Howard, after Malcolm Brodie retired), Coquitlam (Craig Hodge), White Rock (Chris Shields), Chilliwack (Jason Lum, 82%; Ken Popove was not on the ballot per the Poliwave tally). Incumbents who held on: Burnaby's Mike Hurley, Abbotsford's Ross Siemens, Port Coquitlam's Brad West, Pitt Meadows' Nicole MacDonald.

Sources (7): Global BC · Vancouver Sun: 5 take-aways · Langley Advance Times · Langley Strong: Lundgren · Langley Union · Vancouver Sun: New West · Poliwave tally
Why it matters: Next door to Surrey, the new Langley Township council's audit-first, no-new-borrowing stance could slow capital projects and the contracts that come with them.
