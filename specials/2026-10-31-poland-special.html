🚨 The Big Story

🚨 Breaking

TWO SIDES, ONE COURTHOUSE: POLAND'S TOP COURT HAS RIVAL BOSSES

The short version: On Friday, judges picked by Prime Minister Donald Tusk's parliament went into Poland's Constitutional Tribunal under police guard and suspended its PiS-era president, Bogdan Święczkowski, and his deputy.

Read more

President Karol Nawrocki, a PiS ally, turned his Senegal-bound plane around, pushed his way into the building overnight, told the new judges "you are not judges" and left about 4 hours later. He calls it a "forcible takeover". As of Saturday morning Pacific time, the government-backed judges hold the building, Święczkowski's side says it is the real court, and riot police and PiS protesters are still outside.

Nobody has been hurt badly, as far as reports show. Police detained 3 people at the fence Friday night and arrested a man with a knife on Saturday. Is it a coup? That word is one side's framing, used by PiS, the president's office and Święczkowski's court account. The government calls it restoring the rule of law. Even some lawyers who oppose PiS say the legal basis for the suspension is shaky (see The Law, below).

Sources (5): Reuters via MarketScreener · AP News · BBC News · Notes from Poland (Sat) · Euronews

Why it matters: Poland is NATO's front-line state next to Ukraine and one of the EU's biggest economies. A fight over who runs its top court touches defence, trade and the 2027 Polish election.

🚪 After the President Walked In

Update

FOUR HOURS INSIDE: SHOVES, 'YOU ARE NOT JUDGES', A FIGHT OVER KEYS, AND NO DEAL

Getting in: Nawrocki reached the court just before 1 a.m.

Read more

Warsaw time with Supreme Court chief Kapiński and his aides. The judges inside say they were waiting at a side door to greet him and invite him to a meeting room. Korwin-Piotrowska told TVN24 the president "did not intend to stop" and "began pushing me aside with his hands". His camp tells it the other way round. Spokesman Rafał Leśkiewicz and aide Marcin Przydacz say Korwin-Piotrowska and judge Krystian Markiewicz "breached the inviolability of the head of state". Berek says nobody grabbed the president. Several videos exist, and each side reads them its own way. Who pushed first is disputed.

Inside: He told the judges they were not judges. Bogucki was heard accusing Korwin-Piotrowska of a coup d'état. The president's statement says he "enabled the entry" of vice-president Sochański, and Sochański then sat in his own office overnight. Berek says presidential staff tried to get the keys and the judges refused. Sochański held talks with the new judges. He later said he had been told, falsely, that the president had already left. Żurek says the talks produced no compromise. (AP wrote that Nawrocki said he helped the "suspended court president" get in. The president's own statement and Polish media name the vice-president, Sochański. Święczkowski himself was kept outside.)

Leaving: At about 5 a.m. Warsaw time (9 PM PT Friday) the statement was read outside and Nawrocki left. The new judges kept the building. Żurek called the president's conduct "shameful" and said he hopes prosecutors act. A sitting president has immunity, so that is mostly words for now.

Saturday's talks at the palace: PSL (Kosiniak-Kamysz, a deputy PM and defence minister) went to talk about "state security... social peace". Poland 2050's Hołownia said the court "in this shape can't be saved" and saw "will" from the president to talk. PiS sent Kaczyński and Błaszczak. Razem's Zandberg told Nawrocki he was wrong to "leave the role of arbiter and become a party". Morawiecki (Rozwój Plus) announced a constructive no-confidence motion with Marek Jurek as caretaker PM. Confederation's Mentzen wants all 15 judges' terms ended and 15 new ones appointed. No joint agreement had been announced at press time.

Sources (8): Notes from Poland (Sat) · Onet live (Polish) · Rzeczpospolita live (Polish) · GazetaPrawna (Polish) · Euronews · AP News · PAP · dpa via Yahoo

Why it matters: The president has no police or army to command in peacetime. His only lever, as ex-president Duda put it, is "the strength of his mandate", so the fight now moves to politics and the street.

⚖️ The Law: Rival Courts, Rival Judges

Explainer

WHY BOTH SIDES SAY THE OTHER ONE IS BREAKING THE LAW

The court: The Constitutional Tribunal decides whether laws fit Poland's constitution.

Read more

Under Article 194 it has 15 judges elected by the Sejm (lower house) for 9-year terms. Under Article 190 its rulings are final and must be published. A separate law says new judges swear their oath "before the president".

2015, the original sin: PiS's parliament put judges into 3 seats the previous parliament had already filled. Europe's human rights court (ECtHR) and the EU's top court (CJEU) later ruled the tribunal was not an "independent and impartial tribunal established by law". Politico says the CJEU ruling came last December. Since 2023 Tusk's government has refused to publish the tribunal's rulings, and that itself is disputed under Article 190.

2026: Parliament has elected 8 new judges since March. Nawrocki swore in only some of them. He cited problems with how they were chosen, and says the oath is not automatic. The 5 he refused, including Korwin-Piotrowska, Markiewicz and Tusk ally Maciej Berek, took their oaths in parliament instead. Święczkowski would not let them hear cases, even after the ECtHR ordered in May that they be allowed to sit. Earlier this month the Venice Commission (the Council of Europe's legal advisers) said the president's oath role is ceremonial.

Friday's legal hook: The suspension relies on a chain of laws. The tribunal judges' law points to the Supreme Court law, which points to Article 130 of the common-courts law (an immediate "break in duties" for a judge caught committing an offence). Korwin-Piotrowska acted as the judge with the "longest tenure", but that counts her whole judicial career since 1995, not time on this court. Doubters on the anti-PiS side: Prof. Marcin Matczak says the basis is "nonexistent for a million reasons" and that the justice minister can't oversee this court. Ex-tribunal judge Stanisław Biernat says the mechanism was "permissible" but he has doubts. Defenders: Ex-tribunal president Jerzy Stępień says it is "fully justified", because the 2 men can't rule on their own immunity cases (which must be decided within 30 days). He also says the president had "no right" to enter at night.

The president's side cites his constitutional role as guardian of the constitution (Article 126). Supreme Court First President Kapiński backs him and called it "undoubtedly a coup d'état". The Supreme Court is a different body from the tribunal.

Sources (6): Euronews explainer · BBC News · Notes from Poland (Fri) · Politico Europe · Rzeczpospolita live (Polish) · Onet live (Polish)

Why it matters: Rzeczpospolita's editor put it bluntly: each side knows its own move is legally doubtful and doesn't care. That's why outsiders can't just look up who's right.
