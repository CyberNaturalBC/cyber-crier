🔭 Science & Research

ASTRONOMERS WATCH A STAR SPIRAL INSIDE ITS GIANT PARTNER, THEN MERGE, IN ANDROMEDA

NASA says data from NEOWISE, SPHEREx and Webb "clinched" a first: a star slightly less massive than the Sun spiraled into a puffy giant star in the Andromeda galaxy and merged with it, after brightening slowly for seven years.

Read more

What the paper actually says: in Nature (published Nov. 18, peer-reviewed; preprint arXiv:2611.23470), Karambelkar, De, Temmink, Metzger and colleagues say event AT 2025abao is "best explained" by a ~0.7-solar-mass companion inspiraling inside the envelope of a ~7-solar-mass AGB (late-life giant) star, the first direct observation of the friction-driven inspiral of common envelope evolution.

The companion then merged with the core within fewer than 10 orbits, throwing off the outer ~10% of the envelope, and the team pins the share of orbital energy that goes into ejecting the envelope at between 1% and 40%, a wide range. It is one event, interpreted with models; "best explained" is the authors' wording. An earlier paper by the same lead author (ApJ, 2025) had flagged the slow brightening as a possible onset.

Sources (4): NASA Science · Nature (paper) · arXiv:2611.23470 (preprint) · ApJ 2025 (earlier paper)

Why it matters: Common-envelope mergers make the close binaries that later become gravitational-wave sources; this is the first one caught in the act.

ANTARCTIC ICE CLIFFS COLLAPSE FASTER THAN MODELS ASSUME, BUT DON'T RUN AWAY

Press coverage says Antarctica's ice cliffs are collapsing faster than expected, "but there's a twist." What the paper actually says: in Science (peer-reviewed; Needell, Walker and Bassis), the team measured 15,330 ice

Read more

cliffs around Earth's ice sheet margins with laser altimetry and found that cliff height tracks ice-mélange thickness, water depth and crevassing; tall cliffs calve faster than models assume, but thinning and retreat into thinner ice stop runaway retreat.

So models may underestimate short-term calving, but the feared "marine ice cliff instability" chain reaction wasn't seen; the authors say the risk depends on several factors, not cliff height alone. The data cover about four years of satellite observations, so long-term behaviour is still an open question.

Sources (2): ScienceDaily (WHOI release) · Science (paper)

Why it matters: Sea-level projections feed into coastal and Fraser River dike planning; this trims one worst-case scenario.

ARE URANUS AND NEPTUNE 'ROCK GIANTS'? A NOBLE GAS COULD TELL, BUT ONLY WITH A PROBE

Universe Today frames a new study as asking whether the "ice giants" are secretly rock giants.

Read more

What the paper actually says: in The Planetary Science Journal (peer-reviewed; Mousis and four co-authors), the team models how much argon-40 in the planets' air could be leftover from their birth versus made by radioactive potassium in rock, and finds the primordial share could be anywhere from about 1 to 610 times solar levels in Uranus and 1 to 1,220 in Neptune, depending on where and when their building blocks formed.

That means argon-40 can reveal rock content only under certain formation conditions; it is a modelling study, with no new measurement. Using it as a test would need a future probe to measure the planets' argon directly.

Sources (2): Universe Today · Planetary Science Journal (paper)

Why it matters: A case for sending an atmospheric probe with any future Uranus mission.

SPACE DUST POINTS TO A HIDDEN 'HEAVY WATER' RESERVOIR BEYOND NEPTUNE

Universe Today says 12 dust grains collected by a high-altitude aircraft reveal a massive isolated reservoir of "heavy water" (rich in oxygen-17 and -18, not deuterium) beyond Neptune.

Read more

What the paper actually says: in Science Advances (peer-reviewed; Keller and three co-authors from NASA Johnson and UCLA), hydrated interplanetary dust particles with dense solar-particle tracks, which point to a Kuiper Belt origin, carry oxygen isotopes that implicate 17,18O-rich ices, "compelling evidence" for such a reservoir largely isolated from the inner solar system.

The sample is small, a dozen grains, and the Kuiper Belt origin is inferred from the track density. It supports the long-standing theory that UV light reshaped oxygen isotopes in the outer disk.

Sources (2): Universe Today · Science Advances (paper)

Why it matters: Helps explain why Earth's water and rocks differ from the Sun's makeup.

What the paper actually says: in Food Control (peer-reviewed; University of Parma with Cranfield), researchers tested 92 meat alternatives and 120 plant-based drinks from UK shops for 19 mycotoxins and found emerging Fusarium toxins such as beauvericin in 98.9% of meat alternatives and enniatins in about 93.5%; meat alternatives had significantly higher levels than drinks.

The press release says the levels were below EU guidelines; the authors call for more monitoring of combined exposure, not a health warning. UK products only.
