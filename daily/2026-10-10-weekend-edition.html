🌎 World & Politics
UPDATE
UPDATE: ISAIAS SLAMS FLORIDA PANHANDLE, THEN FIZZLES OVER ALABAMA

Isaias made landfall near Destin, Florida, around 8:30 PM CDT Friday with 105 mph winds, the U.S. National Hurricane Center says; a gust of 116 mph was recorded near Santa Rosa Sound and water at Panama City Beach ran 5.6 ft above normal high tide. AP reports hundreds of thousands in Alabama and the Panhandle were without power early Saturday, with flooded roads and downed trees. The BBC notes it is the Atlantic's first hurricane of the season, the first time in more than 30 years none formed before October. By 4 AM CDT it was a post-tropical storm near Montgomery and the NHC issued its last advisory.

Sources (3): National Hurricane Center · AP News · BBC
Why it matters: Gulf refinery and insurance hits can ripple into fuel prices here.
 
CANADA SHEDS 68,000 JOBS; B.C. DOWN 20,000

Statistics Canada says employment fell by 68,000 in September, the second straight drop, and unemployment rose to 6.5%, far worse than the 9,200 gain economists expected. The Canadian Press (via BNN Bloomberg) says that wipes out all of 2026's job gains; the biggest losses were in the public sector (health, education), though manufacturing lost 12,700. The Globe and Mail reports B.C. and Ontario each lost about 20,000 jobs while Alberta added 23,000, and economists still expect the Bank of Canada to hold rates Oct. 28. Wage growth sped up to 2.3%.

Sources (3): Statistics Canada · BNN Bloomberg / CP · The Globe and Mail
Why it matters: A softer job market in B.C. heading into winter; rate cuts aren't a sure thing yet.
 
M7.7 QUAKE ROCKS PANAMA; CANAL UNHARMED

A magnitude 7.7 earthquake struck southern Panama's Herrera province Friday around 12:56 PM local time, AFP reports, collapsing buildings including a cathedral, damaging roads and injuring at least 27; no deaths were reported. CBC and the BBC also covered the quake, which brought dozens of aftershocks, including a 6.6. Tsunami warnings were lifted and AFP says the Panama Canal wasn't affected.

Sources (3): AFP · CBC News · BBC
Why it matters: The Canal carries a big share of Pacific trade, so an intact canal matters to West Coast shipping.
The U.S. sanctioned the International Criminal Court hours after former judge Navi Pillay won the Nobel Peace Prize, The Guardian reports. The Free Press (opinion, centre-right) ran Elisha Wiesel arguing Pillay's win “betrayed” the prize. (The Guardian · The Free Press Big Read (newsletter, opinion))
Police in Poland detained 76 people and checked 50,000 schools after two attacks shook the country, AP reports. (CityNews / AP)

⚔️ Wars & Conflicts
🚨 TRUMP STRIKES RUSSIAN DIESEL DEAL WITH PUTIN

After a phone call with Putin, President Trump said Russia will supply more than 300,000 tons of diesel right away, 500,000 more in November and a million after that, AP reports via NPR; Treasury issued a licence allowing Russian diesel sales until April 7, 2027. CNBC says critics call it a contradiction of a Russia sanctions law Trump signed three weeks ago. Zelenskyy called it “a weak decision of strong partners” (CNN), and RFE/RL says Ukraine called it a gift to the Kremlin as Miami peace talks ended without a breakthrough. Supporters point to record diesel prices; CNN says the first two shipments equal about a day and a half of U.S. demand, and a Eurasia Group analyst called it “a bandaid.”

Sources (4): NPR / AP · CNBC · RFE/RL · CNN via KRDO
Why it matters: Diesel moves trucking and job-site costs; analysts doubt this dents the price much.

🚀 Tesla / SpaceX / Starlink / xAI
TESLA DROPS 'FULL SELF-DRIVING' NAME IN EUROPE

Electrek reports Tesla's driver-assist is now called “Tesla Assisted Driving” in Europe after Germany's Transport Ministry called the FSD name misleading. In return Berlin will back EU-wide approval, but with the system capped at 10% over the speed limit; Reuters reported the Dutch-approved version allowed up to 50%. Eight EU countries allow it now, and an EU vote has slipped to December at the earliest. In the U.S. it's still sold as FSD (Supervised); no Canada change was reported.

Sources (2): Electrek · Reuters (via Electrek)
Why it matters: Regulators pushing names and speed caps hints at the terms Canada could set.

SURREY ADVANCE VOTE TODAY; POLL HAS LOCKE AHEAD, A THIRD UNDECIDED

Advance polls are open today 8 AM–8 PM at 10 sites, including Cloverdale Recreation Centre (6188 176 St) and École Panorama Ridge Secondary (13220 64 Ave); bring your voter card and one piece of ID, the city says. A Leger online poll (Sept. 23–30, 1,207 Metro Vancouver residents) has Mayor Brenda Locke at 24% to Doug McCallum's 12% in Surrey, with 34% undecided; Locke rises to 34% among people certain to vote. The same poll found 64% of Surrey residents say it's time for a change on council. Leger's sample for Surrey alone is smaller than its region-wide one, so read the gap with care.

Sources (3): City of Surrey · Leger · CityNews
Why it matters: Wanting change but leaning to the incumbent: the undecided third decides this one.
