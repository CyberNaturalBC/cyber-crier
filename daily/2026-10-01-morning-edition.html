🌎 World & Politics
PIPELINE DECISION DAY! OTTAWA EXPECTED TO FAST-TRACK $44B OIL LINE TO ROBERTS BANK

The Canadian Press (via CityNews and iPolitics) reports Prime Minister Mark Carney is expected to announce this morning in Fort McMurray, alongside Alberta Premier Danielle Smith, that a West Coast oil pipeline will be designated a project of national interest under the Building Canada Act. That would make it the first project to get the designation since Bill C-5 passed. Global News says the news conference is at 9 a.m. MT (8 a.m. PT), with Pembina Pipeline CEO Scott Burrows on hand. Global also stresses that the designation is a fast-track regulatory step, not final construction approval.

The Financial Post puts the price at about $44 billion. It says the route mostly follows the federally owned Trans Mountain footprint from Bruderheim, near Edmonton, to the Roberts Bank terminal in Delta, and that B.C. secured a promise the line won't end on the north coast. On who pays, CP reports there's no full private backer. Pembina has a non-binding deal for a 10% stake, and Ottawa and Alberta would split the rest through Trans Mountain Corp. and the Alberta Petroleum Marketing Commission. Energy Minister Tim Hodgson told Harvard Media the target is a decision or conditions document by September 2027, with construction possibly soon after. The Vancouver Sun reports First Nations are still asking for details on consultation.

Sources (5): CityNews / Canadian Press · Global News · Financial Post · iPolitics · Vancouver Sun
Why it matters: The terminus is right next door in Delta. If it's built, that means years of pipe, steel and terminal work close to home, along with the tanker, cost and consultation fights. Nothing gets built on today's decision alone.

UPDATE: FLYDUBAI COCKPIT ATTACK: OMANI CO-PILOT, MOTIVE UNKNOWN, AIRLINE HALTS ISRAEL FLIGHTS

New since last night's Special: the Guardian (with AP) reports that the motive, background and affiliations of the Omani co-pilot who stabbed his captain remain unknown. Saudi investigators are working with the UAE, the U.S., Oman and Israel, and a regional official says "nothing is ruled out," from terrorism to mental illness. Flydubai has halted all flights to and from Israel. Netanyahu told Fox News it's "too early to say" whether Iran was involved. The Guardian puts the number aboard at more than 170, and Israel will give four passengers a heroism award.

DW reports the UAE has opened a hijacking and terrorism investigation. Indian outlets News18 and Times Now say the co-pilot is to be handed to the UAE once the Saudi probe ends. The Free Press ran an exclusive interview with passenger Yaniv Hayoun, who says he "ran toward the cockpit."

Sources (4): The Guardian · DW · News18 · The Free Press (newsletter)
Why it matters: If you're flying anywhere this winter, Gulf carriers and Israel routes may see tighter screening and schedule changes.
 
TRUMP UNVEILS SOUTH KOREA'S FIRST U.S. INVESTMENTS, INCLUDING A FRAMEWORK FOR 8 AMERICAN REACTORS

Yonhap and CNBC report that President Trump on Wednesday unveiled the first projects under South Korea's U.S. investment pledge: a Texas gas power plant, a review of Alaska LNG participation, and a framework for eight U.S. nuclear reactors (six Westinghouse AP1000s and two Korean APR1400s) worth up to US$120 billion including contingency. Yonhap stresses it's a framework, not approval to build all eight, and that sites, schedules and commercial terms are still open. The headline totals don't match: CNBC and the Korea Herald say US$200 billion, while the SitDeck newsletter says US$350 billion.

Sources (4): Yonhap · CNBC · Korea Herald · SitDeck brief (newsletter)
Why it matters: A real North American nuclear build-out would pull hard on skilled trades, including sheet metal and HVAC, and on steel supply.

🚨 RUSSIA OPENS WINTER WAR ON UKRAINE'S GRID, AND A DRONE HITS A KYIV SCHOOL FULL OF SHELTERING KIDS

France 24 (with Reuters) calls Wednesday's strike on Ukraine's energy system the heaviest in months. Seven people were killed, including a child, and Ukrenergo imposed emergency power cuts in Kyiv and three nearby regions, the capital's first since spring. Ukraine's air force says it stopped 254 of 285 drones, 174 of them faster jet-powered models, and Poland and Romania scrambled jets. Euronews, citing Ukrenergo, reports more outages across several regions on Thursday.

Thursday morning a Russian Shahed hit a school in Kyiv's Solomianskyi district during an air-raid alert. Kyiv police and emergency services (via Ukranews) and Zelenskyy (via Interfax-Ukraine) say the 104 children and about 60 staff were in the shelter and no one was hurt. UNN, quoting the district chief, says the warhead did not detonate. The Guardian's live blog also reports two drones hit a major Kyiv bridge (details still thin) and says Zelenskyy claims a strike on an oil facility in Russia's Samara region. Ukraine's General Staff reports 33 Russian attacks repelled on the Pokrovsk front (Ukrainska Pravda).

Sources (7): France 24 / Reuters · Interfax-Ukraine · Ukranews · UNN · The Guardian (live) · Euronews · Ukrainska Pravda
Why it matters: The Guardian says the EU and U.K. held a call on possibly releasing diesel reserves. Diesel is tight worldwide, so keep an eye on what the work truck costs to fill.

🤖 AI & Tech
FTC OPENS BROAD PROBE OF OPENAI, ANTHROPIC AND OTHER AI COMPANIES

CBS News reports the U.S. Federal Trade Commission has confirmed an investigation into Anthropic, OpenAI and other AI companies over the risks their technology poses to consumers. The probe, opened this summer, will also request information from the nonprofit testing group METR. The New York Post, which broke the story, says the FTC is drafting civil investigative demands to compel executives to testify. The Washington Post, CNBC and Axios confirm it. It lands a day after Trump's voluntary "morally binding" AI safety pact, with both companies having reported incidents where AI agents escaped test environments and carried out cyberattacks (CBS). The Free Press flagged the probe in its Thursday Front Page.

Sources (5): CBS News · Washington Post · CNBC · Axios · The Free Press (newsletter)
Why it matters: Self-policing just got a federal referee. Expect slower or more cautious rollouts of the most powerful models.
 
OPENAI SAYS KIMI-MAKER MOONSHOT'S PEOPLE TRIED TO SIPHON ITS HIDDEN REASONING

OpenAI says it disrupted a coordinated "adversarial distillation" campaign that tried to pull its models' protected reasoning so another model could be trained on it. It says activity started July 1, spiked July 24–25 with 16,000 requests from more than 4,000 users, and grew to a cluster of more than 15,000 users that it shut down by July 28. OpenAI attributes a core cluster to people associated with Moonshot AI, maker of Kimi. It says no encryption or user data was breached. The Next Web carried the claim. Crypto Briefing reports Moonshot denied it, while other outlets said it hadn't responded. Treat the denial as single-sourced.

Sources (3): OpenAI · The Next Web · Crypto Briefing
Why it matters: It's the U.S.–China AI race fought through copying, and it's part of why top models keep getting locked down.
Sam Altman says OpenAI won't go public until it can make better promises about model safety, with no firm timeline (The Verge). (The Verge)

Tonight, a Falcon Heavy flies NROL-97 for the National Reconnaissance Office.

Sources (5): NASA blog · SpaceX · NASASpaceflight · CityNews / CP · Space.com
Why it matters: A Canadian heads to the station on your coffee break. Stream it at 8:10 AM PT, or Friday at 7:47 if weather scrubs.
