FAA'S THIRD-EVER NATIONWIDE GROUND STOP STRANDS MORE THAN A MILLION TRAVELLERS

Every U.S. flight was grounded for more than four hours Friday afternoon after a telecommunications failure in the air traffic system, only the third nationwide ground stop in FAA history, Ars Technica reports.

Read more

🔭 Science & Research

WEBB FINDS ICE IN BETA PICTORIS'S DISK THAT LIKELY FORMED BEFORE THE STAR DID

The James Webb telescope has found water and carbon dioxide ice in the dusty disk around Beta Pictoris, a 20-million-year-old star 64 light-years away, and the water ice is glassy rather than crystalline, Ars Technica reports, which suggests it formed in deep space before the star.

Read more

Ars headlines it as ice "older than the star it orbits."

What the paper actually says: Betti et al., Science, Nov. 12, 2026 (peer reviewed) report amorphous water ice with under 7% crystallinity and CO2 ice mixed in at about 1:10. They "infer" the ices "likely" formed in the interstellar medium, a reasonable reading but an inference, not a measured age. The even spread of ice implies frequent collisions in the disk. Per Ars, the team couldn't pin down exactly how much crystalline ice there is or rule out other molecules.

Sources (2): Science (paper) · Ars Technica

Why it matters: If water-rich ingredients survive around young stars, watery planets may be common.

PHYSICISTS FILM ELECTRONS 'JUMPING' A FORBIDDEN GAP IN GRAPHENE, FRAME BY FRAME

A team in Regensburg, Marburg and Jülich used intense light pulses and a photoemission momentum microscope to watch electrons in graphene tunnel between energy bands, the Landau-Zener-Majorana transition, ScienceDaily

Read more

reports from the Jülich release, which calls them "seemingly impossible quantum jumps."

What the paper actually says: Eggers et al., Nature 659, 63 (2026), peer reviewed (preprint arXiv:2602.12844), introduce "subcycle band-structure videography" across graphene's whole Brillouin zone with attosecond precision and visualize this tunnelling directly. Nothing here breaks physics: the effect is textbook quantum mechanics, and what's new is filming it. The press release's 100% transition figure wasn't in the abstract we read.

Sources (3): Nature (paper) · arXiv:2602.12844 (preprint) · ScienceDaily

Why it matters: Seeing electrons move at light-speed timescales is a step toward electronics far faster than today's chips.

LOFAR MAKES THE DEEPEST LOW-FREQUENCY RADIO MAP YET: 63,000 SOURCES IN ONE PATCH OF SKY

Astronomers led by Leiden's Jurjen de Jong used 200 hours of LOFAR observations to make the sharpest, deepest low-frequency radio image to date of the ELAIS-N1 field, about 63,000 radio sources in an 8-billion-pixel image from 400 terabytes of data, Universe Today reports.

Read more

What the paper actually says: de Jong et al., arXiv:2611.06152 (a preprint; ASTRON says it's accepted in Astronomy & Astrophysics), catalogue 62,916 sources over 2.5 by 2.5 degrees at about 0.3 arcsecond resolution, the deepest radio images below 1.28 GHz so far. Their main claim is technical: such wide, sharp imaging is now computationally feasible, laying groundwork for LOFAR2.0 and the SKA.

Sources (3): arXiv:2611.06152 (preprint) · ASTRON · Universe Today

Why it matters: A methods milestone: the software, more than the dish, is what opens the next generation of sky surveys.
