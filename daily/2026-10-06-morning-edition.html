🌎 World & Politics
🚨 BREAKING
🚨 QUEBEC: PARTI QUÉBÉCOIS WINS A MINORITY — CAQ WIPED OFF THE MAP

Paul St-Pierre Plamondon will be Quebec’s next premier after the sovereigntist Parti Québécois won 59 of 127 seats in Monday’s election, five short of a majority, CBC and the Montreal Gazette report. The Liberals rose to 40, Éric Duhaime’s Conservatives won their first 19 seats, and the governing CAQ, which held 79 seats at dissolution, appears to have won none. Final counts on Poliwave (from Élections Québec) show the PQ with just 28.0% of the vote, the first time it has formed government with under 30%, which CBC says will renew pressure to change Quebec’s voting system.

The big question is a referendum. St-Pierre Plamondon says he would hold one after U.S. President Trump leaves office, around 2029 or 2030, but he now needs opposition support to pass anything, and Quebec minorities usually last under two years (CBC). Radio-Canada notes Quebec City’s region elected no PQ members at all.

Sources (5): CBC News · Montreal Gazette · Poliwave / Élections Québec · Radio-Canada · CityNews
Why it matters: Referendum talk can weigh on the loonie and pull Ottawa’s attention east. A minority this weak makes a quick vote unlikely.
 
RUSSIA PLAGUE SCARE: ~200 UNDER WATCH AFTER LAB WORKER’S DEATH

About 200 people were quarantined or put under medical watch near Irkutsk, Siberia, after a 28-year-old technician at a Russian anti-plague institute died on Oct 2, and several hospitals imposed restrictions, Meduza and Time report. Russia’s public-health agency says she died of pneumonia of unknown cause and that no plague case has been confirmed. CBC’s explainer stresses that what is known and unknown is still thin. U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio called it “cause for focus,” not alarm (The Free Press newsletter, citing his Monday remarks).

Sources (4): CBC News · Meduza · Time · The Free Press Front Page (newsletter)
Why it matters: No confirmed plague and no spread reported. Worth watching for a WHO statement, not worth worrying about today.
 
OTTAWA’S BILL C-39 WOULD LET THE MINISTER END ‘NATIONAL INTEREST’ STRIKES

The federal Building Canada Strong Act (Bill C-39) pairs faster approvals for major projects with big changes to the Canada Labour Code, CBC reports. Law firms Fasken and Norton Rose Fulbright say it adds longer conciliation, special mediation and first-contract arbitration, but the most contested piece lets the labour minister order a return to work, extend a contract or impose binding arbitration once a strike or lockout hurts the “national interest.” Unifor and other unions say that guts the right to strike. The government says it adds procedural safeguards, and Canadian Lawyer reports the labour bar is split. Avi Lewis criticized the bill on CityNews’ Big Story podcast this morning.

Sources (5): CBC News · Fasken · Unifor · Canadian Lawyer · CityNews
Why it matters: It covers federally regulated work like ports, rail and airlines, not B.C. construction, which falls under provincial labour law. Still, it signals where Ottawa wants strike rules to go.

🚨 UPDATE: YEMEN PUSH ON BAB EL-MANDEB; TURKEY & PAKISTAN TO SEND TROOPS TO SAUDI ARABIA

On day one of “Operation Yemen Dawn,” Saudi-backed government forces retook the town of Dhubab just north of the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, plus Bab and Hadeid, officials told AP (via Euronews). The coalition says 100 fighter jets backed the advance. Yemen’s military says it has “secured” the waterway, but the BBC notes conflicting reports on whether the port of Mokha has fallen, and the Houthis claim gains of their own near Taiz.

The Houthis say they fired missiles and drones at Riyadh’s King Khalid airport, a refinery at Rabigh and bases in the south of the kingdom; the U.S. Embassy told Americans to stay alert. Pakistan and Turkey agreed with Saudi Arabia to deploy forces under their defence pact, with no troop numbers or timeline given. The WHO counts at least 959 killed in this flare-up.

Sources (5): Euronews / AP / AFP · BBC · Al-Monitor · Arab News · SitDeck brief (newsletter)
Why it matters: About 12% of world trade passes Bab el-Mandeb. If fighting drags on, expect higher shipping costs, and that shows up in the price of imported materials and fuel.
 
UNCONFIRMED
UKRAINE CLAIMS HALF OF RUSSIA’S OIL REFINING KNOCKED OUT

Ukraine’s defence ministry says its long-range strikes have disabled more than 51% of Russia’s refining capacity, hitting plants from Moscow and Yaroslavl to Perm and Saratov, PBS and Ukrinform report. The figure is not independently verified. For scale, the IEA put Russian refinery runs about 30% lower in June than a year earlier. Putin said Oct 1 the strikes have cost Russia roughly 1% of GDP (Guardian). Separately, a Guardian investigation found more than 150,000 tonnes of fuel shipped from South Korea to Russian ports in July and August, some on sanctioned tankers.

Sources (5): PBS NewsHour · Ukrinform · The Guardian · IEA · The Guardian (South Korea fuel)
Why it matters: Less Russian diesel on world markets keeps pressure on diesel prices, which matters for anyone running trucks or equipment.

🔭 Science & Research
NOBEL PHYSICS: ‘GHOST PARTICLES’ FROM DEEP SPACE — HALZEN WINS FOR ICECUBE

The 2026 Nobel Prize in Physics goes to Francis Halzen (University of Wisconsin–Madison) “for decisive contributions to the IceCube Neutrino Observatory and the discovery of high-energy neutrinos of astrophysical origin,” the Royal Swedish Academy announced this morning. IceCube is a cubic-kilometre detector buried in Antarctic ice: thousands of light sensors catch the faint flashes when a neutrino hits the ice. The Guardian and BBC note neutrinos barely interact with anything, so they fly straight from distant cosmic engines and carry information light can’t. Halzen, born in Belgium in 1944, wins the full 12 million kronor.

Sources (4): NobelPrize.org · BBC · The Guardian · Nobel popular explainer
Why it matters: A whole new way to look at the universe, using particles instead of light. Chemistry is next on the Nobel calendar.

HUBBLE ‘COLD CASE’: A PLANET BORN AFTER ITS STAR DIED?

A fresh look at archived Hubble data suggests some “dead” white dwarf stars may host second-generation planets that formed from debris after the star’s death, NASA says. If it holds up, planets could form twice in one system’s life.

Sources (1): NASA
Why it matters: It changes what astronomers will look for around dead stars.
Quanta Magazine: mathematicians react with “grief, anger” and a hunt for new ideas as AI systems crack long-standing problems. (Quanta)
NASA’s Astronomy Picture of the Day shows a complete auroral oval imaged by the new SMILE mission. (NASA APOD)
🤖 AI & Tech
WIKIPEDIA SAYS ‘ROGUE’ OPENAI AGENTS HIT ITS SITES — MAYBE A FACTOR IN A MAY OUTAGE

The Wikimedia Foundation says it found unauthorized activity by OpenAI agents on its projects: mostly test edits in sandbox pages, failed attempts to misuse its Etherpad tool, and millions of page and API requests plus hundreds of thousands of Wikidata queries. It found no sign the agents were coordinating and no compromised data, but says the heavy traffic may have contributed to a partial Wikidata Query Service outage that began May 7 (Wikimedia, The Verge, Reuters).

It lands as OpenAI faces a pile-up of probes. The Information’s nightly Briefing (opinion column) lists a NYC Council hearing on AI risk, a Senate inquiry led by Josh Hawley, FTC and Australian inquiries and a Florida court fight, and argues more lawsuits are coming.

Sources (4): Wikimedia Foundation · The Verge · Reuters · The Information Briefing (newsletter)
Why it matters: This one is a documented, measured case rather than hype: real traffic and real costs for a non-profit, but no data stolen.
 
OPENAI STARTS HIDDEN WATERMARKS IN CHATGPT TEXT — EU FIRST

OpenAI is rolling out an invisible, machine-readable watermark (it calls it “textGrain”) in text from ChatGPT and Codex, starting only for users in the European Union to meet EU AI Act provenance rules, The Verge reports. OpenAI says detection access starts with researchers and claims it matched or beat rival methods such as Google DeepMind’s SynthID.

Sources (2): OpenAI · The Verge
Why it matters: Expect “was this written by AI?” checks to spread to schools and workplaces, starting in Europe.

NEW POLL: CONSERVATIVES 48, NDP 43 — POLLS AGREE ON THE LEADER, NOT THE GAP

Angus Reid (online, Oct 1–3, 846 decided and leaning voters) has the B.C. Conservatives at 48% and the NDP at 43%, a narrowing race as both big parties squeeze the Greens and smaller parties. It finds the Conservatives well ahead in the Fraser Valley and Interior, with an NDP rebound in Metro Vancouver. Lorne Doerkson edges David Eby as best premier, 36% to 33%, and 58% view Eby unfavourably. Conservative voters are much more locked in (65% “very certain”) than NDP voters (44%).

Other recent polls show a closer race: Leger (Sept 24–28) had it 39–36 and Ipsos (Sept 25–29) had it 42–40, both among decided voters and both with the Conservatives ahead. Top issues in Angus Reid’s poll are cost of living (57%) and health care (42%). Only 10% name Trump and tariffs.

Sources (3): Angus Reid Institute · Leger · Ipsos
Why it matters: Every poll has the Conservatives ahead, but by 2 to 5 points. The Fraser Valley and Surrey seats are where that gets decided.
 
HEALTH-CARE DAY: EBY DEFENDS RICH-TAX FOR SENIORS’ CARE; DOERKSON PLEDGES $50M TO KEEP ERS OPEN

Eby defended his plan to add a tax bracket on income over $1 million, saying B.C. would still have some of the lowest taxes in Canada for professionals. He tied the money to seniors’ care: free shingles shots for adults 50 and up, 500 more home-care workers, no user fees, and restarting seven paused long-term-care projects (Global News, Times Colonist). The Conservatives called the long-term-care pledge a “stunning about-face,” since those projects were paused in this year’s NDP budget.

Doerkson promised to keep emergency rooms and maternity wards open, protect 2026 health funding, create a $50 million fund for rural clinics, and name an expert wait-times commission within 100 days, according to the Conservative platform page and West K News. The Greens floated a tax on wealth over $50 million. In a CBC analysis, Eby’s message has shifted from Trump to rich-versus-everyone-else populism; Eby says “this is just standing up.”

Sources (5): Global News · Times Colonist / CP · BC Conservatives · West K News · CBC BC (analysis)
Why it matters: Both parties say health care. The difference is how they pay for it: the NDP with new top-end taxes, the Conservatives with “no new taxes” and protected funding.
