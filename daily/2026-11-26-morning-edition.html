BIGGEST TRIAL OF ROBOT-ASSISTED KNEE REPLACEMENT: MORE PRECISE, NO BETTER FOR PATIENTS AT A YEAR

The UK's RACER-Knee trial found that knee replacements done with Stryker's Mako robotic arm were more precise but left patients with the same pain, mobility and recovery after 12 months as conventional surgery, and no more serious complications (University of Warwick via ScienceDaily).

Read more

What the paper actually says: a peer-reviewed, randomized, patient- and assessor-blinded trial in The Lancet of 339 patients (168 robotic, 171 conventional). Twelve-month Oxford Knee Scores were 37.3 vs 36.9, an adjusted difference of 0.3 points (95% CI −1.8 to 1.9), so no meaningful difference. It was co-funded by NIHR and Stryker, the robot's maker.

Robots are used in about 6% of UK, 16% of U.S. and 42% of Australian knee replacements.

Why it matters: If a surgeon pitches the robot as the better option, the best evidence so far says your knee won't feel the difference.
