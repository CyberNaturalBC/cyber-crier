B.C. HOLIDAY-MARKET VENDORS HIT BY SOPHISTICATED COPYCAT SCAM

A sophisticated email scam has been targeting holiday-market vendors across B.C., costing some small businesses more than $1,000, CBC reports Sunday.

Read more

Scammers scrape real market websites for schedules, table sizes and artist names, then send polished invitations that look like official applications. Victoria ceramic artist Sue Britton paid more than $700 in extra “fees” after a fake acceptance to the Out of Hand Modern Market; Kamloops shop owner Calli Duncan lost over $1,100 to a scam posing as the Kamloops Christmas Market.

Real organizers say the wave is province-wide and repeating every couple of weeks. The tell: check that the sender address matches the market’s usual official address, not a look-alike personal webmail account, and confirm directly with the organizer before sending an e-transfer. RCMP did not confirm to CBC whether formal reports have been filed.

Sources (1): CBC News

Why it matters: If a “market invitation” asks for an e-transfer, call the organizer on a number you already trust before sending a dollar.

ONE GRIZZLY CAPTURED NEAR BELLA COOLA AFTER FATAL ATTACK

B.C. conservation officers have captured one grizzly near Bella Coola as they hunt for any bear tied to the attack that killed a mother and daughter, the Vancouver Sun reports.

Read more

The Conservation Officer Service said the animal will be safely held while DNA, measurements and other identifiers are compared with evidence from the attack site. NEW ZEALAND: RIGHT BLOC ON 67 SEATS — SPECIALS COULD STILL FLIP IT

New Zealanders will wait until Nov. 27 for an official result after Saturday’s election left Christopher Luxon’s National–ACT–NZ First bloc with only a wafer-thin preliminary majority, RNZ and Reuters report.

Read more

With 98.5% counted on election night, National had 29.3% of the party vote and 42 of 64 electorates; Labour also had 29.3% and 35 seats. ACT took 10.9% (13 seats) and NZ First 9.8% (12), putting National–ACT–NZ First on 67 of a projected 131-seat parliament — one above the 66 needed. The Greens had 13.3% (16 seats) and newcomer Opportunity crossed the 5% threshold at 6% (7 seats). Read more

CHAINALYSIS SAYS HACKERS GOT CREDENTIALS AND TOKENS FOR EVERY USER

Blockchain-analytics firm Chainalysis told customers that attackers obtained names, usernames, password hashes and live tokens for all of its users after an October breach, Ars Technica reports.

Read more

MODEL: EARTH’S TIDES MAY HAVE KEPT THE EARLY MOON VOLCANICALLY ALIVE

What the paper actually says: Chen and colleagues model tidal heating at the Moon’s core-mantle boundary to explain volcanic basalts only about two billion years old from China’s Chang’e-5 mission.

Read more

Standard cooling models implied the relatively small Moon should have gone geologically quiet long before those lavas erupted. Radioactive decay and late asteroid strikes look too weak. The authors argue that 2 billion years ago the Moon orbited closer (about a 16-day period versus 27 today) on a more eccentric path, so Earth’s gravity kneaded its interior. In a 3D simulation that includes a low-viscosity layer around the core, two symmetric zones — just 2.5% of mantle volume — delivered about 150 gigawatts around 3.2 billion years ago, more than four times the heat from radioactive decay, helping sustain a dynamo and later mantle plumes whose leftover melt produced the young basalts.

This is a theoretical convection/tidal model, not a new sample result. The authors present it as an explanation that still needs testing by future lunar missions. Peer-review status: published in Planet; press coverage via Universe Today / Phys.org.

Sources (2): Planet paper (DOI) · Phys.org / Universe Today

Why it matters: Young lunar lava has been a genuine puzzle — this is a model with numbers, not proof the Moon is still cooking.

TRAPPED IONS RECREATE AHARONOV–BOHM INTERFERENCE IN A GAUGE-THEORY LOOP

What the paper actually says: Saner and colleagues used two trapped ions to simulate a Z₂ lattice gauge theory in which a matter particle sees Aharonov–Bohm interference around a loop of flux.

Read more

Using two strontium ions, the team encoded gauge fields in electronic qubits and matter in vibrational modes (phonons). A single link showed a particle tunneling while a field line appeared and vanished, consistent with a Z₂ version of Gauss’s law; adding an energy cost for the field suppressed the tunneling — a precursor of confinement. With two ions forming a loop, entangled qubits stored either zero flux or a vison-like nonzero flux. Zero flux let the particle tunnel twice as fast as on one link; nonzero flux made the two paths cancel and froze the particle in place. The encoded flux stayed constant aside from slow decoherence.

Limits are explicit: this is a single loop, small enough for a classical computer to solve. The authors want larger lattices and richer Zₙ gauge theories. Peer-review status: published in Nature Physics.

Sources (2): Nature Physics paper (DOI) · Phys.org

Why it matters: A tabletop demo of flux controlling matter is neat quantum-simulation progress — not yet a shortcut past classical supercomputers.
