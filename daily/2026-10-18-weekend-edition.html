EXPLOSION AND FIRE KILL AT LEAST SEVEN AT CFB WAINWRIGHT

At least seven people are dead and dozens injured after an explosion and fire at Canadian Forces Base Wainwright in east-central Alberta, Global News reports, citing multiple military sources. Those sources say a live-fire training exercise hit a gas line or gas well around 4 PM MT Saturday, and strong winds pushed the fire beyond the training area and across the base fence. The Canadian Armed Forces confirms deaths and injuries but won't give numbers until next of kin are told; Defence Minister David McGuinty said he 'cannot recall an incident like this in recent memory.'

Several Alberta hospitals declared a Code Orange mass-casualty response, and STARS air ambulance sent two helicopters, CityNews and CBC report. By 11 PM the Municipal District of Wainwright said the fire was being held. Saskatchewan is sending help, and Premier Eby said B.C. has offered help too. The military's family information line is 1-800-866-4546.

Sources (4): Global News · CBC · CityNews (Canadian Press) · National Defence statement
Why it matters: The death toll is from unnamed military sources and could change; the cause points to live fire near gas infrastructure.
 
FRANCE'S COST-OF-LIVING MARCHES TURN TO RIOTS; LIMOGES CITY HALL BURNS

Police arrested 855 people in the Paris region after masked groups clashed with officers and looted shops on the edges of a march of about 110,000 people protesting living costs and school conditions, AP reports. Authorities counted about 550,000 marchers across France and organizers about 800,000. One person died of a heart attack, and 13 police officers and two firefighters were hurt. Limoges' 19th-century city hall was gutted by a fire the local prefect calls deliberate, and protesters forced their way into Marseille's city hall lobby.

Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu blames radical far-left groups, and the mayors of Paris, Marseille and Lyon are seeking a meeting. X posts summarized by X report a similar march in Milan turned violent. X summaries also say President Macron will address the nation tonight, which no outlet we checked has confirmed.

Sources (4): CityNews (AP) · X news summary: France riots (Grok on X) · X news summary: Milan (Grok on X) · SitDeck Daily Brief (newsletter)

UKRAINE'S 1,000-DRONE RAID SETS ST. PETERSBURG'S OIL TERMINAL ABLAZE

Ukraine launched more than 1,000 drones at Russian oil depots, warehouses and data centres overnight, one of its biggest air raids of the war, The Guardian reports; Russia says it downed 1,015. St. Petersburg's governor says fires broke out at the city's oil terminal, an oil depot and warehouses, and the retailer Ozon and tech firm VK confirmed hits on a logistics centre and a data centre. About 20,000 people in the Leningrad region lost power, and Russian officials report one dead and about 15 hurt.

Russia kept hitting Ukraine's grid: a woman was killed in Zaporizhzhia, and Zelenskyy says Russia attacked Ukraine's energy, oil and gas sites 76 times in the week since Trump announced his 'energy ceasefire'. U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio calls Trump's waiver of new Russia sanctions a '60-day pause' to study the economic impact. Lithuania told residents to shelter for an hour after an object crossed its airspace; Ukraine apologized, saying Russian jamming may have pushed its drones off course.

Sources (4): The Guardian · CityNews (Reuters/AP) · The Guardian: Rubio's '60-day pause' · Kyiv Independent

UPDATE: IRAN SAYS ITS SEVEN-DAY HORMUZ OFFER STILL STANDS

Iran's offer to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days is 'still on the table' if the U.S. lifts its blockade of Iranian ports, drops oil-export sanctions and ends hostilities across the region, Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Sunday, Reuters reports. Under the plan the strait would open within six days and talks would start on day seven; Washington, with Vice-President JD Vance in the lead, wants Iran's nuclear program dealt with first. Iran's Revolutionary Guard says two supertankers carrying about two million barrels each caught fire after hitting mines on unauthorized routes; ship names haven't been released and the claim isn't independently verified, Al Jazeera and AFP report.

The numbers on traffic disagree. Kpler data cited by Al Jazeera show about 20.8 million barrels through the strait on Oct. 8 but only 2.9 million on Oct. 11. U.S. Central Command's Adm. Brad Cooper says about 400 merchant ships passed in the past week, according to X posts summarized by X; we couldn't find that figure in a news report.

Sources (4): Arab News (Reuters) · Al Jazeera (AFP) · X news summary: CENTCOM (Grok on X) · SitDeck Daily Brief (newsletter)
Why it matters: Until ships move freely through Hormuz, oil near US$100 keeps fuel and freight costs high.

JAPAN LAUNCHES A ROUND TRIP TO MARS' MOON PHOBOS ON MONDAY

JAXA's Martian Moons eXploration (MMX) probe is set to launch on H3 Flight 10 from Tanegashima at 4:41 AM Tuesday Japan time, which is 12:41 PM PT Monday, with a backup window running to Nov. 9. The probe will study both Martian moons, Phobos and Deimos, land on Phobos and bring a surface sample back to Earth in 2031, JAXA and ESA say. X posts summarized by X say the goal is at least 10 grams of material and that it carries a small rover named IDEFIX.

Sources (3): JAXA: launch time · ESA · X news summary: MMX (Grok on X)
Why it matters: It would be the first sample ever brought home from a moon of another planet, a clue to where Mars' moons came from.
 
ONE SOURCE
THE BRAIN MAY FLIP BETWEEN WHAT IT SEES AND WHAT IT IGNORES 15 TIMES A SECOND

A new preprint from Doris Tsao's lab at UC Berkeley, led by Janis Hesse, recorded neurons in macaque monkeys while showing each eye a different image, a trick that makes the animal consciously see one while the brain suppresses the other. The team reports that activity in visual and prefrontal areas switched between representing the seen image and the hidden one about every 60 milliseconds, roughly 15 times a second. X posts summarized by X quote Hesse calling it the most important discovery of her career and say outside experts want to see peer review first.

Worth the caution: it's a bioRxiv preprint, not yet peer-reviewed, it's in monkeys, and it measures how the brain tracks two competing images, not consciousness itself.

Sources (2): bioRxiv preprint · X news summary (Grok on X)
Why it matters: If it holds up, it suggests awareness depends on timing in the brain more than on any one region.

CLAUDE CRACKS A 25-YEAR NSA CRYPTO RIDDLE IN HALF AN HOUR

An internal Anthropic research model traced the unexplained 'seed' numbers the NSA supplied for widely used NIST encryption curves in the late 1990s back to an ordinary random-number function, lrand48, fed with timestamps from 1997 and 1998, CoinDesk reports. It took under 30 minutes and matched the published seed for the common P-256 curve on the 1,842nd try. Cryptographers had chased the question for 25 years because the unexplained seeds fed suspicion of a hidden NSA backdoor.

Anthropic says that makes a backdoor much less likely and users of the curves don't need to change anything, though some choices in the curves are still unexplained. Bitcoin uses a different curve and was never on the list. The $36,864 bounty goes to the nonprofit behind Let's Encrypt.

Sources (1): CoinDesk
Why it matters: AI is starting to solve problems experts gave up on, and it can audit the security the internet runs on.
 
TERENCE TAO ON OPENAI'S FLOOD OF 722 AI MATH PAPERS: 'NO MORE HEROES'

On Numberphile's YouTube channel, UCLA mathematician Terence Tao says math is moving from 'proof scarcity' to 'proof abundance' after OpenAI dumped about 722 AI-generated manuscripts on Oct. 6. Of the roughly 10 he read, some were predictable, a few genuinely new, many incomprehensible, and some wrong or later withdrawn. He faults OpenAI for releasing them without asking mathematicians first and for wiping out projects junior researchers were working on, but credits one with a major new result on the Riemann zeta function.

X posts summarized by X say OpenAI also announced a US$5 million donation for math research; we haven't confirmed that in a news report.

Sources (2): Numberphile (video): Terence Tao · X news summary (Grok on X)
Why it matters: A top mathematician says AI output is piling up faster than people can check it, and that's a quality-control problem.

MCCALLUM IS BACK: SAFE SURREY TAKES THE MAYOR'S CHAIR AND A BIG COUNCIL MAJORITY

Doug McCallum won back the mayor's job with 42,241 votes (31.8%) to Mayor Brenda Locke's 30,750 (23.1%), with 163 of 164 tabulators in, the Surrey Now-Leader reports. Mike Starchuk (Imagine Surrey) was third at 12.9%, Linda Annis (Surrey First) fourth at 12.4% and Troy Van Vliet (Surrey Now) fifth at 11.3%. Annis says vote-splitting handed McCallum the win. It's his fifth term, CBC notes; he lost to Locke by fewer than 1,000 votes in 2022.

The Now-Leader's count puts seven Safe Surrey candidates on council: Doug Elford, Meera Gill, Mandeep Nagra, Suhana Gill, Allison Patton, Laurie Guerra and Cheney Cloke. Surrey Connect's Harry Bains topped the poll as the lone non-slate councillor. The Vancouver Sun says six seats, and Cloke led Surrey First's Mike Bose by only about 150 votes late in the count, so watch the official results. On school board, Surrey First Education held five of six seats, and Surrey Now's Michelle Ceniza took the sixth, bumping longtime trustee Gary Tymoschuk. Trustee Garry Thind was re-elected even though he asked people not to vote for him: he's the B.C. Conservative candidate in Surrey-Guildford, and if he wins that seat Saturday he's deemed to have resigned, which could force a trustee by-election.

McCallum promised a four-year property-tax freeze while adding police resources, including a Surrey Police helicopter, plus a SkyTrain push to Newton, a new Newton hospital, a ward system, a partial exit from the Metro Vancouver regional district and a forensic audit of recent city land transfers, CBC reports. Former councillor Barinder Rasode told CBC the vote was a verdict on Locke's handling of the police transition; Locke said 'the voters have truly spoken.'

Sources (6): Surrey Now-Leader: election night · CBC · Surrey Now-Leader: Annis · Surrey Now-Leader: Thind · Vancouver Sun · CTV News
Why it matters: With a council majority, McCallum's tax freeze and police promises will be tested at his first budget.
 
AN ANTI-INCUMBENT WAVE: NEW MAYORS IN VANCOUVER, BOTH LANGLEYS AND DELTA

Green councillor Pete Fry beat Vancouver Mayor Ken Sim by more than 20,000 votes, becoming Canada's first Green mayor, the Vancouver Sun reports. In Langley Township, Langley Strong's Jay Lundgren beat Mayor Eric Woodward 18,302 to 15,459, and his slate won six of eight council seats and all five Township school-board seats, the Langley Advance Times reports. Langley City elected Delaney Mack over Mayor Nathan Pachal. In Delta, Melissa Granum took 49% to beat Dylan Kruger and two-term Mayor George Harvie. Richmond elected Rob Howard, but none of his council slate won. New Westminster, West Vancouver, Port Moody and Maple Ridge also changed mayors, while Burnaby's Mike Hurley and Port Coquitlam's Brad West were easily re-elected.

The outlets read it differently. Analyst Hugh Chan told the Sun 'voters are angry' and may turn that on the province next Saturday. CBC's analysis finds no clear left-right pattern: Vancouver moved left, while many cities picked experienced rivals promising to calm feuding councils. The Tyee (left-leaning) tallies the many mayors ousted around the province.

Sources (6): Vancouver Sun: 5 takeaways · CBC: analysis · Langley Advance Times: Township · Langley Advance Times: City · BIV: Fry · The Tyee (left-leaning)
Why it matters: New mayors next door means new faces at the TransLink and Metro Vancouver tables Surrey deals with.
