🔭 Science & Research

TWENTY YEARS OF DARK ENERGY DATA: THE STANDARD MODEL IS SHOWING CRACKS

The Dark Energy Survey's full six-year results, combined with DESI, hint that dark energy may be weakening over time, meaning the textbook model of the universe may be incomplete, Quanta Magazine reports; "There appear to be cracks in that model," Berkeley Lab's Simone Ferraro says.

Read more

What the paper actually says: DES alone (supernovae, BAO and weak lensing) prefers evolving dark energy at only 2.2 sigma; adding DESI gives 2.3 sigma, and adding the cosmic microwave background gives 3.0 sigma (arXiv:2605.27221). That's a hint, not a discovery (physicists want 5 sigma), and leaving out each probe gives 2.3-3.2 sigma. NOIRLab's summary of the main paper says DES data "mostly aligned with the standard model" but the clumping of matter is still off. The papers are preprints submitted to journals.

Sources (3): arXiv:2605.27221 (preprint) · Quanta Magazine · NOIRLab

Why it matters: If dark energy really changes over time, the universe's long-term fate gets rewritten; for now it's a strong hint that Euclid and Rubin data will test.

Explainer

'RINGING' BLACK HOLES COULD TEST EINSTEIN, SAYS A 70-PLUS-AUTHOR REVIEW

After black holes merge, the new one "rings" like a struck bell, and measuring those tones, black hole spectroscopy, can test general relativity in its most extreme regime, Universe Today reports on a big review in Classical and Quantum Gravity.

Read more

What the paper actually says: it's a 320-page review (Berti, Cardoso, Carullo and about 70 co-authors, including Perimeter Institute's Niayesh Afshordi), not new observations. It sums up theory and modelling of the tones (quasinormal modes), what LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA has seen so far, and what LISA and next-generation detectors could do. It is peer reviewed (published June 2026; preprint arXiv:2505.23895). Universe Today's "one step closer to solving" is generous for a review.

Sources (3): Classical and Quantum Gravity (paper) · arXiv:2505.23895 · Universe Today

Why it matters: A Canadian physicist is helping write the playbook for the next decade of gravitational-wave tests.

LONG MORNING NAPS LINKED TO EARLIER DEATH IN SENIORS, BUT THE NAP ISN'T THE CAUSE

Older adults who napped longer, more often or in the morning died sooner over up to 19 years of follow-up, ScienceDaily reports, calling it a "troubling link." What the paper actually says: in 1,338 Illinois adults

Read more

(average age 81, 76% women) wearing wrist trackers for about 10 days, each extra hour of daytime napping went with 13% higher mortality, each extra nap with 7%, and morning nappers had 30% higher risk than early-afternoon nappers (just significant: 95% CI 1.03-1.64). Irregular napping showed no link. It's peer reviewed (JAMA Network Open, April 2026, so not new) and correlation only; the authors say heavy napping likely flags hidden illness.

Sources (3): JAMA Network Open (paper) · ScienceDaily · Mass General Brigham

Why it matters: Not a reason to skip a lunch-break nap; for aging parents, a sudden change in napping is worth mentioning to a doctor.
