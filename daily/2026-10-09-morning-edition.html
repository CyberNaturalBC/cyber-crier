UPDATE: ISAIAS NEARS MAJOR-HURRICANE STRENGTH, LANDFALL TONIGHT

Hurricane Hunter data show Isaias’ winds up to 110 mph at 4 AM CDT, and the U.S. National Hurricane Center expects it to become a major hurricane this morning before some weakening. Landfall is expected tonight or early Saturday inside the warning area, most likely near the Alabama–Florida border, as a high-end Category 2. The NHC warns of life-threatening storm surge from the mouth of the Mississippi to Suwannee, Florida (5–7 ft in spots) and long power outages. NBC News is running live coverage as it closes in.

Sources (3): National Hurricane Center · NHC discussion · NBC News
Why it matters: Gulf oil and refinery disruptions can show up at the pump here within days.
 
NAVI PILLAY WINS THE NOBEL PEACE PRIZE

Former U.N. human rights chief Navanethem “Navi” Pillay, 85, a South African-born judge now on the International Court of Justice, won the 2026 Nobel Peace Prize for promoting peace and international law, AP reports. The Nobel committee said judges and institutions of international law are “under political attack” as wars multiply. It hadn’t reached Pillay to tell her when it announced the win. The Guardian is running live coverage.

Sources (2): CityNews / AP · The Guardian
Why it matters: A pick that signals the committee is backing international courts at a time critics call them political.

⚔️ Wars & Conflicts
UPDATE
🚨 TRUMP: NO STRIKE ON IRAN BEFORE THE MIDTERMS

A day after reports that the Pentagon told CENTCOM to get ready to resume the war, President Trump posted on Truth Social that the U.S. “will not be attacking Iran at any time prior to the Midterm Elections” on Nov. 3, calling talks with Tehran “productive” and saying the blockade stays in full force. Politico notes he didn’t say what happens after Nov. 3, and etf.net reports the post didn’t cancel the strike preparations. Brent crude fell from a US$105.91 high to about US$103.65 within minutes. Separately, the SitDeck brief says Iranian media reported “massive explosions” in the Strait of Hormuz overnight, with OSINT accounts claiming U.S. forces hit mine-laying boats; no named outlet has confirmed that.

Sources (4): Axios · Politico · etf.net · SitDeck brief (newsletter)
Why it matters: Less risk of a fresh oil spike for now, but the blockade keeps fuel prices high.
 
UPDATE
UKRAINE: KRAMATORSK TOLL HITS 35; ZELENSKYY ASKS U.S. TO ‘OPEN STARLINK’

The Guardian says Thursday’s Russian strike on two buses in Kramatorsk killed at least 35, pushing the two-day toll from Russian strikes past 70. Sources (2): The Guardian · SitDeck brief (newsletter)
Why it matters: Starlink is now a front-line bargaining chip, which puts SpaceX in the middle.

🔭 Science & Research
JAPAN’S ‘SPACE HEIST’ TO GRAB A PIECE OF A MARTIAN MOON

Science magazine calls Japan’s Martian Moons eXploration mission an “audacious space heist” to bring home bits of Phobos, the mysterious Martian moon. NASA has scheduled a briefing on its contributions to the mission, and today’s NASA Astronomy Picture of the Day is Phobos’ huge Stickney Crater. Scientists still argue whether Phobos is a captured asteroid or debris from an impact on Mars.

Sources (3): Science · NASA · NASA APOD
Why it matters: A sample could settle how Mars got its moons, a big open question in planetary science.

🚀 Tesla / SpaceX / Starlink / xAI
REUTERS: TESLA LEANED ON DUTCH REGULATOR OVER FSD REVIEW

Emails obtained by Reuters show Tesla pushed the Dutch regulator RDW to speed up and scale back its FSD review, calling a €365,000 bill for five months of work an “extremely high cost” and holding payment until it got an hour-by-hour accounting, Electrek reports. The Dutch approval underpins FSD in Europe: eight EU countries now allow it and a bloc-wide vote could come in December. No Canada FSD change was reported.

Sources (2): Electrek · Reuters (via Electrek)
Why it matters: Europe’s FSD approval path is a template Canada’s regulators could look at.

SURREY MAYORAL HOPEFULS GANG UP ON LOCKE AT DEBATE

All seven mayoral candidates met Wednesday at the Sheraton Guildford, and most aimed their one free question at incumbent Brenda Locke (Surrey Connect), the Vancouver Sun reports: Honveer Singh Randhawa (Conservative Party of Surrey) raised her election signs on public property, Troy Van Vliet (Surrey Now) asked about the police chief, and Doug McCallum (Safe Surrey Coalition) asked why she closed Fraser Downs in Cloverdale. Promises flew: McCallum, SkyTrain down King George and 60-day permits; Randhawa, 600 police officers and one-week permits for houses and renos; Ammons II (New Surrey+), 60-day permits for big projects and 10 for small; Starchuk (Imagine Surrey), a more open city hall. The Surrey Citizen adds McCallum would cut development cost charges and Randhawa would phase them out. Both forums are on the Board of Trade’s YouTube.

Sources (3): Vancouver Sun · Surrey Citizen · Surrey Now-Leader
Why it matters: Permit speed and police numbers are the clearest differences before tomorrow’s advance vote.
 
ONE SOURCE
THE BIG THREE’S PITCHES: TAX FREEZE, $1.4B PLAN, 300 OFFICERS

CityNews sums up the front-runners: Locke’s $1.4-billion plan puts $600 million into recreation and community facilities and $485 million into public safety; McCallum promises a four-year property-tax freeze, SkyTrain to Newton and zero tolerance on extortion; Linda Annis (Surrey First) would add 300 police officers over four years and a police training centre. Voters pick a mayor, eight councillors and six school trustees.

Sources (1): CityNews
Why it matters: Taxes and policing are where these plans differ most for Surrey homeowners.
