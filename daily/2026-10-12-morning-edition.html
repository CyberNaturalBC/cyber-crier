🌎 World & Politics
HURRICANE SIMON GRINDS UP MEXICO'S PACIFIC COAST

AP reports Category 4 Hurricane Simon pounded Mexico's west-central coast Sunday with its eye just offshore, closing in on the Puerto Vallarta area, where the worst winds and rain were expected overnight into Monday morning. The U.S. National Hurricane Center's early-Monday advisory has Simon weakened to about 115 mph, with a hurricane warning from Playa Perula to El Roblito and warnings of life-threatening storm surge, flooding and mudslides. The NHC now expects Simon to run north just off the coast and make landfall later this week before rapidly weakening inland. SitDeck's brief adds that flooding from Hurricane Isaias is still a problem in the U.S. Southeast.

Sources (3): AP via PBS · National Hurricane Center · SitDeck Daily Brief (newsletter)
Why it matters: If you have a fall trip booked to Puerto Vallarta or anywhere on that coast, check with your airline and resort before you go.

UPDATE: RUSSIA HITS UKRAINE'S GRID HOURS AFTER TRUMP'S 'ENERGY CEASEFIRE'

Trump posted Sunday that Russia and Ukraine had agreed to an 'energy ceasefire' effective immediately, with no details; Zelenskyy said it was news to him but he'd back it if Moscow did, NPR and the Guardian report. The BBC says Russian strikes hit power plants within hours, including one in Kharkiv, causing blackouts, and Zelenskyy is now vowing to retaliate if the grid attacks continue. On Monday Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said only that 'contacts are ongoing', and rejected Zelenskyy's offer of a 60-day ceasefire to hold elections, saying Russia wants a full peace deal and would pause strikes only on election day itself (Reuters, Kyiv Independent). Ukraine kept up its own strikes too, knocking out a Yandex data centre east of Moscow, per NPR. In Ottawa, Prime Minister Carney told Zelenskyy that Trump's Russian diesel deal undercuts allied pressure on Russia, his office said.

Sources (6): BBC · NPR / AP · Reuters via StreetInsider · Kyiv Independent · The Guardian · CityNews / CP (Carney)
Why it matters: A real energy truce would ease pressure on oil and diesel prices; so far there's no sign Moscow has signed on.
 
UPDATE
UPDATE: HOUTHIS HIT RIYADH AIRPORT AGAIN; FLIGHTS SUSPENDED

A second Houthi attack in two days hit a terminal at Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport on Sunday, killing one man and injuring 11, the BBC reports, after Saturday's missile killed 13 and injured more than 300. The Guardian says Saudi Arabia has extended the suspension of all flights there while it checks the airport's safety, and its military says it has detected more missile and drone threats. Energy minister Prince Abdulaziz bin Salman told an energy conference in Riyadh the kingdom's response 'must be equally serious'. NPR says it's the deadliest attack on a Gulf Arab state since the Iran war began and raises the risk of direct U.S. involvement. SitDeck's brief also flags a reported drone strike on a tanker off Oman, which no named outlet has confirmed yet.

Sources (4): BBC · The Guardian · NPR · SitDeck Daily Brief (newsletter)
Why it matters: Attacks on Gulf airports and tankers can push up shipping insurance and fuel costs even when no oil supply is actually lost.
 
NORTH KOREA FIRES SEVERAL BALLISTIC MISSILES

South Korea's military says North Korea fired multiple ballistic missiles from the Pyongyang area toward the East Sea on Monday, Yonhap reports. Japan's defence ministry counted at least two: one flew about 220 km and the other more than 910 km, both landing outside Japan's exclusive economic zone, according to the Japan Times. Early social-media reports disagreed on the count, so treat any number above two as unconfirmed for now.

Sources (2): Yonhap · The Japan Times
Why it matters: Another flashpoint for markets already jumpy over the Gulf and Ukraine.
🔭 Science & Research
THE FIRST STAND-ALONE NUCLEAR CLOCK IS TICKING

A TU Wien and PTB team in Vienna reports in Nature the first self-stabilizing nuclear clock: a laser locked to a tiny energy jump inside thorium-229 nuclei in a crystal, corrected continuously by the nuclei themselves, with no ordinary atomic clock needed. It ran for more than 24 hours with stability approaching one part in 10^15 over a day. A second Nature paper reports another thorium-229 clock synchronized to the nuclear transition. Nuclear clocks could eventually beat today's atomic clocks and test whether nature's constants drift over time.

Sources (3): Nature · TU Wien · Nature (second paper)
Why it matters: Better clocks mean better GPS and navigation, and a sharper test of whether physics' 'constants' are really constant.

ASTRONOMERS CATCH THE COSMIC WEB SPLITTING IN TWO

Shanghai astronomers say they've directly measured, for the first time, matter in a cosmic filament flowing in opposite directions toward the galaxy clusters at each end. They stacked Sloan Digital Sky Survey galaxy data and subtracted cosmic expansion to get the velocities, avoiding assumptions older methods relied on. The study is in Science China Physics, Mechanics & Astronomy.

Sources (1): Chinese Academy of Sciences
Why it matters: It's a direct look at how gravity builds the universe's largest structures.
Antarctic sea ice peaked on Sept 14 at its third-lowest winter maximum in the satellite record, NASA and the NSIDC say. (NASA Earth Observatory)
New Webb and Hubble images show stellar nurseries, galaxies and gravitational lensing. (NASASpaceflight)
🤖 AI & Tech
SILICON VALLEY FALLS IN LINE WITH TRUMP'S 'SUPER INTELLIGENCE' REBRAND

Trump's Sept 29 executive order tells federal agencies to say 'Super Intelligence' and 'SI' instead of 'artificial intelligence', while keeping the legal definition of AI unchanged, per the White House and the Federal Register. The Verge reports that tech bosses are adopting it anyway: Musk promised to rename SpaceXAI to SpaceXSI, and Marc Benioff, Jensen Huang and now Satya Nadella have used the new term. The order doesn't require private companies to follow along. The Verge, which is critical, calls it capitulation; supporters see it as a harmless nod to the White House.

Sources (3): The Verge · White House · Federal Register
Why it matters: Expect 'SI' to start showing up in U.S. government and contractor paperwork.

SURREY: FIVE DAYS OUT, TURNOUT UP AND TAX PLEDGES ALL OVER THE MAP

The City of Surrey says 10,618 people voted on the first advance day, Oct 7, up 30% from the first advance day in 2022; CBC says several B.C. cities are seeing early-vote surges. Advance voting resumes Wednesday and Thursday, 8 AM to 8 PM at 10 sites, and voters pick a mayor, eight councillors and six SD36 trustees. On property taxes, StudioX News's platform comparison shows McCallum promising no increase for four years, Randhawa a $600 residential cut then a four-year freeze, Annis no increase in 2027, and Locke and Starchuk increases at or below inflation. Leger's Oct 7 poll had Locke at 24%, McCallum 12% and a third of voters undecided. The Tyee (left-leaning) asks today whether municipal candidates can really lower taxes.

Sources (5): City of Surrey · CBC BC · StudioX News · Leger · The Tyee (left-leaning)
Why it matters: Tax freezes and caps mean different things for your bill; ask each camp what they'd cut to pay for it.
 
CAMPAIGN TRAIL: FERRY FIXES OVER THE LONG WEEKEND

With ferry lineups long over Thanksgiving, both big parties made BC Ferries pitches, CTV and CHEK report. NDP Leader David Eby promised a pilot reserving 12% of advance bookings for locals on the Horseshoe Bay–Langdale and Powell River–Comox routes. Conservative Leader Lorne Doerkson promised a BC Ferries Charter with standards for reliability, on-time performance, capacity and affordability, a leaner board, talks with Ottawa for more federal money, and high-speed Wi-Fi on major routes. The Greens, meanwhile, want a public say before any AI data centres are built.

Sources (5): CHEK News · CTV Vancouver · BC NDP release · B.C. Conservatives release · CTV (Greens)
Why it matters: Mostly a coastal issue, but it shows how each party talks about fixing Crown corporations.
Latest polls still have the Conservatives ahead: Liaison 43–37 (Oct 7–8), Mainstreet 44.7–37.2 (Sept 30–Oct 1) and Leger 39–36 (Sept 24–28), a narrower gap. (Liaison Strategies · Indo-Canadian Voice (Mainstreet) · Leger)
