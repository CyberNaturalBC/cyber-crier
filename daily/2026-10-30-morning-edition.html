SPAIN CRASH REPORT: AVE HIT THE STOPPED TRAIN AT 160 TO 200 KM/H

Spain's preliminary technical report says the AVE was doing an estimated 160 to 200 km/h when it hit the stationary Iryo train near Urda.

Read more

🔭 Science (papers read)

CAN EINSTEIN EXPLAIN THE SPEED-UP WITHOUT DARK ENERGY? A MATH PAPER SAYS MAYBE

Mathematicians propose that the universe's accelerating expansion could come from an instability in Einstein's equations, with no dark energy needed.

Read more

What the paper actually says: In Proceedings of the Royal Society A (peer-reviewed, open access, also on arXiv), Alexander, Temple and Vogler analyse pressureless Einstein–Euler equations with radial perturbations. They show the critical (k=0) Friedmann solution is an unstable saddle point. Underdense perturbations accelerate away from it at intermediate times and later settle back, which gives a way to get acceleration without a cosmological constant. It's pure mathematics with no sample and no effect size, and it isn't fitted to supernova or cosmic microwave background data. "Explains the universe without dark energy" is a hypothesis, not a result, and Temple has made similar arguments since 2017.

Sources (3): Proceedings of the Royal Society A (paper) · arXiv preprint version · ScienceDaily (UC Davis release)

Why it matters: Treat this as a serious idea still waiting on data, not proof that dark energy doesn't exist.

LESS BRIGHT DAYLIGHT, HIGHER DEMENTIA RISK, IN 87,577 WRIST-SENSOR WEARERS

A large UK study finds people who got more bright daytime light had a lower risk of dementia.

Read more

What the paper actually says: In General Psychiatry (peer-reviewed, open access), Zheng and colleagues followed 87,577 dementia-free UK Biobank adults, average age 62.4, who wore wrist light sensors for seven days. Over a median of 8.1 years, 741 developed dementia. Daytime light above 1,000 lux was linked to 16% lower risk (HR 0.84, 95% CI 0.71 to 0.99, p=0.039), and at least 0.70 hours at 5,000 lux or more to 17% lower risk (HR 0.83, p=0.036). Circadian rhythms and brain structure accounted for up to 33% of the link, and nighttime light showed no association. It's observational, light was measured for only one week and the confidence interval nearly reaches 1, so this is an association, not proof that light prevents dementia. Claims that light "outperformed" other predictors are about prediction, not cause.

Sources (2): General Psychiatry (paper) · ScienceDaily (Wiley release)

Why it matters: Getting outside at lunch is free and might help. Forty-odd minutes of real daylight is a cheap habit to keep up through winter.

A 4.1-BILLION-YEAR-OLD ROSE-COLOURED ROCK FROM A PART OF MARS NEVER SAMPLED

Teghaza 001, an 800-gram, rose-coloured meteorite found in the Sahara in 2022 and named for a salt plain in Mali, is one of the three oldest of the roughly 435 known Martian meteorites.

Read more

What the paper actually says: In Science (peer-reviewed, paywalled; we read the abstract), Liu, Saper, Bell and colleagues describe a slowly cooled igneous rock that formed at least 4.1 billion years ago. It samples ancient crust from a geochemically distinct, primordial mantle source. It also has water-altered minerals showing reactions with 18O-enriched fluids before an impact blasted it off Mars. It's a single rock, there's no effect size to report, and where it came from on Mars is unknown, though the candidate sites are in the southern highlands. A separate Science Advances paper covers its water content.

Sources (2): Science (paper, paywalled) · NASA

Why it matters: Until a sample-return mission flies, rocks like this are the only pieces of early Mars we can actually test.
