🌎 World & Politics
HOT U.S. INFLATION SINKS STOCKS AND PUTS A FED HIKE BACK ON THE TABLE

U.S. consumer prices rose 3.7% in September from a year earlier, up from 3.4% in August and a bit hotter than the 3.6% economists expected, driven by gasoline and diesel costs tied to the Iran war, NBC News and CNBC report. Core inflation, which leaves out food and energy, was 2.6%, also above forecasts, and Reuters says the details show price pressure spreading. After the report, futures traders put the odds of another quarter-point Fed hike at the Oct. 27–28 meeting at about 54%, up from 22% a day earlier, CNBC says, though other measures put it closer to even and some economists think the Fed will hold so close to the Nov. 3 midterms. The Fed already raised rates in September, to 3.75%–4%.

Sources (4): NBC News · CNBC: Fed odds · Reuters via Investing.com · The Free Press, The Front Page (newsletter)
Why it matters: Higher U.S. rates tend to push up Canadian borrowing costs and fuel prices, which hits mortgages, truck payments and job-site budgets here.
 
TRUMP TOUTS 'MASSIVE' GREENLAND DRILLING; DENMARK AND GREENLAND SAY NOT SO FAST

President Trump said 'massive' oil, gas and mineral exploration is about to begin in Greenland, AP reports, linking it to last month's U.S.-Denmark-Greenland security deal. Denmark and Greenland pushed back: they say the Sept. 22 agreement expands U.S. military access but gives the U.S. no special rights to drill or mine, and any U.S. projects must follow Greenland's laws, Al Jazeera and ArcticToday report. Greenland's government told RNZ it is far from 'massive' exploration. 'Greenland' is a top trend on X in Canada and Vancouver this morning.

Sources (4): AP · Al Jazeera · ArcticToday · RNZ
Why it matters: Arctic resources and defence are Canada's neighbourhood too, and new drilling claims can sway energy markets and the loonie.

UPDATE: MUSK SAYS WASHINGTON, NOT SPACEX, SETS UKRAINE'S STARSHIELD LIMITS

'Starshield systems are owned and controlled by the US government. Any limits on speed or usage are decided by the US military and ultimately POTUS,' Elon Musk posted Wednesday, adding that commercial Starlink is limited to civilian or defensive use. He was replying to Ukraine's defence minister Mykhailo Fedorov, who posted that 'Starlink connectivity at speeds of up to 160 km/h is being restored' and said removing Starshield's speed limit entirely is now 'critical.' The Guardian reports the cut-off, which Kyiv says came without explanation, knocked out most of Ukraine's mid-range strike drones and hampered the interceptor drones that chase Russian Shaheds, and President Zelensky told Piers Morgan he feels 'betrayed' by Trump. Late Wednesday Musk also posted 'Time for Zelensky to go.' In a YouTube update, Ukrainian ex-pilot Denys Davydov (a pro-Ukraine voice) says the restored 160 km/h limit lets slower strike drones fly again but still leaves the faster interceptors cut off.

Sources (6): @elonmusk on X: Starshield · @FedorovMykhailo on X · @elonmusk on X: Zelensky · Guardian explainer · Guardian: 'betrayed' · Denys Davydov (YouTube)
Why it matters: Starlink users in Canada aren't affected, but the fight shows that who controls a satellite network can decide what it does in a war.
 
TRUMP'S 'ENERGY CEASEFIRE' FALLS APART AS BOTH SIDES HIT POWER AND OIL

The White House's push for an energy ceasefire has collapsed, the Guardian reports: Russia sent more than 360 drones at the Kirovohrad region overnight, killing three, and Moscow says Ukraine hit a refinery in Russia's Ryazan region with 69 drones and four Flamingo cruise missiles. Zelensky told the Financial Times the pause on refinery strikes is 'out. We're done,' after Russia's barrages this week, and said more than 850 Shahed drones were launched while Ukraine's defences were 'artificially restricted.' Trump has demanded Ukraine stop hitting refineries, blaming the strikes for high diesel prices. SitDeck's brief notes Kyiv's charge that Washington held back missile warnings is an allegation, not an established fact.

Sources (3): Guardian · Guardian Thursday briefing · SitDeck Daily Brief (newsletter)
Why it matters: Refinery strikes and diesel deals feed straight into diesel prices for trucks, generators and heavy equipment.
 
A CONTAINER SHIP SINKS IN THE STRAIT OF HORMUZ

The Singapore-flagged container ship GFS Pride sank after being hit by an unknown projectile in the Strait of Hormuz on Tuesday; all 21 crew got off safely, Singapore's maritime authority says, BBC reports. A sister ship, GFS Giselle, was hit Wednesday and reached anchorage with hull damage. Iran's Revolutionary Guards say a ship hit mines on an 'unauthorised' route, but SitDeck calls the mine explanation uncorroborated. The UN shipping agency counts 106 confirmed incidents and at least 24 seafarers killed since the U.S.-Israel war with Iran began in February.

Sources (2): BBC · SitDeck Daily Brief (newsletter)
Why it matters: About a fifth of the world's oil and gas normally moves through Hormuz, so every sinking props up pump prices here.

THREE 'GHOSTLY' GALAXIES WITH ALMOST NO DARK MATTER MAY PROVE IT'S REAL

Three faint galaxies about 65 million light-years away, DF2, DF4 and the newly confirmed DF9, have far less dark matter than theory says they should, Quanta Magazine reports; DF2 has at most 1/400th of what's expected. A June study in The Astrophysical Journal says they may be debris from an ancient galaxy crash in which the gas piled up while the dark matter sailed straight through. That's a problem for 'modified gravity' theories that say dark matter doesn't exist: 'What this is showing is that dark matter is … not just a modification of gravity,' study author Michael Keim of Yale told Quanta. 'The absence of dark matter is, in a way, some evidence of dark matter, which is ironic,' physicist Shyam Balaji of King's College London told Quanta. Researchers still don't know what dark matter actually is.

Sources (1): Quanta Magazine
Why it matters: For physics and cosmology fans, this is one of the cleaner tests yet of dark matter against its rivals.
 
ONE SOURCE
MICROSOFT'S AI FIX AIMS AT TWO- TO SIX-WEEK FORECASTS

Microsoft researchers say a machine-learning tool called Probabilistic Bias Correction makes forecasts two to six weeks out more accurate by fixing the built-in errors of existing weather models instead of replacing them, The Verge reports on a study published in Nature. One example is correcting models that run too cold because they lean on older climate records. The tool runs on ordinary computer chips rather than supercomputers or GPUs, and it's free, open-source software. Forecasts usually lose their reliability beyond about two weeks.

Sources (1): The Verge
Why it matters: Better month-ahead forecasts help schedule outdoor pours, roofing and other weather-sensitive work.
NASA has stacked both boosters and all four engines for the Artemis III moon rocket ahead of a pad fuelling test, NASASpaceflight reports, and NASA will brief on speeding up its moon plans on Oct. 23. (NASASpaceflight · NASA)

EIGHT STATES SUE OPENAI, SAYING CHATGPT ISN'T SAFE FOR KIDS

Iowa, Kansas, Nebraska, West Virginia, Arkansas, Arizona, Idaho and Montana have each sued OpenAI under consumer-protection laws, saying it sold ChatGPT as 'safe, youth-appropriate' while it was 'dangerous to vulnerable users,' The Verge reports, following Reuters. The suits point to deaths that families blame on the chatbot, to the collection of minors' data, and to recent rogue-AI hacking incidents. OpenAI says it has 'built industry-leading age-specific protections' such as parental controls and wants AI rules that apply to the whole industry. Separately, New York's attorney general is pushing for protections for users' data as Character.AI goes through bankruptcy, The Verge reports.

Sources (2): The Verge: state suits · The Verge: Character.AI
Why it matters: Surrey families whose kids use chatbots for homework should check the parental controls and data settings.
 
THE AI RACE GETS CROWDED: META AND GOOGLE CATCH UP

Within days, Google released Gemini 4 Argon and Meta unveiled Muse Wonder Full, which Meta's own tests say can match OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra and Anthropic's Claude models, The Verge reports, calling it a sign the race is 'anyone's game.' Those are company benchmarks, not independent tests. Anthropic, meanwhile, told investors its third-quarter revenue rose 46% to US$16.8 billion, the Financial Times reported, and The Information says slowing growth could weigh on its IPO price. OpenAI also accuses a former employee of taking more than 100 files of product plans to Apple, The Verge reports.

Sources (3): The Verge · The Verge: OpenAI and Apple · The Information, The Briefing (newsletter)
Why it matters: More real competition usually means cheaper, better AI tools for small businesses and estimators.

U.S. TRIMS SOFTWOOD DUTIES TO ABOUT 35%, BUT MILLS STILL HURT

Canadian softwood lumber now faces combined U.S. duties and tariffs of almost 35%, down from 45% set in August last year, after the U.S. Commerce Department's latest review, the B.C. Lumber Trade Council says, CBC and CTV report. That's a 14.11% countervailing duty, a 10.64% anti-dumping duty and Trump's extra 10% national-security tariff. Interim council president Kim Haakstad says producers are still 'disappointed' by 'unjustified and punitive' measures in a dispute dragging on for nearly a decade.

Sources (2): CBC (Canadian Press) · CTV News
Why it matters: Lumber duties ripple into B.C. mill jobs and framing-package prices on local builds.
 
B.C. DRUG DEATHS HIT THEIR LOWEST SUMMER LEVEL SINCE 2019

The BC Coroners Service says 113 people died from toxic drugs in July and 126 in August, the lowest for those months since 2019 and down 50% and 40% from their peaks, the Canadian Press reports. Deaths from January to August reached 1,015, down 18% from last year and 41% from 2023's record. Fraser Health and Vancouver Coastal Health had the most deaths, 68 each over the two months. The drop mirrors a decline across much of North America.

Sources (2): CityNews (Canadian Press) · BC Gov News
Why it matters: Fraser Health, which covers Surrey, still ties for the most deaths, so the crisis isn't over here.

DEBATE NIGHT: EBY ADMITS FLAWS, DOERKSON KEEPS HIS CAMPAIGN DIRECTOR

In the campaign's only TV debate, NDP Leader David Eby repeatedly admitted his government's 'imperfections' and said he'd never balance the budget 'on the backs of British Columbians' as the deficit sits at a record $14 billion, CTV and the Canadian Press report. Conservative Leader Lorne Doerkson said 'nothing is better than it was years ago' after a decade of NDP rule and stood by campaign director Katy Merrifield over a deleted anti-Islam post, calling her a sexual-violence survivor who 'acted out in a moment of trauma'; Eby said her feed 'reads like the worst corners of the dark web.' The two sparred over no-fault ICBC insurance, with Eby warning that lawyers' costs would push rates up without it and Doerkson saying the system has hurt injured people, CBC reports, while Green Leader Emily Lowan called the race 'déjà vu of 2024.' Afterward, the NDP said Eby had 'the only real plan,' while the Conservatives said Eby 'can't defend his record.' The most recent pre-debate poll, Pallas Data (Oct. 10–11, 513 voters by automated phone), has the Conservatives at 43% to the NDP's 38%, with Doerkson ahead on best premier, 35% to 29%.

Sources (7): CTV Vancouver · CBC · CityNews (CP key quotes) · Surrey Now-Leader · @bcndp on X · @Conservative_BC on X · Pallas Data
Why it matters: ICBC rates, the deficit and DRIPA's effect on project approvals are the big pocketbook and job-site splits between the two main parties.
 
THE MONEY: NDP COSTS ITS PLATFORM; TORIES PROMISE THEIRS BEFORE VOTING DAY

Hours before the debate, the NDP released costings that cut the deficit to about $12 billion by 2028–29, paid for partly by $1 billion a year from higher income tax on the top 3.88% of earners and $730 million from 'targeted economic growth,' Global News reports. The Now-Leader says the plan adds $2.8 billion in capital spending over five years, mostly health care and transit, plus small funds for youth hiring, manufacturing, forestry jobs and tariff relief, and leans on the speculation tax and a proposed unsold-condo tax. The Greens' plan rests on a one-time wealth tax they say would raise $22.2 billion. The Conservatives are the only big party without a costed platform; Doerkson says voters will see a 'fulsome plan' before they vote, and he also promised a 'Respect Local Democracy Act' banning provincial elections within 60 days of municipal ones.

Sources (4): Global News · Surrey Now-Leader: costing · Surrey Now-Leader: election dates · CTV Vancouver
Why it matters: The tax and capital-spending choices decide how much public construction work there is in the next few years.
 
SURREY: LAST ADVANCE DAY, LOCKE AHEAD IN A NEW POLL, AND A WATER-PRESSURE SCARE

Surrey's final advance poll is today, 8 AM to 8 PM at 10 sites including Cloverdale Recreation Centre, and general voting is Saturday, the city says. A new Canada Pulse Insights online poll of 483 residents (Sept. 28–Oct. 9) has Mayor Brenda Locke at 33% of decided voters, with Doug McCallum and Linda Annis at 16%, Troy Van-Vliet and Mike Starchuk at 12%, and Honveer Singh Randhawa at 9%, CityNews and Vancouver Television report; an earlier Leger poll had Locke at 24% with 34% undecided, so the size of her lead depends on the poll. Starchuk, a retired firefighter, called it a public-safety issue; Locke didn't respond to BIV.

Sources (5): City of Surrey · CityNews · Vancouver Television · Surrey Citizen (Leger) · Business in Vancouver
Why it matters: Water rules, fire safety and regional fees all land on Surrey property tax bills, so this is a fair question for every mayoral candidate.
