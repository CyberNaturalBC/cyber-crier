🔭 Science & Research
A 'TREBLE CLEF' IN SPACE: A BLACK HOLE BLAST, THEN A CLUSTER CRASH

Astronomers using NASA's Chandra X-ray Observatory say a strange radio source shaped like a treble clef sits in two galaxy clusters that are merging, NASA reports. The team, led by Andrea Botteon of Italy's INAF, says a supermassive black hole first blasted out charged particles and magnetic fields; that radio glow faded, then the cluster collision squeezed and re-lit it, possibly with shock waves. The object hides behind the Milky Way's disc and was once thought to be a pulsar, and its radio spectrum is one of the steepest ever seen in a cluster, the paper says. Note the hedging: headlines say the origin was found, but NASA itself calls this the 'likely' story and the authors only 'speculate' about what shaped it, so it's a strong explanation, not a settled one.

Sources (3): NASA · Chandra photo release · Botteon et al. (arXiv)
Why it matters: Black holes that shape whole galaxy clusters are a big piece of how the universe's largest structures grew.
 
NASA WATCHES A 'HISTORIC' EL NIÑO; A MILDER B.C. WINTER LOOKS LIKELY

NASA says its satellites are tracking a historic El Niño this fall. U.S. forecasters' outlook gives the Pacific Northwest a 60 to 70% chance of a warmer-than-normal December to February, MyNorthwest reports. El Niño winters often push storms south and leave less mountain snow here, but the USGS notes other ocean patterns add a lot of uncertainty.

Sources (3): NASA · MyNorthwest · USGS
Why it matters: A mild, low-snow winter can mean lower heating bills now but tighter water and wildfire risk next summer.

TSB: WIND AND A RUSHED RISK CHECK LIKELY DOOMED A 2022 B.C. SEAPLANE

The Transportation Safety Board's final report says wind shear, low-level turbulence and a possibly incomplete pre-flight risk assessment likely led a pilot to lose control of a seaplane leaving a logging camp at Strachan Bay on Nov. 23, 2022, killing all three aboard, CTV and CBC report. The plane, bound for Port Hardy, climbed only about 30 metres before it pitched down into the water. The TSB also flagged that several modifications for water and short-field flying may have changed how the plane handled, without clear guidance on their combined effect.

Sources (3): CTV News (Canadian Press) · CBC · Vancouver Is Awesome
Why it matters: Remote B.C. camp crews still fly in on small floatplanes, and this is a reminder that weather checks aren't paperwork.

SURREY VOTES TOMORROW: WHERE THE MAYORAL HOPEFULS STAND

Surrey picks a mayor, eight councillors and six school trustees tomorrow, 8 AM to 8 PM, and you can vote at any of the city's 52 locations,, the city says. There are seven mayoral candidates: Igal Ammons II, Linda Annis, Brenda Locke, Doug McCallum, Honveer Singh Randhawa, Mike Starchuk and Troy Van-Vliet. On taxes, Locke (Surrey Connect) promises increases below inflation, Annis (Surrey First) promises no increase in year one plus an auditor general and says the average household's taxes rose about $1,500 in four years, and Starchuk (Imagine Surrey) would cap increases at inflation, CTV reports. On crime, Annis wants Surrey Police to get real-time access to the city's 640 traffic cameras amid more than 145 extortion cases this year, while Locke points to a planned public-safety campus in Port Kells; Starchuk also pitches bulk-buying transit passes he says would save riders about $560 a year. McCallum has pitched a children's hospital on city land in Newton, an idea the provincial Conservatives have now echoed, the Now-Leader reports.

Sources (4): City of Surrey: where to vote · City of Surrey: mayoral candidates · CTV News · Surrey Now-Leader
Why it matters: Council sets Surrey's property tax rate and policing budget for the next four years, so tomorrow's vote lands on every tax bill.
 
ADVANCE POLLS OPEN AS THE MASSEY FIGHT AND SURREY PROMISES HEAT UP

B.C. advance voting runs today through Wed Oct. 21, 8 AM to 8 PM, at any advance location in the province; bring ID that proves who you are and where you live, Elections BC says. The Conservatives promise to scrap the NDP's $8.5-billion, eight-lane Massey tunnel for a 10-lane bridge, without giving a cost, CBC reports; Eby calls it 'lunacy,' saying 200 workers are already on site and $3 billion in federal financing would be lost, and the operating engineers' union IUOE Local 115 calls scrapping it 'completely irresponsible.' Conservatives call the tunnel a boondoggle whose cost has more than doubled from a first estimate of $4.15 billion, CBC says, and Global News notes it isn't due to finish until 2031. In Surrey, Doerkson promised to finish the Cloverdale hospital and build a women and children's hospital, while Eby promised free bus passes for low-income seniors, $250 million toward Surrey–White Rock bus rapid transit, finishing the Surrey–Langley SkyTrain, and a Surrey Charter giving the city more say over its own affairs.

Sources (7): Surrey Now-Leader: advance voting · Elections BC · CBC: Massey bridge · Global News · @RattanNews on X (IUOE Local 115, via Indo-Canadian Voice) · Vancouver Is Awesome (Canadian Press) · @Dave_Eby on X: Surrey Charter
Why it matters: Tunnel versus bridge decides years of heavy-civil work on the Fraser and how long the Hwy 99 crawl lasts for Surrey commuters.
 
LEAKED CROWN-LAND PLAN, ANOTHER DROPPED CANDIDATE, AND A TIGHT RACE

Leaked Sept. 10 briefing notes, first published by Northern Beat, show the NDP government planned legislation to transfer about 400 parcels of Crown land to First Nations while limiting compensation for forest companies, with none at all if a transfer hits less than 5% of a licence's allowable cut, CTV and the Canadian Press report. Eby says the transfers reflect existing treaties and reconciliation commitments and the law is about saving taxpayers money; Kim Haakstad of the Council of Forest Industries says it's unfair and makes it harder for mills to operate. It isn't in the NDP platform. Meanwhile the Conservatives dropped Vancouver-Strathcona candidate Jordan McIntosh less than an hour after an NDP candidate posted screenshots of anonymous posts, linked to him, that minimize the Holocaust, CBC reports; the NDP says 15 other Tory candidates have made offensive remarks, while the Conservatives point to a regulator's fine against NDP candidate Linda Li. The latest polls, all taken before Wednesday's debate, show a close race: Leger has the Conservatives up 42–40, Ipsos 43–38 and Research Co. 44–42.

Sources (8): CTV News (Canadian Press) · Vancouver Sun · CBC: McIntosh · @bcndp on X · @Conservative_BC on X · Leger · Ipsos · Research Co.
Why it matters: Who controls Crown land and timber rights shapes mill jobs and resource-project approvals across B.C.
