ANTHROPIC STUDY: EXPERTS WENT WITH THEIR AI AGENT'S SECRET COIN-FLIP LEAN 86% OF THE TIME

Anthropic had 50 professional data analysts do a realistic analysis with its Claude Code agent.

Read more

What the paper actually says: analysts went with the agent's lean 86 per cent of the time, against 50 per cent by chance. 🔭 Science (papers read)

COMETS PROBABLY DIDN'T SEED LIFE ON EARTH, SAYS A NEW MODEL

A Cambridge-led team modelled whether comets could have delivered hydrogen cyanide, a key starting molecule for life's chemistry, to the early Earth intact and concentrated enough to matter (Science News; Phys.org).

Read more

What the paper actually says: most comets hit too fast and too steep for fragile organics to survive, and comet impacts fell off fast in Earth's first 500 million years. Successful delivery was "an exceedingly rare event," about one useful impact in over a billion years, needing a big, slow, glancing comet that lands on dry land. Mars comes out slightly more likely. It's peer reviewed (Scientific Reports) and open access through Cambridge's repository, but it's simulations, and it covers one molecule. A Spanish chemist told Science Media Centre Spain the press release overstates it: it doesn't prove comets contributed nothing.

Sources (3): Paper: Anslow et al., Scientific Reports (open copy) · Science News · Science Media Centre Spain (expert reaction)

Why it matters: Points the search for life's origins back at Earth itself, asteroids and volcanoes.

"JUMPING GENES" MAY HAVE HELPED ANTS TAKE OVER AFTER THE DINOSAURS

Ant lineages with the most transposable elements, DNA that copies itself around the genome, also have the most species today, an international team led by the University of Münster reports.

Read more

What the paper actually says: they analysed 163 ant genomes from 12 of 16 subfamilies and found separate bursts of these elements in the ancestors of the most species-rich groups, before they diversified after the asteroid 66 million years ago. Element content correlated with the number of genera and species per subfamily (adjusted R² 0.29 and 0.36), and was linked to expansions of smell-receptor genes. That's a correlation, not proof of cause. Peer reviewed in Science Advances (Sept. 2026), free to read.

Sources (2): Paper: Schrader et al., Science Advances · ScienceDaily

Why it matters: Genome glitches can be fuel for evolution, not just junk.

"LUMINA": THE BIGGEST SIMULATION YET OF THE UNIVERSE'S FIRST 2 BILLION YEARS

Center for Astrophysics | Harvard & Smithsonian researchers built Lumina, a simulation of gas, dark matter and light from the dark ages to about 2 billion years after the Big Bang, to compare against Webb telescope data (Smithsonian).

Read more

What the paper actually says: a box 500 megaparsecs (about 1.5 billion light-years) on a side, with 2 × 6,000³ elements, over 100 times the volume of comparable runs. It predicts hydrogen reionization finished late, around redshift 5.2, mostly driven by stars, while helium reionization was driven by quasars and nearly done by redshift 3. It matches Planck's measurements and broadly fits other data. Accepted in Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society; the arXiv version is free. It's a tool for testing theories, not a discovery.

Sources (2): Paper: Zier et al., arXiv:2605.15310 (accepted in MNRAS) · Smithsonian

Why it matters: When Webb sees galaxies that look "too old," this is what they get checked against.

A FIBRE SUPPLEMENT EASED KNEE ARTHRITIS PAIN IN A SMALL TRIAL

University of Nottingham researchers report that 20 grams a day of inulin, a prebiotic fibre from chicory root, cut knee osteoarthritis pain over six weeks, about as well as an online physio program (ScienceDaily; University of Nottingham).

Read more

What the paper actually says: a randomized, placebo-controlled trial of 117 adults (average age 67.5). Pain fell by about 1.1 points on a 10-point scale with inulin and 1.6 with physio, compared with placebo. Inulin also improved grip strength and pain sensitivity, and only 3.6 per cent dropped out, against 21 per cent for physio. Combining both didn't add up, and the study wasn't powered to test that. It's short and small. Peer reviewed in Nutrients, free to read, published earlier this year; the press push is new.

Sources (2): Paper: Nutrients (2026), INSPIRE trial · University of Nottingham

Why it matters: Cheap and low-risk for sore knees, but ask your doctor and keep doing the exercises.
