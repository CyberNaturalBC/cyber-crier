🚨 BRAZIL RUNOFF: FLÁVIO BOLSONARO LEADS LULA 47–45 — OCT 25 REMATCH

With 100% of ballots counted, Brazil’s Superior Electoral Court has Flávio Bolsonaro (Liberal Party) at 47.03% of valid votes and incumbent Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva (Workers’ Party) at 45.16% in Sunday’s first round — neither cleared 50%, so they meet in a runoff on Oct 25. Agência Brasil and O Globo match those TSE figures; the BBC and El País frame it as a right-wing upset after Lula had led most late polls.

Bolsonaro said he was “very happy” and confident heading into three weeks of runoff campaigning. Lula called the result “unexpected,” admitted he expected a first-round win, and said the “real” race starts now. Smaller right-of-centre candidates’ votes (~7% combined in early counts) are widely expected to lean Bolsonaro’s way — treat that as analysis, not a locked outcome.

Sources (4): Agência Brasil · BBC · El País · SitDeck brief (newsletter)
Why it matters: Latin America’s biggest economy choosing between Lula and Bolsonaroism again — commodity and FX ripples matter for Canadian exporters watching EM risk.

U.S. PULLS B-1 BOMBERS FROM RAF FAIRFORD AFTER IRAN PLOT WARNINGS

The Pentagon confirmed all U.S. bombers that had been at RAF Fairford in England — about a dozen B-1Bs used in strikes related to the Iran campaign — have redeployed to their U.S. home stations. Reuters, Stars and Stripes and Defense News all cite the Sunday pullout, one week after British arrests near the base on suspicion of preparing terrorist acts.

U.K. Prime Minister Andy Burnham has said there are “strong indications” of Iranian involvement; Tehran denies it and summoned Britain’s ambassador. The Free Press Front Page (opinion-adjacent news roundup) and SitDeck both flagged the hasty exit; treat force-protection moves as confirmed by U.S. spokespeople, and the Iran-link attribution as contested.

Sources (4): Stars and Stripes · Defense News / Reuters · The Free Press Front Page (newsletter) · SitDeck brief (newsletter)
Why it matters: Another signal that Iran-related risk is shaping where U.S. heavy bombers sit in Europe — watch Hormuz and fuel prices if the ladder climbs.

🚨 YEMEN: GOVERNMENT LAUNCHES ‘YEMEN DAWN’ TO RETAKE HOUTHI TERRITORY

Yemen’s Saudi-backed president Rashad al-Alimi announced “Operation Yemen Dawn” — a nationwide push to retake Houthi-held territory and restore state authority — in a televised address Sunday. The BBC and Al Monitor report the order followed weeks of Houthi advances, including toward Bab al-Mandeb and the Taiz–Aden corridor. On Monday, government forces claimed gains around Bab al-Mandeb while the Saudi-led coalition said 100 jets were flying support and claimed hundreds of Houthi targets hit (The National).

The Houthis say they struck Aramco-linked sites in Riyadh and Khurais over the weekend; the Saudi coalition has disputed parts of those claims in recent days. SitDeck’s overnight brief puts Iran–Yemen at the top of its flashpoint list — use it as a lead sheet, not a sole source for damage figures.

Sources (4): BBC · Al Monitor / Reuters · The National · SitDeck brief (newsletter)

🔭 Science & Research
NOBEL MEDICINE: OPTOGENETICS — LIGHT SWITCHES FOR BRAIN CELLS

The 2026 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine goes jointly to Karl Deisseroth (Stanford / HHMI), Peter Hegemann (Humboldt University of Berlin) and Georg Nagel (University of Würzburg) “for their discoveries concerning light-gated ion channels and optogenetics,” the Nobel Assembly announced Monday.

Hegemann and Nagel found channelrhodopsin in algae — a protein that opens an ion channel under blue light. Deisseroth turned that into a light-controlled switch for neurons in living animals (2005–2007 papers). The committee says labs worldwide now use the method to map circuits behind memory, emotion and behaviour, with clinical trials aiming to restore sight. Prize sum: 12 million Swedish kronor, shared.

Sources (3): Nobel Prize press release · Reuters · Science Magazine
Why it matters: The tool that lets researchers actually turn brain circuits on and off with light just got the biggest prize in science — practical for anyone watching neurology and psych research.

EBY PITCHES MILLIONAIRE TAX FOR HEALTH CARE; CONSERVATIVES SAY ‘DOCTOR TAX’

Campaigning on Vancouver Island Sunday, NDP Leader David Eby said a re-elected NDP would create a new provincial income-tax bracket on earnings over $1 million (24.5% on that slice) and raise the top two existing brackets, projecting about $1 billion a year at maturity for health care and affordability measures, CBC, Vancouver Sun and Daily Hive report. Party docs say changes would start in the 2027 tax year and hit under 4% of earners.

B.C. Conservatives fired back that the plan would tax doctors and specialists communities are trying to recruit, calling it a “doctor tax,” and restated leader Lorne Doerkson’s “no new taxes” pledge. Weigh both: the NDP frames it as asking high earners to fund services; Conservatives frame it as driving professionals out. Latest province-wide Ipsos (Sept 25–29) still has Conservatives 42% / NDP 40% among decided voters — a statistical tie.

Sources (4): CBC BC · Vancouver Sun · Daily Hive · Ipsos
Why it matters: Health-care access and tax load are kitchen-table issues for B.C. families — know both parties’ lines before Oct 24.
