🔭 Science & Research

'HIDDEN PLANET' IN WEBB IMAGES TURNS OUT TO BE A KNOWN ONE, BUT THE NEW TRICK IS REAL

Northwestern-led astronomers say a physics-based computer model of Webb's NIRCam camera removes starlight 2 to 15 times better than standard methods, and ScienceDaily headlines it as a "hidden alien planet" discovery.

Read more

What the paper actually says: the Nature paper (Ferrer-Chavez, Wang and colleagues, peer reviewed) reports the first detection of 51 Eridani b at 4.3 microns. That planet was already known; what's new is spotting it at that wavelength in existing Webb data. The method fits millions of model parameters to correct for telescope wobbles that limit standard techniques, and the team says it could ease stability demands on the Roman and Habitable Worlds telescopes.

Sources (2): Nature paper · ScienceDaily

Why it matters: Better software squeezing more out of telescopes we already paid for is the real story, not a new world.

RADIO TELESCOPE MAKES THE FIRST DIRECT MEASUREMENT OF COSMIC HYDROGEN'S LARGE-SCALE PATTERN

The MeerKLASS team using South Africa's MeerKAT array reports measuring how neutral hydrogen clumps across about 236 square degrees of sky, from light emitted roughly 4.5 billion years ago, Universe Today reports.

Read more

What the paper actually says: the MNRAS paper (peer reviewed) detects the hydrogen signal's power spectrum at a signal-to-noise of 6.2 at redshift 0.42 by cross-correlating independent subsets of 2021 data. The authors say individual subsets still show excess power from unresolved systematics and the abundance-bias number is loose (1.27, range about 0.67 to 2.35, times 10⁻³). They frame it as proof the technique works for the coming SKA Observatory, not a new picture of the universe yet.

Sources (2): MNRAS paper · Universe Today

Why it matters: A first working measurement that opens the door for the much bigger SKA maps.

COMETS PROBABLY DIDN'T SEED LIFE ON EARTH, CAMBRIDGE MODEL SAYS

A University of Cambridge team led by Richard Anslow says comets usually hit Earth too fast and at the wrong angles for fragile organic molecules to survive, calling the comet-delivery idea "highly unlikely." What the paper actually says: the Scientific Reports paper (peer reviewed, Oct.

Read more

28) combines impact simulations, comet-atmosphere modelling and the history of short-period comets, and concludes that successful delivery of prebiotic molecules in useful concentrations was "an exceedingly rare event." It's a modelling result, not a measurement, so it narrows the odds rather than closing the case.

Sources (2): Scientific Reports paper · ScienceDaily

Why it matters: It points the origin-of-life search back toward chemistry on Earth itself.

DIFFERENT MOSQUITOES PICK DIFFERENT PEOPLE, BASED ON SKIN ODOUR

Florida International University researchers found no single "mosquito magnet": among 119 volunteers, three dangerous species each favoured a different set of people, ScienceDaily reports.

Read more

What the paper actually says: the iScience paper (peer reviewed; published July 31, so not new) tested Aedes aegypti, Aedes albopictus and Culex quinquefasciatus in an olfactometer. Ae. aegypti was slightly more drawn to men; its attraction, like Culex's, was linked to the absence of odours such as cyclic alcohols and monoterpenes, while Ae. albopictus attraction was linked to ketones. These are associations, not proven causes.

Sources (2): iScience paper · ScienceDaily

Why it matters: Could lead to better repellents for travellers; for now, regular repellent still works.
