🌎 World & Politics
FOOD BANK VISITS HIT A RECORD 2.3 MILLION A MONTH

Food Banks Canada's HungerCount report, out today, counted more than 2,355,000 food bank visits across Canada in March 2026, up 8.7% from last year's record, The Canadian Press reports via CityNews. More than a third of visits, nearly 776,000, were by children, and one in five food banks ran out of food before meeting demand. The report says food prices are up about 30% since 2021, housing 28% and transportation about 24%. CEO Kirstin Beardsley says 'a job is not enough' for many families now, and calls Ottawa's new groceries benefit positive but not enough.

Sources (1): The Canadian Press via CityNews
Why it matters: Working families, not just the jobless, are now in the food bank line, and grocery costs are a live issue in Saturday's vote.
 
OTTAWA SETS NOV. 5 BUDGET; A U.S. IMPORTER SUES OVER TRUMP'S 50% TARIFFS ON CANADA

Finance Minister François-Philippe Champagne says the Carney government will table its 2026 budget on Nov. 5, its second, as tariffs squeeze the economy after U.S. trade talks collapsed in August, The Canadian Press reports. The budget officer expects deficits about $3 billion a year higher than projected in June, though war-driven gas prices are lifting revenue this year. Separately, the Liberty Justice Center sued in the U.S. Court of International Trade on behalf of food importer Gellert Global Group, arguing Trump's use of a 1930 law (Section 338) to put 50% tariffs on Canadian goods exceeds his powers. The White House says the duties answer provincial U.S. liquor bans, dairy supply management and vehicle quotas; the suit says two of those were themselves responses to Trump's tariffs.

Sources (2): The Canadian Press via CityNews: budget · The Canadian Press via CityNews: lawsuit
Why it matters: The tariff fight decides a lot of B.C. jobs and prices; watch Nov. 5 for infrastructure money that turns into construction work.

🔭 Science & Research
THE BIG BANG'S HELIUM, MEASURED TO HALF A PERCENT

Astronomers have measured how much helium the universe made in its first five minutes to an uncertainty of 0.5%, about three times sharper than before, using 130 hours on the Large Binocular Telescope, ScienceDaily reports from a University of Minnesota release. Instead of extrapolating from many galaxies, the team used 15 of the most chemically 'pristine' small galaxies known. The peer-reviewed paper in The Astrophysical Journal gives a helium mass fraction of 0.2458 ± 0.0013, which agrees with the Big Bang prediction from Planck data (0.2467). Co-author Evan Skillman calls it one of the biggest findings of his 40-year career. Note the paper came out Sept. 9; it's getting fresh coverage this week.

Sources (3): ScienceDaily · The Astrophysical Journal (paper) · University of Minnesota
Why it matters: It's a hard test of the standard Big Bang story, and so far the story passes: no sign of extra unknown particles.

SPACEX BLOWS PAST FORECASTS, AND MUSK CONFIRMS TESLA MERGER TALKS ARE FORMAL

SpaceX posted Q3 revenue of $14.6 billion, up 161% from a year ago, and net income of $4.4 billion, its first profit as a public company, @SpaceX posted and Reuters reports; analysts expected about $12.7 billion. Its AI segment (renting data-centre compute to Anthropic, Google and others) nearly tripled from Q2 to $7.56 billion, Starlink brought in $5.2 billion with 13.6 million subscribers, and the first paying Starship flight carried 26 Starlink V3 satellites. On the call, Musk said both companies formed special committees of independent directors to study a merger at shareholders' request: 'Do I think it would be a good idea? Yes,' he said, but he's recused and Tesla would need a majority of all shares, 'quite a high hurdle,' CNBC reports. Shares jumped about 8% after hours. Electrek, which is openly critical of Musk, says a merger would dilute every shareholder; capital spending nearly tripled to $19.6 billion and SpaceX guided $35 to $40 billion for Q4.

Sources (6): @SpaceX on X: Q3 highlights · Reuters via Investing.com · CNBC live · BBC · Electrek (opinion-heavy) · The Information: The Briefing (newsletter)
Why it matters: A merger would change what owning Tesla stock means; for Starlink users, more capacity in orbit should mean steadier speeds.

SKYTRAIN TO NEWTON: BOTH BIG PARTIES PROMISE IT, MINUTES APART

The B.C. Conservatives and NDP issued releases within minutes of each other Tuesday promising a SkyTrain extension along King George Boulevard to Surrey's Newton, The Canadian Press reports via CBC. Eby says the NDP is moving $250 million in its costed platform, first set aside for bus rapid transit to White Rock, and will partner with mayor-elect Doug McCallum as on the Langley line, noting the precast concrete factory is already running. The Conservatives promise to work with Ottawa to 'fund and build' it and say Newton was 'left at the back of the line' after light rail was scrapped in 2018. Critics on each side poke holes: Conservative-leaning commentator @alex_shiff says Eby only 'offered a meeting', while Eby says Surrey promises 'need to be backed up with real funding.' Neither has given a full cost or timeline.

Sources (5): The Canadian Press via CBC · @bcndp on X · @alex_shiff on X (opinion) · Global News · Surrey Now-Leader
Why it matters: A real Newton line means years of station and guideway work in Surrey, but $250 million is a down payment, not a full SkyTrain.
