UPDATE: FOUR SOLDIERS KILLED AT CFB WAINWRIGHT, TWO FROM B.C.

The Canadian Armed Forces has confirmed four members died in Saturday's fire at CFB Wainwright: Sgt. Morgan Wong of Edmonton, Pte. Shaun Farrell of Prince Albert, Sask., Pte. Aidan Goulding of North Vancouver and Pte. Hayden Lykkemark of Langley, Global News and National Defence report. Thirty-nine members were hurt; Premier Danielle Smith says 19 were in critical condition Sunday. The soldiers were on a live-fire exercise, training ahead of a deployment to Latvia.

Maj.-Gen. Wade Rutland says the fire had been known about for hours and was a kilometre away when the wind suddenly shifted: 'It is not normal for a fire to move a kilometre in 30 to 60 seconds.' Global News cites a CAF memo saying the fire risk was rated low before the wind shift. A military board of inquiry is expected and will take more than a week (the Globe says at least a month), and there's no sign of terrorism. Premier Eby posted condolences naming the two B.C. soldiers, and Prime Minister Carney ordered flags to half-mast.

Sources (6): National Defence · Global News · CTV/CP24 live · BBC · The Globe and Mail · @Dave_Eby on X
Why it matters: Two of the four were Lower Mainland kids; the toll is lower than first feared, but 19 are still critical.
 
ALBERTA VOTES TODAY: STAY IN CANADA, OR START DOWN THE ROAD TO SEPARATION

Albertans answer 10 referendum questions today, and Question 10 asks whether the province should stay in Canada or start the legal process toward a second, binding vote on separating, CBC reports. Polls close at 8 PM MT (7 PM our time), and Question 10 is counted first. About 1.3 million people have already voted by special ballot or at advance polls; the other nine questions cover immigration and provincial powers.

The last polls lean heavily toward staying: Leger (Oct. 14–16) has 74% for remaining and 21% for starting the separation process, and Abacus (Oct. 7–13) has 68% to 24%. Abacus also found 61% expect Alberta to vote on separation again someday. Premier Smith and Prime Minister Carney both campaigned for staying, while separatists say the campaign has already shifted the conversation, X posts summarized by X say.

Sources (5): CBC: referendum day · Leger poll (PDF) · CBC: Abacus poll · Elections Alberta · X news summary: Alberta vote (Grok on X)
Why it matters: A big 'stay' vote calms things for pipelines and trade; a close one would hang over Canada's economy for years.

RUSSIAN MISSILES BLACK OUT 550,000 HOMES; ZELENSKYY SAYS THE U.S. GAVE NO WARNING

Russia fired 16 ballistic missiles at Kyiv and the region and more than 200 drones across Ukraine overnight, hitting more than 10 energy facilities and cutting power to about 550,000 households, Reuters and DW report. A woman was killed in Kyiv and two people in Kryvyi Rih; a medical centre and a metro station were hit. Ukraine's air force says it downed 243 drones and four cruise missiles. Russia's defence ministry says it hit defence-industry, drone and missile sites.

'Once again, there was no early warning from our partners,' Zelenskyy posted. Ukraine's air force says it got no warning before the 11 PM wave but did before a 3 AM strike. Marco Rubio said 'there's been no pause' in intelligence sharing and mentioned a recent 'software problem' with the warning system, The Guardian reports. Trump posted that he doesn't want to drive Russia 'away from the bargaining table' and that Zelenskyy 'doesn't want to end this war.'

Sources (5): DW (AFP/Reuters/AP) · Reuters via Devdiscourse · The Guardian · AP · SitDeck Daily Brief (newsletter)
Why it matters: With winter coming, both sides are going after energy, which keeps world fuel prices jumpy.

UPDATE: JAPAN'S PHOBOS ROUND TRIP LAUNCHES AT 12:41 PM OUR TIME TODAY

JAXA has set the launch of its Martian Moons eXploration (MMX) probe on H3 Flight 10 from Tanegashima for 4:41 AM Tuesday Japan time, which is 12:41 PM PT today; the launch window runs to Nov. 9 if weather or hardware push it back. COULD DARK ENERGY HAVE FLIPPED FROM NEGATIVE TO POSITIVE? A GRAVITY THEORY SAYS MAYBE

A team led by Özgür Akarsu at Istanbul Technical University found a forgotten branch of an alternative gravity theory (teleparallel f(T) gravity, which uses 'torsion' instead of Einstein's curvature) where dark energy's density starts out negative and turns positive about 9.5 billion years ago, according to a university press release on Phys.org. The idea fits hints from the DESI galaxy survey that dark energy changes over time, and the authors say it could ease the 'Hubble tension' over how fast the universe expands.

The release itself is careful: this is a theoretical paper (Physical Review D, 2025), DESI's hints aren't a confirmed discovery yet, and the model still hasn't been tested against DESI's full data. Treat it as an idea worth testing, not a result.

Sources (2): Phys.org (ITU press release) · EurekAlert (same release)
Why it matters: If dark energy really changes, the textbook picture of how the universe ends gets rewritten.

UPDATE: ANTHROPIC PLEDGES 5% OF ITS SHARES TO A PUBLIC FUND, AND ITS DRAFT AI BILL IS CONFIRMED

Anthropic says it will put at least 5% of its shares into a new Public Benefit Fund for causes like the job impact of AI, with up to 2% more at the board's discretion, and that none of it will go to lobbying or political donations, the BBC and The Information report. The BBC says Anthropic's Q3 revenue was US$16.8 billion, up 45%. Elon Musk replied that he'd rather give shares 'directly to the public' (see Tesla / SpaceX).

Saturday's X-only report is now confirmed: Politico, Reuters and Tom's Hardware say Anthropic has privately circulated a draft U.S. bill that would let Washington halt an AI model only for an imminent risk of more than US$1 billion in damage or more than 50 deaths, from bioweapons, loss of control or cyberattacks. The Commerce Department would register outside testers and would be the only agency able to seek a court order. Critics say the bar is too high; Anthropic calls it sensible.

Sources (5): BBC · Politico · Reuters via Investing.com · Tom's Hardware · The Information: The Briefing (newsletter)
Why it matters: AI companies are writing their own rules and handing out shares to win public trust; read both with eyes open.

UPDATE: CLOCKS STOP CHANGING NOV. 1, AND MORNINGS GET DARK TILL NEARLY 9

B.C. moves to permanent daylight time on Nov. 1, so we won't fall back. That leaves us on the same time as Alberta, two hours behind Ontario and one hour ahead of Washington state, CBC reports. Skies could stay dark until as late as 9 AM in Vancouver this winter. B.C.'s construction association is worried about later sunrises for crews who start before dawn. Federal offices are adjusting: CRA call-centre staff will work until 6 PM, and the border agency has shifted hours at nine land crossings. The Peace region and Creston aren't affected. YVR warns some destinations that used to share our time won't anymore.

FINALLY, THE PRICE TAGS: CONSERVATIVES SAY 'RESTRAINT, NOT AUSTERITY'

The B.C. Conservatives released their 71-page costed platform Sunday afternoon, after advance voting had started. It promises $2.6 billion in extra revenue over three years and a deficit shrinking to $10.8 billion by 2030, CBC and The Canadian Press report. The money would come from faster growth (2.5% in 2027, 2.8% in 2028, 3.2% in 2029, including 15% more gas production), a core review of ministries worth about $1.8 billion over four years, and a freeze on outside hiring plus attrition worth about $2 billion. The tax cuts, including raising the basic personal exemption from $13,126 to $32,000 and no PST on major projects until they start operating, add up to $11.3 billion by 2030.

Critics pounced on the growth numbers: CP notes the Bank of Canada sees just 1.4% national growth in 2027. Economist Rob Gillezeau called it 'one of the more reckless' fiscal plans, and the NDP says it's built on 'wildly unrealistic growth projections and deep healthcare cuts,' with a $6 billion gap. Conservative Leader Doerkson wasn't available for questions Sunday. The party calls its approach 'restraint, not austerity,' and also rolled out a kids' plan: no phones in class, no child hungry at school, and up to $500 per child for sports, music, arts and camps.

Sources (6): CBC · The Canadian Press via Yahoo · CTV News · @robgillezeau on X · @bcndp on X · @Conservative_BC on X: kids plan
Why it matters: Whoever wins, a $10–11 billion deficit means tight budgets for hospitals, schools and projects for years.

'YOU CAN'T BECOME PREMIER WITHOUT WINNING IN SURREY'

Ten of B.C.'s 93 ridings are in Surrey, The Tyee (left-leaning) reports: the Conservatives won six to the NDP's four in 2024, six were decided by fewer than 800 votes, and Surrey-Guildford went NDP by just 22. KPU political scientist Shinder Purewal thinks Surrey leans Conservative and points out it has half of B.C.'s school portables. 'You can't become premier in British Columbia without winning in Surrey,' says Conservative riding president Norman Stowe, while NDP supporters point to the insurance freeze and the gas cut. Surrey turnout trailed the provincial average in nine of 10 ridings in 2024.

Turnout so far: 337,961 ballots in the first two days of advance voting, about 9% of eligible voters, CTV reports from Elections BC figures. The latest polls haven't changed since Saturday's paper: Leger (Oct. 9–12) Conservatives 42%, NDP 40%; Ipsos (Oct. 7–11) 43 to 38; Mainstreet (Oct. 10–12) 47 to 36, by a poll tracker's count.

Sources (6): The Tyee (left-leaning) · CTV News: turnout · @ElectionsBC on X · Leger · BC Ballot polls · Northern Vibe poll list
Why it matters: A few hundred votes in Surrey ridings could pick the next government.
