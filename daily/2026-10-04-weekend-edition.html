🌎 World & Politics
BRAZIL VOTES TODAY — LULA AND FLÁVIO BOLSONARO HEAD THE FIRST ROUND

Brazilians cast ballots today (Sun Oct 4) in the first round of the 2026 presidential election. Polling and TSE coverage put incumbent Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva (PT) and Flávio Bolsonaro (PL) as the clear front-runners; an AtlasIntel poll cited by Poder360 had Lula near 47% of valid votes and Flávio near 44%, making an Oct 25 runoff the base-case outcome if neither clears 50%.

Official TSE results begin after polls close at 5 PM Brasília time. Treat early partial counts with caution until the electoral court posts certified tallies.

Sources (3): TSE (Brazil) · G1 / Globo · Poder360 / AtlasIntel
Why it matters: A Brazil runoff reshapes South American trade and commodity politics that eventually show up in Canadian fuel and softwood markets.

UPDATE: FIRST NATIONS KEEP PRESSING PACIFIC LINK CONSULTATION GAPS

Thursday’s national-interest listing of Pacific Link (Alberta-to-Roberts Bank oil pipeline) remains the Canadian story of the week. Global News and APTN report Indigenous leaders saying the designation-phase consultation window was only about two months — extended from one after protests — and that route, environmental and rights detail was thin; the Assembly of First Nations flagged “red flags” and Coldwater First Nation talked of court or other action.

Ottawa says it heard more than 100 Indigenous submissions and consulted more than 130 communities, with conditions (including at least 10% Indigenous ownership via loan guarantees) due by September 2027 and operations eyed for 2032–33. Athabasca Tribal Council chiefs, after meeting Carney, said they left encouraged on equity and river protections — a different tone from some B.C. Nations along the southern route.

Sources (4): Global News · APTN · PMO · Athabasca Tribal Council
Why it matters: Roberts Bank is next door in Delta — steel, terminal and tanker fights will keep showing up on Fraser Valley ballots and job boards.

UPDATE: MORE HORMUZ TANKERS HIT; HOUTHIS CLAIM ARAMCO STRIKE NEAR RIYADH

CNBC, citing UKMTO, says at least two vessels were struck over the weekend near Oman and in the Strait of Hormuz — including a Sunday report of a tanker hit by an unknown projectile that damaged its engine room (crew safe; no environmental impact reported). Iranian Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf said Sunday the strait will not reopen until Iran’s seven Islamabad-Memorandum conditions are met.

Separately, Al Jazeera and Reuters report Yemen’s Houthis claimed a ballistic-missile and drone strike on an Aramco facility south of Riyadh on Saturday, with flames and smoke seen near the site; Saudi-led coalition spokesman Turki al-Maliki called the Riyadh-targeting claim “misleading.” AFP photos showed fire at a facility south of the capital. Treat damage and casualty claims as contested until Riyadh or Aramco posts an official account. Brent closed Friday near US$102; G7 diesel/crude stock releases were cited as tempering Friday’s oil move.

Sources (4): CNBC · Al Jazeera · Al Jazeera (Saudi response) · ISW Iran Update Oct 3
Why it matters: Hormuz and a Saudi energy scare both price diesel and furnace oil at the Cloverdale pump first.

UPDATE: MERZ IN KYIV AS RUSSIA HITS NORTHERN BRIDGE AGAIN

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz made an unannounced Sunday visit to Kyiv amid air-raid sirens, meeting President Zelenskyy at the National Academy of Sciences building damaged in an earlier drone strike. Al Jazeera, The Guardian and Kyiv Independent report Russia struck Kyiv’s Northern Bridge over the Dnipro again Sunday morning — the fourth consecutive day of bridge attacks in the capital — injuring two people and damaging a heating main, Mayor Vitali Klitschko said.

Zelenskyy, in a Reuters interview cited widely over the weekend, said Ukraine will intensify strikes on Russian oil refineries as Moscow escalates winter infrastructure attacks; Russia’s Defence Ministry vowed to intensify strikes on Kyiv and other cities and warned foreign nationals to leave. Zelenskyy said Russia used more than 1,900 attack drones, 1,700 aerial bombs and 25 missiles against Ukraine this week alone.

Sources (4): Al Jazeera · Kyiv Independent · The Guardian · RFE/RL
Why it matters: Bridge and energy strikes keep European fuel nerves high — another input into global prices that reach B.C.

WHAT DOES THE FOURTH DIMENSION LOOK LIKE?

Quanta Magazine’s Joy of Why podcast features UT Austin topologist Maggie Miller on visualizing four-dimensional space. She explains why familiar knots always untie in 4D while spheres can become knotted in ways that won’t, how she pictures 4D as a reel of 3D snapshots, and how she and collaborators resolved a 1982 Charles Livingston question about Seifert surfaces for the unknot/unlink. Pure math — no new lab instrument — but a clear window into why dimension four breaks 3D intuition.

Sources (1): Quanta Magazine
Why it matters: Weekend science curiosity — topology is the quiet toolkit behind GPS, materials and data analysis.
 
DRC BUNDIBUGYO EBOLA OUTBREAK STILL LARGE — WHO FIGURES

WHO Disease Outbreak News on Bundibugyo-virus Ebola in the Democratic Republic of the Congo reported 7,890 confirmed cases and 3,799 deaths (case-fatality about 48%) as of 23 September 2026, spanning dozens of health zones with Ituri as a main epicentre. Later regional reporting (e.g. Afro.com citing Oct 1 tallies) has put the toll higher — around 8,300 cases / 4,000+ deaths — so treat the newest round numbers as moving.

SitDeck’s Sunday brief quoted the WHO-vintage 7,890 / 3,799 pair; those match the Sept 23 WHO DON, not a fresh weekend tally. No Canadian travel advisory change is claimed here — check PHAC before any Africa travel.

Sources (3): WHO DON · ECDC outbreak page · SitDeck brief (newsletter lead)
Why it matters: Large African outbreaks rarely hit Cloverdale directly, but they drive WHO alerts and travel rules for any family trips.
🤖 AI & Tech
OPENAI SAYS IT CUT OFF A MODEL-REASONING THEFT CAMPAIGN

OpenAI’s Sept 30 post says it disrupted a July 2026 “adversarial distillation” campaign that tried to extract hidden model reasoning by replaying encrypted chain-of-thought across accounts. Activity peaked around July 24–25 with about 16,000 extraction requests from more than 4,000 accounts before OpenAI banned accounts and closed the replay pathway. OpenAI links a core cluster to people associated with China-based Moonshot AI while saying it cannot prove every operator was one actor.

The Hacker News and Tom’s Hardware summarize the same figures. This is separate from last week’s safety-staff resignations covered in Saturday’s paper.

Sources (3): OpenAI · The Hacker News · Tom's Hardware
Why it matters: Lab security fights shape which AI tools (and chip stocks) stay available for shop-office and side-project work.
 
META OPEN-SOURCES MUSE GADGET FIRMWARE AND LINUX SDK

The Verge reports Meta released Muse Gadgets under Apache 2.0 — ESP32 firmware plus a Linux/Raspberry Pi SDK — so hobbyists can wire Muse to displays, buttons, sensors and actuators. Devices need an SDK token and Muse app Developer mode; Meta is also offering a limited free Muse Home Link adapter to some U.S. subscribers. Code lives in Meta’s facebookincubator muse-gadget-sdk repo.

Sources (2): The Verge · Muse Gadgets (Meta)
Why it matters: If you tinker with phone-sensor or home AI gadgets, open firmware beats locked black boxes.

TESLA SEMI ENTERS VOLUME PRODUCTION IN NEVADA

Electrek reports Tesla officially launched high-volume Semi production at a new 1.8-million-sq-ft Nevada plant built only for the truck — capacity cited at up to 50,000 Class 8 units a year — with deliveries “start now.” The redesigned Semi uses 4680 cells, a Cybertruck-shared drive unit, electric steering and Megacharger recovery of up to 60% range in 30 minutes; Standard Range ~325 miles and Long Range ~500 miles at 82,000 lbs were the February specs Electrek reprises.

Customer logos at the event included PepsiCo, DHL, US Foods and others; Electrek previously reported quotes near US$290k (500-mile) / US$260k (standard). State of Charge’s Oct 2 Autoweek newsletter also flagged the Semi launch alongside VW renaming the ID.4 as the ID.Tiguan. Canada FSD: no new unsupervised approval in this coverage.

Sources (3): Electrek · Tesla Semi page · State of Charge (newsletter)
Why it matters: Freight electrification is the Semi’s real market — useful context if you’re watching TSLA after Friday’s delivery beat.

EBY DEFENDS SNAP ELECTION, LNG AND TARIFF FIGHT IN CBC INTERVIEW

In a CBC Daybreak North interview posted Sunday, NDP Leader David Eby said he has no regrets calling the snap Oct 24 election despite polls showing many British Columbians did not want one. He cited a one-vote majority, repeated Conservative non-confidence attempts, and “culture war” legislature fights as reasons he needs a clearer mandate.

Eby argued Trump tariffs are “supercharging” cost-of-living pressure on housing materials, fuel and groceries; defended recruiting nearly 1,000 U.S. health-care workers; called a ~$1.5-billion LNG royalty spreadsheet error “awful” and said the Auditor General is reviewing it; and pitched LNG Canada Phase 2 as up to $28 billion in lifetime provincial revenue — saying if B.C. won’t produce low-carbon LNG, the U.S. will with looser standards. Conservatives dispute the snap-call rationale and the LNG pitch — treat as campaign framing, not settled fiscal math.

Sources (1): CBC BC
Why it matters: Trades, fuel and housing costs are exactly the kitchen-table issues Eby is campaigning on — and Conservatives will counter.
