🌎 World & Politics
POPE LEO XIV TO HAVE SURGERY ON LUNG NODULE

Pope Leo XIV announced Sunday he will have surgery in the coming days to remove a nodular lesion on his lung that was found during recent medical checks, AP reports (via CityNews). No details were given on the date or what the lesion is.

Sources (1): AP via CityNews
Why it matters: Worth watching for Catholic families; more details are expected this week.
 
109 MIGRANTS DIE AS BOAT CAPSIZES OFF DJIBOUTI

At least 109 migrants, including 15 children, died after a boat carrying 305 people capsized off Djibouti in east Africa, the International Organization for Migration said Sunday, AP reports.

Sources (1): AP via CityNews
Why it matters: The Red Sea migrant route is one of the deadliest; the same waters sit next to the Houthi conflict.

⚔️ Wars & Conflicts
🚨 BREAKING
🚨 HOUTHI STRIKE ON RIYADH AIRPORT KILLS 12, INJURES 300

The BBC reports 12 people were killed and about 300 injured in Saturday's attack on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh, the deadliest attack in Saudi Arabia since May 2015; U.S. and Saudi officials blame Yemen's Iran-backed Houthis, and the BBC separately reports the Houthis claimed attacks on Saudi airports. The Guardian argues the strike forces a rethink of Trump's plan to 'park' the Iran and Yemen wars. Separately, the BBC says a tanker identified as the Acers was hit by several projectiles in the Gulf off Qatar, with casualties reported. Early reports differed on the toll (one BBC piece said three Saudis killed), so the number may still move.

Sources (4): BBC · BBC (Houthi claim) · The Guardian (analysis) · BBC (tanker)
Why it matters: More Gulf attacks on airports and tankers tend to push oil, and pump prices, higher.
 
UPDATE
UPDATE: TRUMP SAYS UKRAINE NEEDS 'A NEW LEADER'; KYIV AND LONDON PUSH BACK

A day after his deal to buy Russian diesel, Trump said Ukraine needs 'a new leader', blaming Zelenskyy for high U.S. diesel prices and not doing enough to end the war, The Guardian reports. AP says Zelenskyy answered Sunday that only Russia is responsible for the war and vowed to keep striking Russian oil refineries, as Ukrainian drones hit a Russian data hub. Britain's defence secretary Wes Streeting said the U.K. stands behind Zelenskyy and that only Ukrainians should decide who leads them. Supporters of Trump's approach say cheaper diesel matters to U.S. drivers; critics call it pressure on Ukraine that rewards Moscow.

Sources (3): The Guardian · AP via CityNews · The Guardian (U.K.)
Why it matters: Diesel prices drive the cost of trucking and job-site fuel here too.
The BBC reports Gazans still face Israeli strikes despite the ceasefire as the peace deal stalls. (BBC)
🔭 Science & Research
AN AI FINDS SOLUTIONS 'HIDING IN PLAIN SIGHT' ACROSS 22 FIELDS

Science magazine reports an AI system surfaced existing solutions to open problems in 22 scientific fields, answers that were already in the literature but hadn't been connected. Quanta Magazine, in a related thread, reports mathematicians raced to finish the 'Unique Games' proof as AI closed in on it. Both pieces raise the same question: how much credit, and checking, AI-found results need.

Sources (2): Science · Quanta Magazine
Why it matters: Shows where AI is genuinely useful: digging up answers people already found but lost track of.
Japan's mission to bring home bits of Mars' moon Phobos is profiled by Science. (Science)
🤖 AI & Tech
NADELLA: ASSUME EVERY AI MODEL IS 'COMPROMISED'

Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella wrote in a long post on X that the world can no longer treat AI as 'a set of nested black boxes' whose advice we simply accept, and that we should assume all AI models are compromised, The Verge reports. The Verge also reports Anthropic is cutting off its internal evaluations from the internet, and that Anthropic's AI gave Philadelphia police a fake tip about an unsolved homicide.

Sources (2): The Verge (Nadella) · The Verge (Philadelphia tip)
Why it matters: A practical rule for anyone using AI tools: check the answer before you act on it.
🚀 Tesla / SpaceX / Starlink / xAI
TESLA DROPS 'FULL SELF-DRIVING' NAME IN EUROPE

Electrek reports Tesla now calls its package 'Tesla Assisted Driving' in Europe after Germany's Transport Ministry called the FSD name misleading, part of a deal in which Berlin backs Tesla's push for EU-wide approval. Reuters-sourced emails, per Electrek, show Tesla pushed the Dutch regulator RDW to scale back its FSD review before RDW approved it; eight EU countries now allow the system. No change to Canada's status, where FSD (Supervised) branding is unchanged.

Sources (2): Electrek · Electrek / Reuters
Why it matters: How Europe handles approval could shape what Canadian Tesla owners get next.
 
STARSHIP: SHIP 42 BACK FOR MORE CRYO TESTS

NASASpaceflight reports Ship 42, slated for Starship Flight 15, went back to Massey's for more cryo-proof testing while Ship 40 returned home. No Flight 15 date has been set.

Sources (1): NASASpaceflight
Why it matters: Flight timing is one of the things SpaceX investors watch.

B.C. CONSERVATIVE CAMPAIGN DIRECTOR UNDER FIRE OVER ANTI-ISLAM POSTS

CBC reports Conservative campaign director Katy Merrifield posted on X in June, 'F--k Islam. Defend Western civilization,' a post later removed; the National Council of Canadian Muslims said it was 'deeply hurt'. NDP Leader David Eby called on interim Conservative leader Lorne Doerkson to fire her, CTV reports. It follows the party dropping its Victoria-Beacon Hill candidate Joachim Agou over posts targeting Palestinians and Pakistanis; Agou has since apologized. BIV columnist Rob Shaw (commentary) says the Conservative leader has 'little room for error' after the quick firing. As of this morning no outlet reported a decision on Merrifield.

Sources (3): CBC News · CTV Vancouver · BIV (commentary)
Why it matters: A campaign-week distraction for the poll leaders; watch whether it moves numbers before the Oct 14 debate.
 
CAMPAIGN TRAIL: JOBS, DATA CENTRES, A CENTRIST PITCH

The Canadian Press (via CTV) says parties spent the start of the long weekend on jobs, data centres and resource royalties, a day after B.C. lost 20,000 jobs in September. CBC profiles Elenore Sturko's CentreBC, pitching itself as the centrist option. Earlier this week the Surrey Now-Leader reported a poll roundup with the Conservatives as frontrunners, and Doerkson promised in Surrey to raise the basic personal tax exemption to $32,000 from $13,216 (about a $1,000 cut); Eby promised in Surrey to fast-track international medical graduates.

Sources (4): CTV / CP · CBC News · Surrey Now-Leader (tax cut) · Surrey Now-Leader (doctors)
Why it matters: Tax and doctor promises both land on Surrey family budgets.
 
SURREY CIVIC: ADVANCE VOTING CONTINUES THIS WEEK

The Surrey Now-Leader reports advance voting for the civic election opened Saturday with more dates this week before final voting day Oct. 17. CBC reports candidates say overlapping civic and provincial elections are confusing voters. Check surrey.ca for sites before heading out.

Sources (3): Surrey Now-Leader · CBC News · City of Surrey
Why it matters: Two ballots, two dates: Surrey Oct 17, B.C. Oct 24.
