🌎 World & Politics
POPE LEO HAS LUNG CANCER; SURGEONS REMOVE PART OF HIS RIGHT LUNG

Surgeons at Rome's Gemelli hospital removed the lower lobe of Pope Leo XIV's right lung on Tuesday, and tests during the operation confirmed lung adenocarcinoma, the most common type of lung cancer, the Vatican says. The 90-minute keyhole operation had no complications, and the 71-year-old pontiff spent a quiet night, the Holy See press office said Wednesday morning, AP reports. Doctors are waiting on tests of the removed tissue before deciding on further treatment, and the bulletin doesn't give the cancer's stage, InfoVaticana notes. Audiences are cancelled through Oct. 20, and a Nov. 6–17 trip to Uruguay, Argentina and Peru hasn't been changed yet.

Sources (4): AP · CNN · Vatican News · InfoVaticana
Why it matters: It was caught early on a routine check, which is why doctors sound hopeful. Early lung screening works the same way for anyone.
 
STELCO PRESSES ON WITH LAYOFFS DESPITE OTTAWA'S LEGAL THREAT

U.S.-owned Stelco is pressing ahead with up to 500 layoffs, including 300 to 350 in Hamilton, despite Ottawa's threat of legal action over job promises Cleveland-Cliffs made when it bought the company in 2024, Global News and BNN Bloomberg report. The company blames a 58% plunge in steel exports to the U.S. under tariffs, plus cheap foreign imports; Ottawa says the commitments are binding, and Prime Minister Mark Carney says the government will pursue its legal options. Ontario NDP Leader Marit Stiles and federal NDP Leader Avi Lewis want the steelmaker nationalized, CBC reports, while critics warn that would scare off investors.

Sources (3): Global News · BNN Bloomberg · CBC
Why it matters: Tariff fights over steel feed straight into the price of sheet and coil on B.C. job sites.

🚨 BREAKING
RUSSIA DARKENS KYIV, AND STARLINK GOES QUIET FOR UKRAINE'S DRONES

Russia fired 39 ballistic missiles and more than 120 drones at energy sites in Kyiv, the Kyiv region and Kharkiv overnight, leaving at least 600,000 people without power, President Zelensky said; Ukraine's prime minister says all three of Kyiv's city heat-and-power plants were targeted. At least four people were killed in Kyiv, most of them in a nine-storey apartment block, the Kyiv Independent reports (other outlets' counts are lower so far). Separately, the Guardian reports that Starlink terminals moving faster than about 160 km/h on the military version of the service suddenly lost their connection Tuesday evening, and presidential tech adviser Serhiy Beskrestnov says several combat missions failed. SpaceX hasn't confirmed any change, fixed terminals still work, and activist Serhii Sternenko's claim that it was done at the Trump administration's request is unproven. Washington has been pushing Kyiv to stop hitting Russian refineries; Trump denies cutting off intelligence, and Ukraine's air force says it got no warning of the first missile wave but did get warnings for three later ones.

Sources (5): Guardian · Kyiv Independent · Kyiv Post · RFE/RL · X news summary
Why it matters: Ordinary Starlink service isn't reported affected, but it shows one company's settings can change a war overnight.
 
HOUTHI MISSILE HITS ARAMCO'S HEADQUARTERS AS THE SAUDI FIGHT WIDENS

A Houthi ballistic missile hit an office tower at Saudi Aramco's headquarters in Dhahran overnight, causing what the Saudi energy ministry calls a 'limited fire' and no injuries, the BBC reports; an Aramco source told Reuters the top three floors were damaged. Debris from intercepted missiles hurt four people in Riyadh. The Houthis said it was payback for Saudi-led strikes on a fuel depot near Sanaa and an oil terminal at Ras Isa, and they promise more attacks on Saudi airports and oil sites in the coming days. The IMF's Kristalina Georgieva warned of 'negative spillovers for everybody,' and Trump said the Houthis are 'purposefully trying to drive up oil prices.' The SitDeck brief cautions that damage to an office isn't the same as lost oil output.

Sources (2): BBC · SitDeck brief (newsletter)
Why it matters: With oil near US$95, every hit on Gulf energy sites shows up at the pump in Surrey.

A NEW TELESCOPE IN CHILE HUNTS FOR THE UNIVERSE'S FIRST SPLIT SECOND

The US$120 million Simons Observatory in Chile's Atacama Desert starts this month with its 6-metre Large Aperture Telescope, which will map the cosmic microwave background, light from 380,000 years after the Big Bang, Science reports. Its three small telescopes are hunting for faint swirls in that light that would be evidence of 'cosmic inflation,' the theorized burst of expansion in the universe's first instant. The team is careful: a South Pole telescope claimed those swirls in 2014 and had to retract it a year later because the signal was mostly dust in our own galaxy, and spokesperson Jo Dunkley says it may take 'five years or so' to see them. Meanwhile it should find up to 40,000 galaxy clusters and may finally pin down the neutrino's mass.

Sources (1): Science
Why it matters: Note the scientists' own caution: this is a five-year hunt, not a discovery yet.
 
PERSEVERANCE IS NOW THE FARTHEST-DRIVEN VEHICLE ON ANOTHER WORLD

NASA's Perseverance rover passed 45.22 km of driving on Mars on Oct. 11, beating the old off-world record of 45.16 km set by the Opportunity rover, NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory says. It had already passed the totals of the Soviet Lunokhod 2 moon rover and Apollo 17's astronauts in their lunar buggy. The team got a Guinness World Records certificate, and collectSPACE notes Perseverance and Curiosity are still driving.

Sources (2): NASA JPL · collectSPACE
Why it matters: Every extra kilometre is free science from hardware that's already paid for.

🤖 AI & Tech
GOOGLE FIRES BACK WITH GEMINI 4 ARGON, AND UNDERCUTS ON PRICE

Google released Gemini 4 Argon, its first big frontier model since Gemini 3 late last year, and says it beats OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra and Anthropic's Claude models on coding, cybersecurity, science, finance and law tests, The Verge reports; those are Google's own benchmark claims. It starts at US$2 per million input tokens and US$10 per million output tokens, about a fifth of its rivals' prices, doubling after November. It's rolling out to paying Pro and Ultra users first. Google DeepMind also put out a robotics version and a safety write-up, and it landed a day after Meta's new Muse Wonder model, The Verge notes.

Sources (3): The Verge · Google DeepMind · The Information, The Briefing (newsletter)
Why it matters: A price war among the big AI labs means cheaper tools for estimating and paperwork.
 
OPENAI ENGINEER CHARGED WITH BUGGING AN EXECUTIVE BOARDROOM

Federal prosecutors charged OpenAI engineer Abdulrahman Alfozan, a 35-year-old Saudi national, with trying to intercept conversations, after a recording device wrapped in toilet paper turned up in a TV's USB port in an executive conference room, The Verge and BBC report. The Justice Department says the device went in around Sept. 28 and CCTV showed Alfozan near the room on several dates. OpenAI says it used its own AI models to trace the device, fired him and called the FBI, and it has 'no indications' that model weights, code or customer data were taken.

Sources (3): The Verge · BBC · The Information, The Briefing (newsletter)
Why it matters: AI secrets are now worth spying for, so expect tighter security at every tech company.

TESLA PAUSES THE $39,490 MODEL 3 IN CANADA UNTIL MARCH

Tesla has stopped taking Canadian orders for the Shanghai-built Model 3 Premium (RWD and AWD), and its site says 'high demand exhausted Tesla's import quota' with orders expected to resume in March 2027, Electrek reports. But Ottawa's own tracker shows 33,069 of this year's 49,000 permits for Chinese-built EVs still unused, so Electrek suspects Ottawa set aside room for newcomers such as BYD and Geely; Global Affairs Canada hasn't published per-company numbers. The only Model 3 you can order in Canada now is the Performance AWD, listed at $936 a month in financing.

Sources (1): Electrek
Why it matters: The cheapest new Tesla in Canada is off the menu until spring: pay up for the Performance, look at used, or wait.

Tesla posted that its next-generation Roadster event streams live Thursday at 7:55 PM Central, which is 5:55 PM PT, two weeks after the original Oct. 1 date, Electrek notes; X posts say bad weather caused the delay and the event is near Waco, Texas. Sources (4): @Tesla on X · Electrek: Roadster · Electrek: China · X news summary

SPACEX SHARES DROP 4% AS THE FAA OPENS A BIG STARBASE REVIEW

SpaceX (SPCX) fell 4.2% to US$156.54 Tuesday. FinanceFeeds and Invezz tie the drop to the FAA's notice that it will do a full environmental impact statement and public hearings on SpaceX's plan to go from 25 Starship launches a year at Starbase to as many as 456 launches and 912 landings. Investing.com points instead to worries over SpaceX's planned US$40 billion of new debt for Nvidia chips, and 24/7 Wall St. to a New York City AI-safety probe where SpaceX testifies Friday, so the cause isn't settled. In orbit, @SpaceX says Cargo Dragon (CRS-35) docks with the space station Thursday around 8 AM PT, and Ship 40, the first Starship to come home, is standing in a Starbase Megabay for heat-shield study.

Sources (5): FinanceFeeds · Invezz · Investing.com · 24/7 Wall St. · @SpaceX on X
Why it matters: A long FAA review could slow Starship flights, and that matters for Starlink's next-generation satellites.

SOUTH FRASER HOSPITAL BACKERS SAY FRASER HEALTH IS SHORTCHANGED

The foundations behind Langley Memorial, Peace Arch and Delta hospitals have formed a South Fraser Healthcare Alliance to lobby Victoria, the Langley Advance Times reports. Their numbers: Fraser Health gets about $2,621 per person a year, against $3,922 to $4,312 in B.C.'s other four health regions, and the wait for a long-term care bed averages 402 days, compared with 290 province-wide. The alliance is chaired by former Langley City mayor and MLA Peter Fassbender, and it wants hospital growth tied to housing and population targets. 'No matter who the next government is, the issue is going to be equally prominent,' he said.

Sources (1): Langley Advance Times
Why it matters: South Fraser families get the least health money per head in B.C.; ask candidates about it.

DEBATE NIGHT: TORIES LEAD, BUT ONE VOTER IN SEVEN HASN'T DECIDED

David Eby (NDP), Lorne Doerkson (Conservative) and Emily Lowan (Green) meet tonight from 6:15 to 8 PM in the campaign's only scheduled leaders' debate, moderated by Angus Reid Institute president Shachi Kurl, Global News says. Two fresh polls both put the Conservatives ahead, by different margins: Ipsos for Global (Oct. 7–11) has them at 43% to the NDP's 38%, and Research Co. (Oct. 9–11, 808 likely voters) has them at 44% to 42%, with the Greens at 8%, CentreBC and OneBC at 2% each, CTV reports. Research Co. finds 15% undecided, almost double the 8% at this point in 2024, many of them 55 or older. Doerkson's approval is 45% against Eby's 49%, and Ipsos's Kyle Braid says Doerkson's job tonight is simply to 'look like a premier,' while Eby has to make a Conservative government sound risky.

Sources (3): Global News · CTV Vancouver · Ipsos release
Why it matters: With this many undecided, tonight could swing tight Fraser Valley seats like Surrey-Serpentine River.
 
DAYCARE MATH, SHIPYARDS AND PST HOLIDAYS: THE MONEY FIGHT

Eby says his family has donated back the whole $10-a-day saving, about $8,000 since 2024 counting this year's planned gift, the Langley Advance Times reports, and he wouldn't say whether he claimed a tax receipt; the Conservatives posted on X that the figure is $3,200 since mid-2024, so the two sides' numbers don't match. Conservative Margareta Dovgal says her party would keep existing $10-a-day spots and is open to means testing, while child-care advocate Sharon Gregson says the real issue is the NDP's unkept promise of $10-a-day care for everyone. At the Board of Trade, Doerkson pitched a three-year PST holiday on business investment in machinery, tools and software plus capped permit timelines; Eby promised a B.C. shipyard to build ferries at home and higher taxes on top earners, calling Tory plans 'billions of dollars of unfunded commitments'; and Lowan pitched a wealth tax starting at 5% above $50 million, the Surrey Now-Leader reports. BIV notes none of them is seriously tackling the projected $13.8-billion deficit.

Sources (5): Langley Advance Times · @Conservative_BC on X · Surrey Now-Leader · BIV · Vaughn Palmer, Vancouver Sun (opinion)
Why it matters: A PST break on tools and machinery would hit small shops' costs right away, but someone pays for the deficit.
 
SURREY ANGLES: MASSEY TUNNEL MEMO, NEW CLINIC PROMISES

A December 2025 memo from the premier's office shows the province expected the Massey Tunnel replacement to cost about $8.5 billion, roughly double the $4.15 billion still listed in February's budget, CTV News reports; Conservative Trevor Halford (Surrey-White Rock) calls it misleading voters, and Eby says the cost wasn't final and bids are coming in 'significantly lower.' The memo also pushes completion from 2030 to 2031 for a crossing that carries more than 80,000 vehicles a day. On health care, the NDP promises three more urgent and primary care centres in Surrey on top of the four already open, plus one in North Delta with no timeline given, the Surrey Now-Leader reports.

Sources (2): CTV Vancouver · Surrey Now-Leader
Why it matters: Highway 99 and the tunnel are the South Surrey to Richmond lifeline, and the bill keeps climbing.
 
SURREY: LAST TWO ADVANCE DAYS, AND LOCKE GRILLED OVER THE CHIEF'S FIRING

Surrey's advance polls are open today and Thursday, 8 AM to 8 PM, at 10 sites, and today is the last day to request a mail ballot, the city says. About 26,000 ballots were cast on the first two advance days, Oct. 7 and 10, and Cloverdale Recreation Centre was the busiest site both days (1,949, then 2,363). Imagine Surrey mayoral candidate Mike Starchuk is pressing Mayor Brenda Locke on what she knew before the police board fired Surrey Police chief Norm Lipinski; Locke has said 'Not really,' and her Surrey Connect party says the mayor 'has no involvement in HR matters,' the Vancouver Sun reports. Surrey First's Linda Annis promises an anti-corruption review, and Lipinski's untested court petition claims political motives. The left-leaning Tyee frames the race as Locke against old rival Doug McCallum, who lost to her by under 1,000 votes in 2022, with policing, the arena land swap and health care as the big issues.

Sources (4): City of Surrey · Surrey advance turnout · Vancouver Sun (paywalled) · The Tyee (left-leaning)
Why it matters: Cloverdale Rec Centre (6188 176 St) is open till 8 tonight; skip Saturday's lineup by voting early.
