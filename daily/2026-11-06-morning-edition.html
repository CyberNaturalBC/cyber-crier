NASA'S SWIFT OBSERVATORY RE-ENTERS AFTER NEARLY 22 YEARS

NASA’s Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory re-entered Earth’s atmosphere over Indonesia after nearly 22 years on orbit, ScienceDaily relays from NASA — more than ten times its original two-year design life.

Read more

Swift’s three telescopes pioneered rapid multi-wavelength follow-up of gamma-ray bursts, feeding black-hole and exploding-star science.

What the paper actually says: this is a mission-end announcement, not a new research paper. Peer-review status: n/a (agency release). No scientific claim beyond the re-entry and legacy is asserted here.

Sources (1): ScienceDaily / NASA

Why it matters: A workhorse GRB telescope is gone — burst alerts will lean harder on remaining assets.

SOFTWARE 'DIGITAL TWIN' SQUEEZES MORE EXOPLANETS OUT OF JWST DATA

Ars Technica reports new software that builds a physical model of JWST to cut noise and pull more usable exoplanet imaging from existing observations — a “digital twin” approach also covered by Universe Today.

Read more

The pitch is better contrast for faint companions without new hardware.

What the paper actually says: coverage describes a methods/engineering advance on calibration and modelling; check the underlying methods paper/DOI in the Ars piece for sample size and claimed contrast gains before treating any specific new planet as confirmed. Peer-review status: verify on the linked journal/preprint — do not assume JWST press framing equals a discovery paper.

Sources (2): Ars Technica · Universe Today

Why it matters: Better pipeline maths can unlock science already paid for in JWST time — no new launch required.

ARXIV: LITTLE RED DOTS AND EARLY BLACK-HOLE SEEDS STILL CAN'T BE UNTANGLED

What the paper actually says: In arXiv:2611.07006, “Black Hole Seeds and Little Red Dots…,” the authors argue JWST’s faint AGN and compact “Little Red Dots” challenge simple seeding models — high number densities and, in some systems, black-hole mass that looks high relative to stellar mass.

Read more

They propose a Multi-Cycle Treasury program because “black holes have no memory”: current samples cannot separate seeding from later growth. Sample: demographic synthesis / proposal paper, not a single new object discovery. Peer-review status: preprint on arXiv — not peer reviewed.

Press framing often jumps to “JWST broke black-hole theory”; the paper is more cautious and asks for a coordinated observing program.

Sources (1): arXiv:2611.07006 (preprint)

Why it matters: A reminder that flashy JWST headlines often outrun what the preprint actually claims.

NEW METHOD MARGINALIZES GW DETECTOR CALIBRATION ERROR — STILL FINDS TWO RINGDOWN MODES

What the paper actually says: arXiv:2611.07023 extends analytic marginalization over gravitational-wave detector calibration uncertainty into the time domain with basis splines, then applies it to black-hole ringdown in GW250114 and GW250207.

Read more

For GW250114 the authors still find strong evidence for two ringdown modes after marginalizing calibration. Limitations: method paper on two loud events; calibration systematics are “generally small” but non-zero. Peer-review status: preprint — not peer reviewed.

This is incremental reliability work for LIGO/Virgo/KAGRA inference, not a new detection headline.

Sources (1): arXiv:2611.07023 (preprint)

Why it matters: Cleaner error bars on ringdown tests are how general relativity gets stress-tested for real.
