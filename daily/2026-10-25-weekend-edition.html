ONE SOURCE
ONE ANTIMATTER PARTICLE, TRAPPED FOR MONTHS, TO TEST THE STANDARD MODEL

Gerald Gabrielse's team at Northwestern has trapped a single positron (the antimatter twin of the electron) in a 'quantum cyclotron' cooled to a fraction of a degree above absolute zero, and can hold it for months, Physical Review Letters reports. They can step it up and down its energy levels and read its spin without disturbing it. The goal: measure the positron's magnetism about as precisely as the electron's (1 part in 10 trillion), maybe 30 times better than before. If the two ever differ, it would break a core symmetry of physics (CPT) and point to new physics.

Sources (1): Phys.org
Why it matters: This is the setup, not the result; the payoff is the measurement still to come.
 
ONE SOURCE
BENNU WAS BORN WHERE THE INNER AND OUTER SOLAR SYSTEM MIXED

ETH Zurich researchers analysing half a gram of NASA's Bennu samples found iron, titanium and chromium isotope fingerprints that match asteroid Ryugu and rare CI meteorites, and fit neither the inner nor outer Solar System. They propose Bennu's material gathered near the 'water-ice line', where dust from both regions mixed, with young Jupiter acting as a barrier that let only fine dust through (Science Advances). 'Bennu is a hybrid,' says Maria Schönbächler. Japan's Phobos sample-return mission is due to launch at the end of October.

Sources (1): ScienceDaily / ETH Zurich
Why it matters: It reshuffles where Earth's building blocks, water included, may have come from.
Today's Astronomy Picture of the Day is a supercomputer map of dark matter filaments strung through the universe like spider webs (NASA APOD). (NASA APOD)

STRIKE ON AT HIGHLAND VALLEY COPPER, CANADA'S BIGGEST COPPER MINE

United Steelworkers Local 7619 walked out at Teck's Highland Valley Copper near Logan Lake at 8 AM Saturday, CFJC reports. 🚨 BREAKING
CONSERVATIVES WIN 64 SEATS: FIRST TORY GOVERNMENT IN B.C. SINCE 1928

With 99.6% of ballot boxes counted, Elections BC has the Conservatives leading in 64 of 93 seats with 48.1% of the vote, the NDP 27 (37.4%) and the Greens 2 (11.0%); CentreBC and OneBC took about 1.5% each and no seats. 47 is a majority. In 2024 it was NDP 47, Conservatives 44. Interim leader Lorne Doerkson, 58, the Cariboo-Chilcotin MLA, becomes B.C.'s 38th premier: 'B.C. is definitely blue,' he said, promising to 'cut red tape like it's never been cut before.' David Eby, who called the election two years early, narrowly held Vancouver-Point Grey and is resigning as NDP leader: the loss 'breaks my heart, and I accept full responsibility.' Four NDP cabinet ministers lost, including Surrey's Jagrup Brar and Jessie Sunner. Preliminary turnout was about 48%, down from 58.7% in 2024, and final polls had the gap at 4 to 6 points; the real margin was about 11.

Sources (5): Elections BC preliminary results · CBC News · Reuters (via Investing.com) · Vancouver Sun (8 takeaways) · CBC News (ministers)
Why it matters: A big majority means the new government can pass its program without needing anyone else's votes for four years.
 
UPDATE
SURREY GOES 10 FOR 10; SAHOTA WINS SURREY-SERPENTINE RIVER BY 4,435

Surrey-Serpentine River, all 70 boxes counted: Jessy Sahota (Conservative) 10,114 (58.1%), Sikander Kang (NDP) 5,679 (32.6%), Judy Higginbotham (CentreBC) 890 (5.1%), Todd Holowchak (OneBC) 593 (3.4%), Errol Povah (Freedom) 127 (0.7%). The margin went from 435 votes in 2024 to 4,435. Across Surrey, Conservatives took all 10 seats, up from six: Nicholas Cartmell (Cloverdale, by 3,305), Purewal (Fleetwood), Garry Thind (Guildford), Harj Dhaliwal (Newton), Zeeshan Wahla (City Centre, by about 395), Mandeep Dhaliwal (North), Megan Knight (South), Bryan Tepper (Panorama) and Trevor Halford (White Rock). NDP incumbents Sunner, Begg, Brar and Shah are out. 'I did everything possible I could do,' said six-term MLA Brar. CentreBC leader Elenore Sturko finished third in Surrey South. In Langley, Conservatives Jody Toor, Misty Van Popta and Harman Bhangu won.

Sources (3): BC Ballot: Surrey-Serpentine River · Surrey Now-Leader · Surrey Now-Leader (City Centre)
Why it matters: Sahota becomes Surrey-Serpentine River's MLA once the final count is done; City Centre is the only Surrey seat close enough to watch in the final count.
 
WHAT'S NEXT: AN INTERIM-LEADER RULE, A FINAL COUNT, AND A SWEARING-IN DATE TBA

The Conservative constitution has a rule about interim leaders that has to be dealt with before Doerkson can stay on; party president Mauro Francis says the board will do whatever is necessary. No swearing-in date or cabinet has been announced. The final count runs Nov. 6-10. The NDP will need a new leader; the party says Eby's record includes 'a million more people connected to a family doctor'. Doerkson's platform promises: a tax cut and freeze with no new taxes, doubling natural gas output by 2032, faster permits, cancelling the PST on professional services (BIV) and repealing DRIPA (Tyee). Carney congratulated Doerkson and said he wants to 'get more homes built'; Poilievre called it 'a strong majority mandate'.

Sources (6): CBC News (5 reasons) · The Tyee (left-leaning) · BIV · BC NDP on X · Mark Carney on X · Pierre Poilievre on X
Why it matters: Watch the first 100 days: the budget, and which promises get done first.
 
ON THE JOB SITE: CBAS AND THE MASSEY TUNNEL ARE NOW ON THE TABLE

The Conservative platform promises to 'restore broad competitive bidding for provincial infrastructure', which ends the Community Benefits Agreement rules that steer big public projects to building-trades crews, and to cancel the Massey Tunnel and go back to a bridge. For: the Progressive Contractors Association, whose members include CLAC-affiliated firms, says CBAs raised costs about 20% and shut out 85% of the construction workforce, and wants them gone within 100 days. The ICBA called the result a 'sigh of relief' (BIV). Against: the BC Building Trades says cancelling the tunnel would put 200 workers on site out of work, put 1,700 jobs and up to $3 billion in federal money at risk, and waste $581 million already spent. UBC economist Werner Antweiler told BIV the deficit remains a problem for any government, and that a confrontational approach to First Nations could mean court fights that scare off investment. Nothing has changed yet: no bill has been tabled and no cabinet named.

Sources (3): Progressive Contractors Association · BC Building Trades · BIV
Why it matters: CBAs decide who staffs B.C.'s hospitals, bridges and SkyTrain jobs, so this is the platform item that most directly touches construction workers.
Green leader Emily Lowan, 26, won her Saanich seat and the Greens held two: 'We may have a small caucus, but tonight we have inherited an enormous responsibility... to hold a majority Conservative government to account' (CBC). (CBC News)
