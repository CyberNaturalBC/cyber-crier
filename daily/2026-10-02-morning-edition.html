UPDATE: CARNEY LISTS PACIFIC LINK AS NATIONAL INTEREST — $44B OIL LINE AIMED AT ROBERTS BANK

Prime Minister Mark Carney announced Thursday in Fort McMurray that the West Coast oil pipeline — now officially named Pacific Link — is the first project listed as a project of national interest under Schedule 1 of the Building Canada Act. CBC and the PMO say that moves Ottawa from whether to build to how, consolidating and fast-tracking approvals and environmental assessments. Conditions are targeted by September 2027, with construction eyed thereafter and operations later (CBC cites a 2032–33 view).

The Vancouver Sun puts the price near $44 billion for a roughly 1,250 km line from near Bruderheim, Alta., to an expanded terminal at Roberts Bank in Delta. Capacity is described as about one million barrels a day. Carney tied the project to ocean-protection measures and Alberta's Pathways carbon-capture work. The Tyee (left-leaning) and Indigenous leaders warn the designation short-circuits consultation; CP reports First Nations along the route were given about two months to comment on the designation itself. A court challenge is widely expected.

Sources (5): PMO · CBC News · Vancouver Sun · The Tyee · CJME / CP (consultation)
Why it matters: The terminus is next door in Delta. Years of pipe, steel and terminal work could follow — along with tanker, cost and consultation fights that hit Fraser Valley politics.

UPDATE: OVER 100 DRONES HIT UKRAINE OVERNIGHT; KYIV HIGH-RISE DEATH; FP-7 MISSILE'S FIRST COMBAT USE

Zelenskyy said Russia launched more than 100 attack drones overnight into Friday, nearly 50 of them jet-powered. Ukrinform and the Kyiv Independent report hits on Kyiv's Southern Bridge, a residential high-rise (one dead, others injured), a Kherson kindergarten, a Kharkiv grocery store, and apartments in Kramatorsk and Sumy. Ukraine's air force tallied 108 Shahed-type drones including 47 jet-powered ones. A separate FPV strike hit a passenger bus in Kramatorsk Friday morning.

The Guardian's war briefing says Zelenskyy hailed the first combat use of Ukraine's indigenous FP-7 tactical ballistic missile (~200 km range, per Ukrainian analysts), with Fire Point saying it is also being developed as a ballistic interceptor. Wednesday's heavy grid strike still echoes: France 24 reported seven dead and emergency power cuts in Kyiv. Al Jazeera, citing Ukraine's military, says Russia took a 2026 monthly casualty record in September (~46,230) — treat that as Kyiv's figure.

Sources (5): Ukrinform · Kyiv Independent · The Guardian · France 24 / Reuters · Al Jazeera
Why it matters: Winter grid war plus Hormuz risk both push fuel prices — the work truck and home heating bill feel it first.

🔭 Science & Research
WEBB MAPS PLANET-SHATTERING COLLISIONS IN 21 EXTREME DEBRIS DISKS

NASA says the James Webb Space Telescope, with Spitzer data, surveyed 21 "extreme debris disks" — dusty leftovers of violent impacts. TechTimes and NASA summarize two flavours: silica-rich disks from high-energy, Mars-scale smashups seen only around stars younger than about 300 million years, and silica-poor, forsterite-rich disks from gentler Moon-scale or glancing hits that appear across ages. The chemistry maps how terrestrial planets form — and sometimes destroy each other — after the protoplanetary disk clears.

Sources (2): NASA Science · TechTimes
Why it matters: A clean cosmology/planetary-science beat — how rocky worlds get built (and broken).

IPSOS FOR GLOBAL: CONSERVATIVES 42%, NDP 40% — STATISTICAL TIE AFTER FINDLAY EXIT

An Ipsos poll for Global News (field dates Sep 25–29, n=1,001 online, ±3.7 pts) puts the B.C. Conservatives at 42% of decided voters (+7 from Sep 8–14) and the NDP at 40% (−5) — a statistical tie. Ipsos and Global frame it as a rebound after Kerry-Lynne Findlay's resignation, with interim leader Lorne Doerkson still little-known (29% haven't heard of him; 20% favourable / 19% unfavourable). David Eby still leads as preferred premier 33%–17% over Doerkson; 36% have no opinion. Greens ~10%, OneBC and CentreBC ~3% each in the decided tables.

Both angles: Conservatives can claim momentum without Findlay; NDP can note Eby's name recognition and that a 2-point gap is inside the error bar. No lecturing — just the numbers.

Sources (2): Global News · Ipsos
Why it matters: Province-wide horse race with three weeks left — in Surrey-Serpentine River, the ballot still comes down to local names on Oct 24.
