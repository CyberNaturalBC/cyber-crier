🌎 World & Politics

UPDATE: PACIFIC LINK — FIRST NATIONS PRESS CONSULTATION AS FAST-TRACK BEGINS

Thursday’s national-interest designation for Pacific Link — the Alberta-to-Roberts Bank oil pipeline Carney listed under the Building Canada Act — is still the Canadian political story of the week. CBC says Ottawa has moved from whether to build to how, with a conditions document due by September 2027 and operations eyed for 2032–33. Capacity is described as about one million barrels a day on a southern route that avoids B.C.’s North Coast tanker ban zone.

CBC BC reports First Nations leaders saying meaningful consultation should have come before major deals were signed; B.C.’s energy ministry has framed it as Alberta’s and Ottawa’s file. Canada and Alberta would share equal ownership, with Indigenous communities offered at least 10% financed through loan guarantees, and Pembina Pipeline as a private investor alongside Trans Mountain Corp. leading development.

Sources (4): PMO · CBC News · CBC BC · BBC
Why it matters: Roberts Bank is next door in Delta. Years of steel, terminal and tanker fights will sit on Fraser Valley ballots and job boards.

UPDATE
UPDATE: THIRD U.S. CARRIER AND MARINES HEAD TO GULF AS MORE HORMUZ TANKERS HIT

Al Jazeera, citing a U.S. official, says the USS Theodore Roosevelt carrier strike group left San Diego bound for waters near Iran, followed by the Makin Island amphibious force with more than 2,000 Marines. By late November, three carriers and two amphibious groups could be positioned near Iran. Fortune/AP put the reinforcement near 9,000 additional troops aboard those ships. Trump has rejected Iran’s Hormuz-reopening proposal as “not good enough.”

UKMTO and maritime reports say six tankers were struck in about a week in the Strait of Hormuz, including Kuwait’s Kazimah III and other vessels near Oman’s coast. The New York Times, via regional reporting, says Iran has been launching dozens of drones and missiles at commercial ships weekly; Iran’s Revolutionary Guard has publicly claimed regular Hormuz strikes. Oil flows are partly recovering even as the threat continues.

Sources (4): Al Jazeera · Fortune / AP · World Ports / UKMTO tally · Inquirer / NYT data
Why it matters: Hormuz still prices diesel and furnace oil. A wider Gulf fight shows up first at the pump and on shop materials.

UPDATE: RUSSIA HITS KYIV BRIDGE; UKRAINE OPEN TO INDIA-BACKED ENERGY TRUCE TALK

Al Jazeera and The Independent report Russia struck Kyiv’s Northern Bridge, suspending traffic; multiple Dnipro road bridges have been closed or damaged in recent days. Overnight into Saturday, Ukraine’s air defence tallied about 157 strike drones (RBC-Ukraine). The General Staff reported 194 frontline clashes Friday, with heavy pressure near Kostiantynivka and Pokrovsk.

India Today says Ukraine is open to an India-backed limited truce covering energy infrastructure and the Black Sea, while Moscow has rejected a broader frontline ceasefire. Zelenskyy has again accused Putin of ordering forces to abandon rules of war as bridge and city strikes continue.

Sources (4): Al Jazeera · RBC-Ukraine · LB.ua · India Today
Why it matters: Grid and bridge strikes keep European energy nerves high — another input into global fuel costs that reach B.C.
🔭 Science & Research
ONE SOURCE
SEA MONKEYS HELP REWRITE A RULE OF TURBULENCE

Quanta Magazine reports physicists watching brine shrimp (“sea monkeys”) found that a simple factor can reverse the usual one-way energy cascade in turbulent flow — something textbooks treated as fixed. The experiment shows living swimmers can push energy upscale as well as down, rewriting an assumption about how turbulence redistributes energy.

Sources (1): Quanta Magazine
Why it matters: Pure science curiosity — and a reminder that field observations still rewrite fluid-dynamics rules.
 
NASA CREW-12 HEADS HOME FROM THE SPACE STATION

NASA says SpaceX Crew-12 is concluding its ISS science mission, with Jessica Meir, Jack Hathaway, ESA’s Sophie Adenot and Roscosmos cosmonaut Andrei Fedyaev scheduled to return in early October. Separately, NASA opened applications for the next class of mission-control flight directors at Johnson Space Center.

Sources (2): NASA · NASA (flight directors)
Why it matters: Routine station ops — the SpaceX human-spaceflight cadence that underpins SPCX.

OPENAI SAFETY LEAD QUITS AND GOES PUBLIC; GOOGLE PUSHES GEMINI 4 ARGON

The Verge reports David Robinson, who wrote safety reports for major OpenAI model releases, resigned and published a warning in The Atlantic. Separately, The Hacker News says OpenAI parted ways with three safety researchers over mishandling of sensitive information, and ABC notes OpenAI disclosed another unauthorized access incident involving an Australian government agency.

Sources (5): The Verge · Google DeepMind · CNBC · OpenAI (GPT-6 guide) · The Hacker News
Why it matters: Lab safety drama and model races both move the AI tools (and chip stocks) tradespeople and side projects lean on.
 
APPLE TIGHTENS MAC DISK ACCESS AS AI AGENTS RAISE RISK

Apple said it will limit “full disk access” on Mac because AI agents substantially increase the risk of over-broad file permissions. The Verge, citing Apple’s Friday update, says new controls aim to keep users — not free-roaming agents — in charge of what software can see on disk.

Sources (1): The Verge
Why it matters: If you run AI helpers on a Mac at home or in the shop office, permission prompts are about to get stricter — on purpose.
🚀 Tesla / SpaceX / Starlink / xAI
TESLA Q3: 486,532 DELIVERIES — DOWN 2% YOY, BUT A CLEAR BEAT

Tesla’s SEC exhibit and Electrek/CNBC report 486,532 vehicles delivered in Q3 2026 and 464,391 produced. Deliveries fell about 2.1% from last year’s record 497,099 but beat Tesla’s own compiled consensus (~461,974) by roughly 25,000 units, and rose from Q2’s 480,126. Model 3/Y were 478,237 of deliveries; “Other Models” (mainly Cybertruck) were 8,295. Energy storage deployed 13.7 GWh.

Shares jumped about 4.7% Friday to US$370.59. Full Q3 financials and a Q&A webcast are set for Wednesday, Oct 21 after the close. Canada FSD status: no new regulatory green light in this report — Supervised Autopilot / FSD remains limited relative to U.S. robotaxi talk.

Sources (3): Tesla / SEC exhibit · Electrek · CNBC
Why it matters: Delivery beats move TSLA — and set the tone for Oct 21 earnings, which Tesla owners and investors alike will be watching.

🏔️ Local — BC / Fraser Valley / Surrey
SURREY CONNECT UNVEILS ~$1.5B CAPITAL PLATFORM — CLOVERDALE/CLAYTON POOL LISTED

The Cloverdale Reporter says Mayor Brenda Locke and her Surrey Connect slate, endorsed by firefighters Local 1271, unveiled a nearly $1.5 billion four-year capital platform on Oct 1, with taxes promised at or below inflation. Line items include a $120 million Cloverdale/Clayton pool, a $150 million South Surrey ice arena, a $150 million concert hall, a $120 million City Centre pool, a $350 million public safety campus, and street/traffic-calming, parks and bike-path packages.

Locke said Highway 10 congestion around the new Cloverdale hospital precinct is mainly a provincial road file, while any bypass would be the city’s. The police union has separately voiced concerns about the firefighters’ endorsement (Cloverdale Reporter, Oct 2). Election stories continue in Elections.

Sources (2): Cloverdale Reporter (platform) · Cloverdale Reporter (police union)
Why it matters: A Cloverdale/Clayton pool and Hwy 10 hospital traffic are kitchen-table Surrey issues — and Locke is asking for another term on Oct 17.

🚨 BREAKING
🚨 TODAY 1 PM: B.C. NOMINATIONS CLOSE — SURREY-SERPENTINE RIVER HAS FOUR ON THE LIST

Elections BC’s candidate website report stamped Oct 3, 2026 7:00 AM lists four complete nominations in Surrey-Serpentine River before the 1 PM Pacific close: Jessy Sahota (Conservative), Sikander Kang (BC NDP), Todd Holowchak (OneBC), and Errol Povah (Freedom Party of BC). Linda Hepner, who won the seat for the Conservatives in 2024 by 435 votes, has withdrawn. Late filings before 1 PM can still change the official list.

Province-wide, Ipsos (Sept 25–29, 1,001 adults; ±3.7 pts) has Conservatives at 42% and NDP at 40% among decided voters — a statistical tie, with Conservatives up seven points and NDP down five since mid-September (Global News / Ipsos). Advance voting for B.C. runs Oct 16–21; final voting day is Sat Oct 24.

Sources (5): Elections BC candidate list (PDF) · Elections BC candidate guide · Ipsos · Global News · BCPoliTracker
Why it matters: Surrey-Serpentine River is an open seat. After 1 PM the ballot names lock — know who’s filed before you decide.
