TWO GROUPS CERTIFY THE CLAUDE-ASSISTED RIEMANN PROOF: 'FULL CONFIDENCE' IT'S CORRECT

The Lean Focused Research Organization and the Mathlib Initiative said Saturday they have "full confidence" that Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge's machine-checked proof of the Riemann hypothesis is correct, after independent re-compiles and a "comparator run" checking the code didn't quietly change what the symbols mean (Guardian, Scientific American).

Read more

MIT's Andrew Sutherland said the chance anything is fishy is "approximately zero." Alpöge says he and Claude worked it out over about six weeks.

What the paper actually says: there is no human-readable paper yet, only Lean code, which Officechai and France 24 describe as about 17 million lines, or 250,000+ pages if written out; Elliot Glazer says the formal statement is the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis. Lean verification checks the logic step by step but isn't journal peer review, and mathematicians like Fields medallist James Maynard and physicist Tony Padilla say understanding why it works could take years (Nature, France 24). AI critic Gary Marcus stresses AI didn't do it alone: a human expert steered it.

Sources (6): The Guardian · Scientific American · Nature (news) · France 24 · Officechai · @GaryMarcus on X

Why it matters: If it holds, a 167-year-old problem about how prime numbers are spread out has fallen to a human-AI team, a milestone for what AI can do in hard research.

A STAR SPIRALS INTO A GIANT IN ANDROMEDA, AND WEBB CATCHES THE AFTERMATH, OR DOES IT?

Astronomers led by Viraj Karambelkar of Columbia report in Nature (Nov.

Read more

18) that a giant star in the Andromeda galaxy, nicknamed Big Bao, swallowed a smaller companion that spiralled inside it for years, brightening it, until the pair erupted in October 2025 as the event AT 2025abao (Science News, NASA). Webb observed it 47 days after the eruption peaked, the earliest follow-up of such an event.

What the paper actually says: the team estimates only about a tenth of the giant's outer gas escaped and that the energy suggests the small star reached the core, which together "point to a merger"; dust hides what's left and may take years to clear (Science News). AI VISION MODELS AREN'T AS 'BRAIN-LIKE' AS THOUGHT, A YORK UNIVERSITY STUDY FINDS

York University researchers say AI vision models that predict brain activity well don't actually work the same way, because many of their internal units can't be predicted from real brain signals (ScienceDaily).

Read more

"Until now, scientists mostly tested this in one direction," senior author Kohitij Kar said.

What the paper actually says: the peer-reviewed study (Muzellec and Kar, Nature Machine Intelligence, published March 25, 2026) used recordings from the inferior temporal cortex of macaque monkeys, not humans. Models with about 50% forward predictivity often contained units the brain data couldn't predict, while monkey-to-monkey mappings were symmetric; the authors call their 'reverse predictivity' metric a conservative diagnostic, not proof that AI and brains are unrelated. The press release headline is broader than the monkey-based finding.

Sources (2): Nature Machine Intelligence · ScienceDaily

Why it matters: Worth remembering the next time someone says an AI 'thinks like a brain.'

IMPORTANT GENES DON'T MUTATE MORE SLOWLY AFTER ALL, BATH RESEARCHERS SAY

A University of Bath study says the 50-year-old idea that evolution protects important genes by giving them lower mutation rates is "a triumph of hype over substance," in co-author Sofia Radrizzani's words (ScienceDaily).

Read more

What the paper actually says: in the peer-reviewed PLOS Biology paper (Radrizzani and Hurst, Nov. 12), a gene's importance, measured by protein level and essentiality, did not predict its mutation rate once other factors were controlled for. It's in two microbes, not people, and the authors note earlier replication attempts on this question had disagreed.

Sources (2): PLOS Biology · ScienceDaily

Why it matters: Supports the textbook view that mutation is random and selection does the sorting.
