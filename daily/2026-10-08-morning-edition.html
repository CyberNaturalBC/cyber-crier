UPDATE: ISAIAS IS NOW A HURRICANE, AIMED AT THE FLORIDA PANHANDLE AND ALABAMA

Isaias became the Atlantic season’s first hurricane, with 80 mph winds early Thursday, and Hurricane Hunters say it is still strengthening, the U.S. National Hurricane Center says. Hurricane warnings run from Ocean Springs, Mississippi, to the Florida Panhandle, with a storm-surge warning from the mouth of the Mississippi to Florida’s Steinhatchee River. AP (via CityNews) says landfall is expected late Friday or early Saturday, with mandatory evacuations in parts of Okaloosa County, school closures and some Gulf oil wells pausing production. It formed one day short of the 1905 record for the latest first Atlantic hurricane, a quiet season AP blames on a strong El Niño.

Sources (3): National Hurricane Center · CityNews / AP · SitDeck brief (newsletter)
Why it matters: Paused Gulf oil wells and refineries in the path add to the Iran pressure on gas and diesel prices.
 
UPDATE
OTTAWA SHELVES MAID FOR MENTAL ILLNESS ALONE — INDEFINITELY

Justice Minister Sean Fraser said Wednesday the Liberals will table a bill this fall to indefinitely bar medically assisted death for people whose only condition is a mental illness, scrapping the expansion due March 17, 2027, CBC and AP report. Fraser said there’s no medical consensus on when mental illness is irremediable, and he cited poor access to mental-health care. The same bill would allow advance requests for people with incurable illnesses who will lose capacity, with provinces deciding whether to offer it; the National Post notes Alberta has already banned them. Canadian Affairs reports some critics want the two changes split into separate bills, and the government opposed a broader Conservative private member’s bill on the same day.

Sources (5): CBC News · AP News · National Post · Canadian Affairs · iPolitics
Why it matters: Settles a long-running question for now, and opens a new one on advance requests that B.C. will have to decide.
 
HOUSE VOTES DOWN CONSERVATIVE DIESEL-TAX RELIEF, 196–133

The House of Commons on Wednesday defeated a Conservative motion to scrap federal diesel taxes until Canada Day, permanently remove taxes on diesel production and fast-track fuel infrastructure, by a vote of 196 to 133, the Commons record shows. The Liberals, NDP and Bloc voted against it, The Canadian Press reports. The Conservatives say the Liberals voted against saving Canadians money with fuel relief.

Sources (3): House of Commons vote record · The Canadian Press · Conservative Party of Canada (partisan)
Why it matters: Diesel costs ride along on every job truck, delivery and grocery bill. No federal relief is coming from this motion.

🚨 PENTAGON TELLS CENTCOM: GET READY TO RESUME THE IRAN WAR

The Pentagon told U.S. Central Command several days ago to finish preparations for resuming major combat operations in Iran, Axios reports, citing U.S. officials. There’s no date and Trump hasn’t decided. U.S. and Israeli sources say it could come before the Nov. 3 U.S. midterms, with massive strikes on energy, infrastructure and nuclear targets. NBC News, citing two sources, says the national security team discussed options for the coming weeks. One Israeli official told Axios the odds before the midterms are “not high” and rise after them. Meanwhile, a tanker was hit by “multiple projectiles” off Qatar with casualties reported, the BBC says, the first attack that far west of Hormuz since September.

Sources (5): Axios · NBC News · Arab News · BBC (tanker) · SitDeck brief (newsletter)
Why it matters: Oil already jumped on the report. A renewed war would hit pump prices and costs across B.C.
 
🚨 BREAKING
🚨 RUSSIAN STRIKE ON BUSES IN KRAMATORSK KILLS AT LEAST 33

A Russian strike hit two buses in the eastern city of Kramatorsk on Thursday morning, killing at least 33 people, the BBC reports, citing officials. The Guardian had 30 dead and 18 wounded, and says the regional prosecutor blamed an aerial bomb; local public transport was suspended. It follows Wednesday’s strike on a Pryluky apartment block, where the Guardian now counts at least 22 dead, including five children. Zelenskyy called it a deliberate hit on an “ordinary public transport stop” and urged allies to send more air defences. Kramatorsk and Sloviansk are the last big cities in northern Donetsk fully under Ukrainian control.

Sources (4): BBC · The Guardian · The Guardian (war briefing) · SitDeck brief (newsletter)
Why it matters: Two of the deadliest strikes on civilians this year, in two days. Expect louder calls for Canada and NATO to send air defence.
 
HOUTHI ATTACKS ON SAUDI AIRPORTS KILL THREE; RIYADH VOWS PAYBACK

Two people were killed and 28 wounded at Abha airport near the Yemen border, and one killed and eight wounded at Riyadh’s King Khalid International, Saudi Arabia’s aviation authority says, the BBC reports. The Houthis claimed both attacks as payback for Saudi airstrikes. The Saudi-led coalition said the attacks “will not go unpunished” and says it hit more than 80 Houthi sites. UN chief António Guterres condemned the cross-border attacks, and the UN says more than 200,000 people have been displaced in Yemen’s renewed fighting.

Sources (2): BBC · SitDeck brief (newsletter)
Why it matters: A wider Yemen war adds risk to Red Sea shipping, one more push on freight and fuel costs.

A PLANET’S MAGNETIC FIELD, SEEN DIRECTLY FOR THE FIRST TIME?

Astronomers say they may have directly detected the magnetic field of an exoplanet for the first time: Beta Pictoris b, a giant planet about 63 light-years away, Smithsonian Magazine reports. By tracking radio bursts at several wavelengths, the team found a pattern that suggests powerful auroras and a field about 2,000 times Earth’s. The preprint, posted Sept. 15, hasn’t been peer reviewed. An outside radio astronomer, Joe Callingham, calls it a potential “slam dunk” if it holds up.

Sources (1): Smithsonian Magazine
Why it matters: Magnetic fields help shield planets from stellar wind, so this opens a new way to judge which faraway worlds could be habitable.

UPDATE: OPENAI’S MATH DUMP INCLUDED A ‘UNIQUE GAMES’ PROOF — AND HUMANS RACED IT

Part of OpenAI’s Oct. 6 release of AI-made math results was a claimed proof of the unique games conjecture, one of the most famous open problems in theoretical computer science, alongside 376 other results, Quanta Magazine reports. MIT’s Dor Minzer and two students rushed out a 95-page paper on a closely related milestone in September, after rumours of the AI proof began to spread, to avoid being upstaged. Quanta says mathematicians are reacting with a mix of alarm and curiosity. Checking proofs of this size will take time.

Sources (1): Quanta Magazine
Why it matters: If it holds, it settles how hard some everyday optimization problems really are.

MICROSOFT AND NVIDIA: AI AGENTS ARE MOVING INTO WINDOWS

At a San Francisco event Wednesday, Microsoft showed Copilot getting access to local files and the power to take actions across Windows, The Verge reports. The Verge notes PC prices keep climbing because of shortages.

Sources (3): The Verge (Copilot) · The Verge (event recap) · NVIDIA Blog
Why it matters: An AI that can act on your files is handy and a new security risk. Check the permissions before you switch it on.

CYBERTRUCK SALES SAG; A CYBERCAB LOSES A DOOR SKIN IN PHILLY

Tesla delivered just 8,295 “Other Models” in Q3, down 48% from a year ago and the lowest since 2021, Electrek reports. With the Model S and X out of production, Electrek estimates 7,000 to 8,000 of those were Cybertrucks, a pace of about 30,000 a year from a line planned for 250,000, even with a cheaper model. Separately, a Cybercab turned up on Philadelphia’s Broad Street missing the outer skin of its driver’s door after an apparent low-speed crash. Tesla has no Pennsylvania permit for automated driving, and it isn’t known who was at fault. Electrek notes the bolt-on plastic panels are meant to be swapped, not repainted.

Sources (2): Electrek (Cybertruck) · Electrek (Cybercab)
Why it matters: Slow Cybertruck sales could mean better deals, or fewer updates, for anyone eyeing one for work and camping.

SUMAS FIRST NATION CHIEF READIES FIGHT OVER CARNEY’S PACIFIC LINK PIPELINE

Sumas First Nation Chief Dalton Silver, whose territory near Abbotsford has lived with Trans Mountain spills for decades, says the proposed Pacific Link oil pipeline will follow a similar route, and he’s weighing a legal challenge, CBC reports. Silver says the process “hasn’t been inclusive whatsoever.” CBC’s interviews along the route found a split: some leaders feel left out, while others are interested in getting the project built. Treaty 8 Grand Chief Trevor Mercredi says standing behind Carney and Alberta’s Danielle Smith at last week’s announcement doesn’t mean his community has consented.

Sources (1): CBC News
Why it matters: A Fraser Valley pipeline build means big trades work, and a legal fight that could stretch out the timeline.
 
B.C. ASKING RENTS DOWN 2.9% FROM A YEAR AGO; CANADA ON A TWO-YEAR SLIDE

Average asking rents across Canada fell 4.2% year over year to $2,034 in September, two full years of declines and the longest downturn in recent history, according to Rentals.ca and Urbanation (via CBC). B.C. was down 2.9%, though CBC notes it remains among the country’s most expensive markets. Condo rents fell faster (−7.8%) than purpose-built apartments (−2.7%). The report adds that average weekly earnings rose 19.4% over five years, so affordability has improved.

Sources (1): CBC BC
Why it matters: Good news for renters. Landlords may have more room to bargain.

EBY AND DOERKSON BOTH CAMPAIGN IN SURREY ON SMALL BUSINESS

Speaking outside a Surrey grocery store, NDP Leader David Eby promised interest-free loans of up to $1 million, for up to 72 months, for small and mid-sized firms hurt by U.S. tariffs, plus a tax credit for businesses that give a young person their first job, CBC reports. Also in Surrey, Conservative Leader Lorne Doerkson promised to approve major capital projects in 120 days or less, scrap PST on new machinery, equipment and business software, and pass a “one-in, two-out” red-tape law, returning regulation to 2017 levels by the end of a first term. Green Leader Emily Lowan, in Vancouver, pitched commercial rent protections and restored freight and passenger rail corridors. None of the three released costs with these promises.

Sources (1): CBC BC
Why it matters: PST off equipment and faster project approvals would matter to contractors and shops, and so would tariff loans. Ask how each one gets paid for.
 
POLLS: CONSERVATIVES AHEAD, BUT BY HOW MUCH DEPENDS WHO YOU ASK

Mainstreet Research (released Oct. 6) has the Conservatives at 44.7% to the NDP’s 37.2% among decided and leaning voters, with the Greens at 11.2%, OneBC 5.7% and CentreBC 1.1%. Mainstreet says 59% disapprove of Eby calling a snap election. Angus Reid (Oct. 1–3) has a tighter 48–43 Conservative lead, with both big parties gaining, and Doerkson edging Eby 36–33 as best premier. Ipsos for Global BC (Sept. 25–29) found the NDP ahead in Metro Vancouver, 43 to 41. The polls agree on the leader, not on how big the lead is.

Sources (3): Mainstreet Research · Angus Reid Institute · BC Ballot poll tracker
Why it matters: Metro Vancouver is where the race is close, and Surrey ridings like Surrey-Serpentine River (decided by 435 votes in 2024) are where it gets settled.

Surrey’s next advance voting days are Saturday Oct. 10, Wednesday Oct. 14 and Thursday Oct. 15, 8 AM–8 PM, at 10 sites, up from six in 2022, including Cloverdale Recreation Centre, the Now-Leader and the city say.
