🔭 Science (papers read)

TWO ELECTRONS BEAT A 30-YEAR LIMIT ON LASER-MADE X-RAYS

TU Wien and UC San Diego physicists fired intense lasers at helium and got X-rays at higher energies than the standard theory says is possible (TU Wien via ScienceDaily).

Read more

What the paper actually says: above about 2 × 10^15 W/cm², helium showed a weak second plateau past the usual cutoff of 120 to 150 eV, reaching 280 eV, the "water window." Simulations point to two correlated electrons returning to the atom together and releasing one high-energy photon. That gives a cutoff of up to 5.5 times the electrons' ponderomotive energy, versus the textbook 3.2. Argon and neon didn't do it. It's peer reviewed and open access in Nature Photonics, published Aug. 7; the explanation is the authors' best fit from modelling, and the signal is weak.

Sources (2): Paper: Wang et al., Nature Photonics (open access) · ScienceDaily (TU Wien release)

Why it matters: Brighter tabletop X-ray sources could mean sharper imaging of cells and materials.

BETELGEUSE'S HIDDEN COMPANION LEAVES A WAKE IN ITS ATMOSPHERE

Radio images of the red supergiant Betelgeuse show its outer atmosphere stretching into an egg shape and back in step with the orbit of a suspected close companion, Siwarha (NRAO).

Read more

What the paper actually says: VLA and ALMA observations at seven frequencies (2021 to 2026) show the atmosphere has recovered from the 2019-20 Great Dimming. Ellipticity stays at 10 per cent or less, but it's largest near the companion's predicted greatest separation and near zero when it passes in front of or behind the star. The authors call that "consistent with" perturbation by the companion; they didn't detect it directly and say it's too faint at radio wavelengths. Peer reviewed in The Astronomical Journal (published Sept. 1); we read the arXiv version.

Sources (2): Paper: Matthews et al., AJ 172, 184 (arXiv version) · NRAO release

Why it matters: Makes the case stronger that Orion's shoulder is two stars, not one.

"DOOMSDAY GLACIER" COULD ADD TWICE THE SEA-LEVEL RISE MODELS EXPECTED

A new modelling study by Poinelli, Rignot, Seroussi and colleagues projects Antarctica's Thwaites Glacier could add up to about 30 millimetres to global sea level by 2100, roughly double earlier estimates, even without more warming (ScienceDaily).

Read more

What the paper actually says: the model only matched about three decades of observed retreat when the grounding line was treated as a 2 to 5 km-wide melting zone rather than a sharp line. Using today's satellite-measured zones of 4 to 6 km, Thwaites has about a 60 per cent chance of pulling off a stabilizing seafloor ridge by 2100, as early as 2038 at 5 to 6 km, giving 30 ± 6 mm. That's against a 15.2 mm average from the big ISMIP6 model comparison. It's one glacier under a single lower-bound climate scenario. Peer reviewed and open access in Nature Communications (Oct. 12).

Sources (2): Paper: Poinelli et al., Nature Communications · ScienceDaily

Why it matters: Thirty millimetres sounds small, but it's from one glacier, and coastal dikes like Surrey's are planned around these numbers.

Explainer

RUBIN OBSERVATORY FINDS ITS FIRST DWARF PLANET CANDIDATE, OUT PAST PLUTO

The Vera C. Rubin Observatory has found 2026 ED138, a bright object about 46 AU from the Sun that could be one of the largest dwarf planets beyond Neptune (Rubin Observatory).

Read more

What the data actually says: this isn't a journal paper yet. It's a Minor Planet Center announcement (MPEC 2026-U85) plus the team's notes. Matthew Holman of the Center for Astrophysics found it Oct. 13 in early Rubin data: 30 Rubin observations on 20 nights from March to July, plus older Dark Energy Camera images back to 2016. Its brightness (H 3.19, only nine known trans-Neptunian objects are brighter) means roughly 600 to 1,500 km across depending on how reflective it is. It's near a 12:7 resonance with Neptune, which isn't settled. It needs infrared or occultation measurements to confirm its size and that it's round.

Sources (2): Rubin Observatory · Rubin community notes (orbit data)

Why it matters: Rubin's 10-year sky survey has barely started, so more finds like this should follow.
